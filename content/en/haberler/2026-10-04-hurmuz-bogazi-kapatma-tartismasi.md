Whether the Strait of Hormuz is fully open, fully closed, or somewhere in between remains contested in Iran's press. According to an analysis by moderate-conservative Khabaronline, the waterway's status is "neither entirely open nor entirely closed"; most traffic has been disrupted and energy transit has grown more expensive. The newspaper frames this as an important lever in Tehran's hand.

By Khabaronline's account, oil prices exceeding $100—versus around $70 before the war—suggest the strait's blockade is working to some degree. Yet the article warns that Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and Iraq are building permanent "workarounds": pipelines and ship-to-ship transfers south of the strait. Though these routes carry only modest volumes now, institutionalizing them could permanently weaken the strait's geopolitical clout. For this reason, the newspaper argues, keeping the strait closed long-term may pose a bigger strategic risk than reopening it.

Hardline Kayhan frames the issue differently. In a report titled "Day 218 of the War," it notes that Trump has promised for seven months that oil prices would fall when the conflict ends, yet prices remain roughly $30 above prewar levels. Despite Treasury Secretary Bessent's supply pledges and America's release of 40 million barrels from reserves, Brent crude climbed back to $103. Kayhan views this as evidence that Iran's pressure on the strait is straining the U.S. economy.

The article also cites former International Energy Agency official Neil Atkinson, noting that the EU and IEA are ready to release emergency stockpiles—but only for short-term relief, as global supply will remain structurally constrained.

## Context

The Strait of Hormuz is a narrow waterway from the Persian Gulf to open ocean carrying significant shares of global oil trade. Iran's coastal position gives Tehran strategic leverage over traffic. In wartime, security risks and Iran's restrictions have pushed crude markedly above prewar levels. This discussion links to [an earlier story about a tanker strike](../2026-10-03-hurmuz-tanker/): every strait incident becomes a fresh calculation for Tehran's political camps and global energy markets.

Khabaronline's core concern is the "normalization" of alternate routes. While a few million barrels daily bypassing the strait seem minor today, if this flow hardens it could weaken the strait's deterrent power for years—a strategic loss the newspaper judges worse than opening the waterway outright.
