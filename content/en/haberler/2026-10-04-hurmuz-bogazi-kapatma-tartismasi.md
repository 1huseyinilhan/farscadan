Whether the Strait of Hormuz is fully open or fully closed has been a debated question in Iran's press for months. According to an analysis published by moderate-conservative Khabaronline, the strait's status is "neither fully open nor fully closed"; most traffic has been disrupted and energy transit has become more expensive. The newspaper characterizes this as an important lever in Iran's hands.

According to Khabaronline's analysis, oil prices exceeding $100—compared to the pre-war level of $70—indicate that the strait blockade is working to some degree. However, the article points out that Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and Iraq are establishing "workaround" routes through pipelines and ship-to-ship transfers south of the strait; although these routes currently carry smaller volumes, they could permanently reduce the strait's geopolitical value over time. For this reason, keeping the strait closed for an extended period could produce a more dangerous outcome than opening it.

Hardline Kayhan frames the situation differently. In its "day 218 of the war" report, it notes that Trump has repeated for seven months that oil prices will fall once the war ends, yet prices remain $30 higher than pre-war levels. Against Treasury Secretary Bessent's promise of increased supply, the market has dismissed these statements. Despite the U.S. releasing 40 million barrels from reserves, Brent crude has risen back to $103. For Kayhan, this proves that Iran's pressure on the strait is straining the U.S. economy.

Kayhan also reports that the European Union, together with the International Energy Agency, is prepared to release emergency reserves, but according to former IEA official Neil Atkinson, this will provide only short-term relief while global supply will structurally remain depressed.

## Context

The Strait of Hormuz is the narrow waterway connecting the Persian Gulf to the open sea through which a significant portion of the world's oil shipments pass; its partial encirclement by Iran's coastline gives Tehran strategic control. Under wartime conditions, security risks in the strait and restrictions Iran has imposed have raised oil prices significantly above pre-war levels. This discussion connects to [the previous report on the tanker strike](../2026-10-03-hurmuz-tanker/): each security incident in the strait becomes a new calculation for both political camps in Tehran and global energy markets.

The core risk Khabaronline emphasizes is the "normalization" of alternative routes. According to the article, while a few million barrels daily flowing south around the strait currently seems minor, the permanence of such flows could weaken the strait's deterrent power for years; the newspaper considers this a greater strategic loss than opening the strait.
