Whether the Strait of Hormuz is fully open or closed has been a debated topic in the Iranian press for months. According to an analysis published by the moderate-conservative Khabaronline, the strait's status is "neither completely open nor completely closed"; most traffic is disrupted and energy transit is more expensive. The newspaper frames this as an important leverage in Iran's hands.

According to Khabaronline's analysis, oil prices exceeding $100 compared to pre-war levels of $70 suggest the strait blockade is working to some degree. However, the article highlights how Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and Iraq are increasingly establishing "workaround" routes via pipelines and ship-to-ship transfers south of the strait. While these alternatives currently handle small amounts of oil, they could permanently reduce the strait's geopolitical value over time. For this reason, Khabaronline argues that keeping the strait closed for extended periods could produce a more dangerous outcome than opening it.

Hardline Kayhan presents a different framework. In a report titled "War Day 218," it notes that Trump has repeated for seven months that oil prices will fall when the war ends, yet prices remain $30 higher than pre-war levels. Against Treasury Secretary Bessent's promises of increased supply, the market has ignored these announcements—even as U.S. stock releases caused Brent crude to briefly drop, it climbed back to $103. Kayhan sees this as evidence that Iran's pressure on the strait is straining the U.S. economy.

Kayhan also reports that the European Union stands ready, alongside the International Energy Agency, to release emergency stockpiles. However, according to former IEA official Neil Atkinson cited in the report, this would provide only temporary relief, and global supply would continue to face structural constraints.

## Context

The Strait of Hormuz is a narrow waterway connecting the Persian Gulf to the open ocean, through which a significant portion of the world's oil shipments pass. Its partial encirclement by Iranian shores gives Tehran strategic control capabilities. In wartime conditions, security risks in the strait and restrictions Iran has imposed have pushed oil prices significantly above pre-war levels. This debate connects to [a previous report covering the attack on a tanker](../2026-10-03-hurmuz-tanker/): each security incident in the strait becomes a recalculation point for both Tehran's political factions and global energy markets.

The main risk Khabaronline emphasizes is the "normalization" of alternative routes. According to the paper, even though a few million barrels per day bypassing the strait may seem marginal now, if this flow becomes permanent, the strait's deterrent power could be weakened for years—which the newspaper views as a greater strategic loss than opening it.
