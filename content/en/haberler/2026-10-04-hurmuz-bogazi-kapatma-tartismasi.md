Whether the Strait of Hormuz is fully open or fully closed has been a debated question in the Iranian press for months. According to an analysis published by the moderate-conservative Khabaronline, the strait's status is "neither fully open nor fully closed"; much of traffic is disrupted and energy transit is more expensive. The newspaper describes this as a significant lever in Iran's hand.

According to Khabaronline's analysis, oil prices exceeding $100 per barrel, compared to $70 before the war, suggest that the strait's blockade has worked to some degree. But the article notes the establishment of "workaround" routes such as pipelines from Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and Iraq, as well as ship-to-ship transfers south of the strait: though these routes currently carry small volumes, they could permanently diminish the strait's geopolitical value over time. For this reason, according to the newspaper, keeping the strait closed for a long time could be a more dangerous outcome than opening it.

The hardline Kayhan paints a different picture: in a report titled "War Day 218," it notes that Trump has repeated his claim for seven months that "oil prices will fall when the war ends," yet prices remain $30 higher than before the war. Kayhan observes that the market is ignoring Treasury Secretary Bessent's promises to increase supply, and that despite the U.S. releasing 40 million barrels from its reserves, Brent crude has climbed back to $103. According to Kayhan, this shows that Iran's pressure on the strait is straining the U.S. economy.

Kayhan also reports that the European Union is prepared, alongside the International Energy Agency, to release emergency reserves—but according to former IEA official Neil Atkinson, this would provide only short-term relief, with global supply remaining structurally depressed.

## Context

The Strait of Hormuz is a narrow waterway where the Persian Gulf meets the open ocean and through which a significant portion of global oil shipments pass; its partial encirclement by Iran's coasts gives Tehran strategic control. Under war conditions, security risks in the strait and Iran's imposed restrictions have pushed oil prices substantially above pre-war levels. This discussion is linked to an [earlier report about a tanker strike](../2026-10-04-hurmuz-bogazi-kapatma-tartismasi/): each security incident in the strait becomes a recalculation point for both Tehran's political factions and global energy markets.

The principal risk that Khabaronline emphasizes is the "normalization" of alternative routes. According to the article, though a few million barrels of oil detouring from the south currently seem insignificant, the establishment of this flow as permanent could undermine the strait's deterrent power for years; the newspaper argues this would be an even greater strategic loss than opening the strait.
