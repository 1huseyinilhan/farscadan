According to Khabaronline, Pakistan's military spokesman Brigadier General Ahmad Sherif Chaudhry officially confirmed in an interview with CNN broadcast Wednesday that Pakistani troops are permanently deployed in Saudi Arabia. Chaudhry said this presence continues within the framework of the "Mecca Joint Defense Agreement" signed in August and that Pakistani forces are operating in Saudi territory "in various capacities and operational areas."

According to Khabaronline, Chaudhry stated, "I confirm that Pakistani armed forces are currently in Saudi Arabia," and emphasized that this deployment is not a new development, noting that Pakistan has sustained military cooperation with Saudi Arabia for decades in various capacities. The spokesman also addressed regional defense partnerships, saying "The trilateral defense alliance between Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, and Turkey did not emerge suddenly; it is based on deep diplomatic, political, military, and people-to-people ties between the three countries." The Mecca Agreement was signed in early August by Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, and Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, and provides for a reciprocal defense principle under which an armed attack against any one party would trigger military action by the others.

According to Donya-ye Eghtesad, on the same day Syria's interim government announced it had rejected sending troops to assist Saudi Arabia in its anti-Houthi operations in Yemen; Syria's information ministry said "Syria's priority is the reconstruction of its own country." According to Donya-ye Eghtesad's further report, Axios cited a U.S. official saying Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman met this week in Riyadh with Syrian leader Ahmad al-Sharaa and attempted to persuade al-Sharaa to send troops to reinforce Saudi-backed forces in Yemen; Damascus did not directly deny the claim, only stating that its priority is domestic reconstruction; therefore, the claim is being evaluated as "unconfirmed."

## Context

The Mecca Joint Defense Agreement is a reciprocal defense framework signed in August 2026 between Saudi Arabia, Turkey, and Pakistan, under which an attack on any signatory party obliges the others to military response; the agreement was signed during a period of intensified security concerns created by the U.S.-Iran war in the region. The Pakistani military spokesman's statement this week demonstrates that the agreement has not remained on paper and has been transformed into actual troop deployment; this also raises the question of whether a similar military contribution will be expected for Turkey's portion of the agreement.

Syria's same-day rejection of sending troops to Yemen reveals that each country commits at different levels to this regional security architecture. The Houthis' [tanker attacks in the Hormuz Strait](../2026-10-03-hurmuz-tanker/) and missile claims against Saudi Arabia form the backdrop to Riyadh's push to strengthen regional alliances. Turkey's own preparations, under the same pact, to [send troops and air defense systems to Saudi Arabia](../2026-10-07-turkiye-suudi-arabistan-asker-hava-savunma/) show the Mecca Joint Defense Agreement turning into concrete military steps for all three signatory countries.
