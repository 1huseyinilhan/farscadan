According to Hamshahri's report citing Atlantic magazine, two U.S. government officials told the magazine's reporters that the White House requested the Pentagon prepare Iran strike options that could be implemented before the November 3 midterm election. The report states that no final decision has yet been made on this matter.

According to Iran International's separate report citing Axios, the Pentagon ordered U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM) to "complete" preparations for resuming large-scale combat operations against Iran; Trump has not yet made a final decision, but U.S. and Israeli sources have voiced the possibility of a strike before the midterm election. Hamshahri's report in the same article, also citing Axios, provides different information: according to this report, Axios claimed that Pentagon ordered CENTCOM to "suspend" preparations for resuming Iran operations. Because two Persian-language sources summarized the same Axios report in opposite directions, whether the Pentagon ordered CENTCOM to complete or suspend Iran operation preparations remains "unconfirmed."

According to Adnkronos's August report citing Atlantic, the Trump administration's strategy at that time was oriented toward removing the war from the agenda before the midterm election: the administration planned to shift emphasis from military strikes to sustained sanctions and economic pressure campaigns, thereby reducing war's political cost; a possible hardline shift after the election was left open. This does not precisely align with the framing in today's Persian reports of "strike options should be prepared before the election"; whether strategy changed between the two periods could not be verified.

## Context

The war, now entering its eighth month since March 2026, is opposed by most of the American public in polls. High-level officials like JD Vance and Marco Rubio are reported to be attempting to keep the war relatively quiet before the election out of concern to preserve Republicans' narrow congressional majority; this situation keeps open the possibility of military activity intensifying again after the midterm election.

In a separate report the same day, ISNA suggested the U.S. suffered $3.3 billion in losses in the war with Iran and sustained damage to 81 aircraft and unmanned aerial vehicles. According to Kayhan's report, Democratic Senator Chris Murphy claimed such war losses are censored from the American public; according to a Reuters poll cited by Kayhan, only 31 percent of U.S. voters approve of the war and 63 percent disapprove. Kayhan reports that according to Reuters's screening of midterm candidates, 41 of 62 Republican candidates in critical races have distanced themselves from Trump's policies or from Trump himself, and at least 12 candidates removed Trump references from their campaign websites.

The report comes on the same day as [Vance's statement that Iran must reduce its enrichment capacity for the war to end](../2026-10-07-vance-iran-zenginlestirme-sarti/) and [news of the U.S. sending a third aircraft carrier to the region](../2026-10-03-abd-ucuncu-ucak-gemisi-ortadogu/); this also indicates that diplomatic channels are being kept open while military options are evaluated simultaneously.
