According to ISNA, the U.S. Department of Defense (Pentagon) quietly updated official casualty statistics in its war against Iran without announcement or media notification; the new figures show increases from previously disclosed totals.

By five U.S. officials who spoke to the Washington Post, more troops died in the Middle East Iran war than the Pentagon's public DCAS (Defense Casualty Analysis System) database shows. Five of them stated the true death toll stands at least at 22—four more than the public database—while a sixth official said 23 service members have died since February 28, plus three U.S. contractors in the theater. The Washington Post reported that the DCAS database showed 18 military deaths at that time.

Per Stars and Stripes, in July the Pentagon moved war-related casualties out of the "Operation Epic Fury" category into a new "Overseas Operations" classification; this shift coincided with four names being temporarily removed from the casualty database, sparking stern Congressional reaction. According to the Washington Post, the Pentagon's Middle East Command (CENTCOM) incurred $43.6 billion in war costs by early September; $28 billion went to munitions. The Washington Post reports that Sergeant Devin Seibel, who died in a training accident in Erbil, Iraq on May 31, and Major Sorffly Davius, who died of a medical emergency in Kuwait on March 6, do not appear in the DCAS database under the "Iran war" category, though both deaths occurred while on duty at bases that had been struck by Iranian attacks.

Per ISNA, this data update comes as Pentagon casualty-disclosure practices have been under debate for months. By an MSNBC-Ipsos poll cited by the Washington Post, only 28 percent of Americans approve of Trump's Iran policy, 62 percent disapprove; Congress this week saw seven Republicans join Democrats in a symbolic vote to end the war, signaling public discontent. The Washington Post reports Senate Democrats said they would investigate Pentagon casualty-reporting practices if they regain the majority in midterm elections; a group of military-veteran House members said they are drafting legislation to prevent Pentagon "manipulation" of casualty data. The Presumption of Innocence applies.

## Context

The U.S. war against Iran, launched jointly with Israel in February 2026, is known in Congress as "Operation Epic Fury"; the Pentagon had reported over 620 wounded and, by official count, 18-19 deaths. However, officials spoken to by the Washington Post say this figure does not reflect actual losses, particularly deaths after the July ceasefire collapsed that were reclassified into a separate category and hidden from public view. By an [earlier public-opinion poll measuring U.S. views of the war](../2026-10-03-abd-kamuoyu-iran-savasi-anketi/), a large majority disapproved; today's data update reignites debate over how much of the human cost is withheld from the public. Because the Pentagon has offered no official explanation for the updated figures, the precise number of lives covered by the recount remains *unconfirmed*.
