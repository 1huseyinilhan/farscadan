Led by the UK Foreign Office, the foreign ministers of 29 countries including the U.S., France, Germany, and Canada issued a joint statement Monday reiterating security allegations against Iran. According to Ettelaat, citing IRNA, the statement backs London's September 27 assessment of an incident at Fairford airbase and reiterates the claim that Iran was involved.

Under the headline "Joint Statement on Iran," the signatory nations pledged support for London's security assessment without presenting new evidence to substantiate the accusation. The statement emphasized that Iran's attempts to threaten or harm individuals in signatory territories are unacceptable and committed those nations to collaborate on identifying, investigating, and prosecuting perpetrators. According to Khabaronline's account, NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte on the same day posted support on social media, claiming the Fairford incident demonstrated the IRGC's capacity for hostile cross-border operations.

British Prime Minister Andy Burnham had previously repeated in Parliament, without presenting evidence to the public, the claim that the incident was likely coordinated in Iran and carried out under IRGC direction. According to Ettelaat, Burnham also announced preparation for additional sanctions against Iran; the UK Foreign Office shortly afterward added two Iranians to its sanctions list. Ettelaat noted that the sanctions announcement did not explicitly link the targeted individuals to the Fairford case; however, according to the official GOV.UK statement, the targets are identified as R.B. (said to oversee IRGC counterintelligence operations) and Sh.A.T.J. (described as a facilitator of a Zeinashti criminal network). London characterized the sanctions as punishment for "hostile activity" and summoned Iran's ambassador to the Foreign Office. The presumption of innocence applies.

According to Ettelaat, seven individuals detained near Fairford due to three suspicious minibuses were released on bail, and counterterrorism police continue their investigation; no improvised explosives were found in the vehicles. Iran firmly denied any connection to the incident; Iran's embassy in London called the accusations "baseless and malicious" and blamed them for fueling anti-Iran propaganda in Britain. Iran's Foreign Ministry also summoned Britain's ambassador to Tehran in response.

## Context

RAF Fairford is America's sole forward operating base for heavy bombers in Europe; the base played a critical role throughout the seven-month U.S.-Iran war. As reported in [an earlier story on B-1 withdrawals](../2026-10-05-b1-ucaklari-fairford-cekilme/), days after the September 27 incident, twelve strategic bombers departed Britain; at that time five suspects had been released and charges remained unproven. Today's 29-nation statement and new sanctions represent London's bid to internationalize its allegations; however, the official Canada.ca text confirms that signatories stated only their support for Britain's assessment—no new evidence of direct Iranian responsibility was shared. The gap between London's accusation and Tehran's denial **remains unconfirmed**. One notable detail: Turkey is not among the 29 signatories. The U.S. Treasury's [October 16 sanctions wave against Iran-linked individuals and entities](../2026-10-16-abd-hazine-iran-yaptirim-golge-bankacilik/) shows this international pressure continuing on another front.
