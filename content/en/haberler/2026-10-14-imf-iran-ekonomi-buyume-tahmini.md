The International Monetary Fund (IMF), in its latest World Economic Outlook report, projected that Iran's economy will contract 7.6% in 2026 due to destruction from the war. According to Kayhan, the same report forecast that the economy will return to 3.6% growth in 2027 and inflation will fall from 78.6% to 42%.

By the IMF's figures, unemployment will decline from 9.2% this year to 9% next year; the current account balance will shift from a deficit of 1.2% of 2026 GDP to a surplus of 1.2% in 2027. Kayhan presented these figures as a sign of notable recovery in 2027 "despite the consequences of two wars and external pressures." Opposition outlet Iran International reported the same 7.6% contraction figure but with a different frame: its story focused on the contraction within 2026 itself, reminding readers that Iran's Statistics Center recorded a 10.1% decline in gross domestic product in spring 1405 (compared to the same period the previous year). This means Tehran's own official data paints a bleaker picture than the IMF's annual forecast.

## How the Persian press covered it

| Source (orientation) | Headline / framing |

| Kayhan (hardline) | With headline "Inflation to drop by half this year," highlighted the 2027 recovery |
| Iran International (opposition, diaspora) | With headline "7.6% contraction in 2026," emphasized the contraction and Tehran's worse own data |

Both sources reported the same IMF figure of 7.6% for 2026 contraction; the difference lies not in the numbers but in which year and which data point appears in the headline. Because the IMF report's publication date and full citation were not explicitly stated in the stories, whether these figures belong to the IMF's April, July, or later update could not be independently verified; the most current verified figures in English-language sources are 6.1% contraction for April 2026 and 5.4% for the July 2026 update. Therefore, the 7.6% figure cited in the Persian press remains **unconfirmed** as to which dated report it originates from.

## Context

In its April 2026 update, the IMF had cut Iran's 2026 growth forecast by 7.2 points from its January projection, attributing it to war damage in energy and transport infrastructure and disruptions around the Strait of Hormuz; the July update raised the forecast to 5.4% contraction due to partial recovery in oil exports. The institution itself emphasized that projections are "contingent on the duration of conflict and the extent of infrastructure damage." This contraction forecast can be read as the statistical reflection of a process now three months into the government's announcement that it was [restructuring the economy for war conditions](../2026-10-04-pezeskiyan-savas-ekonomisi/). The fact that Iran's official statistics show a bleaker picture than the IMF's annual average may indicate that the economic impact of war has intensified seasonally within the year.

The IMF's report noted that growth forecasts were also downward-revised across the broader Middle East and North Africa region, with the regional average sharing in the impact of the war's translation into energy markets. Kayhan's reporting emphasized that Iran's current low crude oil exports relative to other OPEC members form the basis of expectations for renewed export growth in 2027. However, independent energy analysts have not publicly shared separate assessments confirming this export growth projection; therefore, how realistic the 2027 recovery scenario is also remains **unconfirmed**.
