French President Emmanuel Macron will travel to the Gulf region next week to discuss recent developments in the Strait of Hormuz and the global energy crisis. According to IRNA, citing Reuters, the Élysée Palace announced Wednesday evening that Macron will visit Kuwait, the United Arab Emirates, Bahrain, and Qatar on October 19–21 (the 27–29th of Mehr).

According to the statement, Macron will discuss developments in the Strait of Hormuz and the effects of rising energy prices with officials in these countries. Macron has previously said that ongoing conflict in the Middle East is worsening the global energy crisis and has called for a diplomatic solution that ensures the strait remains open. According to IRNA's account, Macron said: "In the coming weeks, we will hold a meeting with G7 members to address energy matters; this will be to strengthen cooperation between G7 members and key partners and avoid unnecessary tensions, and to examine options such as possible release of strategic reserves or lifting restrictions." Macron also stated that France would send military forces and defense systems to Saudi Arabia to help protect the Yanbu port, which is vital for energy shipping in the Red Sea.

Khabaronline and Hamshahri relayed the same Reuters-based story in nearly identical language; both outlets additionally reported—drawing on French media—that gasoline prices in France have reached historic highs, diesel prices are approaching records, and these price increases have intensified protests in France. According to Macron's itinerary, the delegation will first visit Kuwait, then the UAE, Bahrain, and finally Qatar; all four countries are members of the Gulf Cooperation Council and are directly affected by the Hormuz and energy trade crisis.

## Context

Macron's visit represents the diplomatic side of the Hormuz crisis that began [October 11](../2026-10-11-hurmuz-nefkes-sepah-centcom-celiskisi/) and continued this week with successive tanker and container-vessel incidents. France's proposal to discuss strategic petroleum reserve releases within the G7 framework signals that the strait disruption is already pushing global energy prices higher. The acute fuel prices and accompanying protests in France show that Europe is as severely affected by this crisis as regional actors in the Middle East. Macron's offer of defense support to Saudi Arabia reflects France's aim to remain a neutral mediator while directly contributing to Gulf security.
