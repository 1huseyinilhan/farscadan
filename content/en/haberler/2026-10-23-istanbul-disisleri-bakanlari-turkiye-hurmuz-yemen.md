Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan hosted foreign ministers from seven nations in Istanbul on Friday. According to Iran International's reporting, the meeting's agenda included the Iran-U.S. war, security of the Strait of Hormuz and Bab al-Mandab, developments in Yemen, and the Palestine issue. Reuters confirmed that this was the first such meeting in this format.

According to the report, Turkey, Saudi Arabia, Egypt, and Pakistan sat down with the United Kingdom, France, and Germany—the first time the four regional countries designated as "R4" have gathered in such a security meeting. The R4 group has been conducting regular consultations on the Houthi threat in Bab al-Mandab since March. Turkey, Saudi Arabia, and Pakistan are also signatories to the Mecca Defense Pact, committing their military forces to defend Riyadh; Egypt, though not a pact member, is evaluating military support options for Saudi Arabia, with Egypt's Parliament expected to vote on this on October 26.

According to Iran International's analysis, the meeting's significance extends beyond the number of participants; the regional countries' strengthening defensive cooperation against Houthi attacks could reduce U.S. direct military support for Saudi Arabia, freeing Washington resources for greater involvement in the Iran war. The report suggests this could create two parallel tracks: direct U.S.-Israeli military pressure alongside regional and European efforts to constrain Iran's regional proxies. However, regional countries are avoiding alignment with Israel as a formal coalition partner, and while maintaining political-security relations with Washington, have not formally joined Israel's strategy.

According to the report, Turkey, Saudi Arabia, and Egypt continue to prioritize diplomatic solutions, and some have not severed talks with Tehran. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi reportedly transmitted through Qatar a seven-day proposal for normalizing ship traffic in the Strait of Hormuz, conditioning its implementation on Tehran's terms; Araghchi has also indicated readiness to mediate between Saudi Arabia and Ansarullah. However, independent dynamics on the Yemen front are complicating this process.

## Context

The R4 group formed following Houthi threats to merchant traffic in Bab al-Mandab and gained institutional defense dimensions through the Mecca Defense Pact ([related report](../2026-10-22-mekke-pakti-meclis-badr-anlasmasi/)); the pact remains under review in Turkey's Grand National Assembly. The participation of the United Kingdom, France, and Germany reflects concerns about navigation security in the Strait of Hormuz and energy supply, indirectly strengthening NATO's role in the region. For Turkey, hosting the meeting strengthens its position in regional diplomacy; the attendance of President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan as one of his priority objectives has been noted. The analysis emphasizes that while the regional countries' strengthened defense cooperation with Saudi Arabia does not directly weaken Iran's hand in the Strait of Hormuz, a reduction in the Houthi threat could somewhat limit bargaining power; however, this does not mean the war will end militarily with Iran.
