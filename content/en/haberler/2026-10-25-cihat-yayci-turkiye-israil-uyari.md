Retired Turkish admiral Cihat Yaycı, in an interview with Turkish media outlet GZT, evaluated what is reported to be an Israeli "security map" and described it as a "massive Israeli propaganda operation." According to the Ettelaat report, citing ISNA, Yaycı argued that this map aims to turn Syria into Israel's "backyard" and to prevent Damascus's central government from consolidating influence—a "game within a game"—and claimed Israel is trying to fuel broad sectarian conflict between Sunni and Shia communities in the Middle East through these moves.

Yaycı stated that in the alleged map, countries' internal structures are divided, showing Gaza and the West Bank as well as Iraq and Syria as separate hostile camps. The most striking part of the interview involved a direct military conflict scenario between Ankara and Tel Aviv: Yaycı claimed Israel lacks the capacity to wage full-scale ground warfare against Turkey; it could conduct air operations, but if so, "30 million soldiers will move south and no one can stop them." The retired admiral continued describing a catastrophic scenario for the region, asserting that Turkish forces would face no obstacles: "Syria cannot stop us, Lebanon cannot block us, we simply pass through Israel." He concluded with a direct threat to the Israeli military: "If the Turkish army and the Israeli army face each other on the battlefield, the Turkish army will crush the Israeli army like insects. I have no doubt of this, and no one else will either. We will crush them like insects."

## Context

Cihat Yaycı is a retired admiral who served in the Turkish Navy and is recognized as one of the architects of the "Blue Homeland" maritime doctrine. Since the Gaza war began, he has regularly warned Turkish media about Israel's regional objectives. He currently holds no public office; his statements do not reflect the Turkish Republic's government position. In earlier statements in Turkish media including GZT, he has claimed that Israel targets the Akkuyu Nuclear Power Plant and Turkey's defense industry, and has commented that the Israeli military is employing a warfare tactic described in the Bible.

The fact that Iran's press is carrying this interview suggests Tehran wants to present Turkey-Israel tensions as reinforcing its own war narrative; Iranian media had recently covered a meeting of foreign ministers held in Istanbul—which Turkey opposed—in a similar frame. However, because the interview lacks independent verification in English-language sources and no second Persian-language source reporting the same claims has been found, these statements should be read as one retired officer's personal assessment, not as an official position of the Turkish Armed Forces or the Turkish Republic government. Yaycı's reference to "30 million soldiers" is not based on an official mobilization plan, but on his own strategic assessment.
