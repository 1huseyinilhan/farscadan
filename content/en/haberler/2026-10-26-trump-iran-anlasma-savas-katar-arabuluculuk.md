According to Iran International's report, US President Donald Trump stated that a "decision point" is approaching between military action and a deal with Iran; in a separate statement he said there would be "no strike on Iran tonight." In an interview with Fox News broadcast Sunday, Trump summarized his preference this way: "Do I make a deal with them or do I finish them off?" According to CBS News's live feed, Trump did not reject this framing and continued to keep military options on the table.

In the same report, a Reuters source with knowledge of the matter said Qatari mediators were in Tehran on Sunday, coordinating with the US to reduce regional tensions. According to L'Orient Today, Trump ruled out a strike for that day but did not take the military option off the table for the future. The Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson said diplomatic messages were continuing through intermediaries, while Iran's Washington ambassador accused Trump of sabotaging talks unless sanctions are lifted.

The US is maintaining its naval blockade of Iran while keeping the military option open. According to CBS News, some officials said Trump might delay a possible strike until after the US midterm elections on November 3; this information has not been directly confirmed by Trump.

According to Iran International's report, Trump also said in the same interview that they are "watching very closely" Iran's nuclear facilities and that they know "exactly what to do" if necessary, though he did not specify when a military move might come. Trump also implied that Iran's return to the negotiating table requires the blockade to continue—a direct contradiction of Iran's official position that "no deal can be made under pressure." Foreign Ministry Spokesperson Ismail Baghayeri reiterated that Iran will not submit to one-sided US demands and that Washington's claims cannot be trusted.

Qatar's mediation role represents a continuation of a pattern that has lasted since the war began. Doha has previously served as a message carrier between Washington and Tehran alongside Oman and Pakistan, several times delaying planned strikes or conveying calls for return to negotiations. That this visit takes place during a period when tensions have escalated before an election underscores that Qatar's role remains active.

## Context

Trump's "deal or finish" phrasing fits a pattern repeated throughout the war since February: the US has repeatedly delayed strikes and renewed threats between them, while Gulf states like Qatar, Saudi Arabia, and Oman have maintained their mediation roles. The Iran side argues that the continued blockade has undermined trust in negotiations; Washington says it is waiting for concrete Iranian steps on nuclear and missile programs. Whether the US will launch major military action near its own midterm election dates mirrors the election-war timing debate seen in the Netanyahu-Zamir disagreement ([related story](../2026-10-26-netanyahu-zamir-ihtilafi-iran-saldirisi-secim/)). The claim that Trump might delay a strike until after the midterms is based on a single news source and has not been officially verified.
