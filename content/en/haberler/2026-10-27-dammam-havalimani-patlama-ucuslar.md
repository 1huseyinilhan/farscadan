At least four explosion sounds were heard Monday night into Tuesday in Dammam, in eastern Saudi Arabia, and a security alert was issued in the area. According to Iran's state news agency IRNA, citing Yemen-based Al-Masirah television, flight tracking website FlightRadar data shows that flight operations at King Fahd International Airport in Dammam have halted.

According to IRNA's report, the information is based on unofficial Saudi sources; no official Saudi authority has commented on the cause or source of the explosions. Iran's semi-official ISNA agency also confirmed on the same night that at least four explosion sounds were heard, noting that the report is based on unofficial sources. Economic newspaper Donya-ye Eghtesad carried the story under the headline "Eastern Saudi Arabia rocked," citing IRNA.

It should be noted that Al-Masirah is an outlet affiliated with the Houthi movement in Yemen, and the claims originate from this source; responsibility for the explosions is not verified in these reports. Saudi authorities in Dammam and surrounding areas have not shared independent verification or information on damage or casualties; IRNA's report also contained no official Saudi statement.

## Context

King Fahd International Airport was the target of similar attack claims throughout October. According to aviation sources, a munition reportedly fell near the airport on the night of October 11, prompting Qatar Airways to temporarily suspend Riyadh and Dammam flights. In another attack on October 21, Saudi civil aviation authority GACA announced two deaths and eight wounded, and flights were rerouted from the airport according to FlightRadar24 data; GACA announced operations had returned to normal on October 22, though some airlines suspended Dammam service until late November. Who is responsible for these attacks is unclear in the sources; some of the earlier attacks have been attributed to Houthi forces. Dammam is one of the major cities in Saudi Arabia's eastern region where oil infrastructure is concentrated and home to one of the kingdom's busiest airports. This, along with [earlier drone attacks on Saudi's Safaniyah oil field](../2026-10-22-seybe-petrol-sahasi-iha-saldirisi/), shows that the country's eastern energy and transport infrastructure remains exposed to repeated security risks. Some airlines, following earlier attacks, have preferred to suspend Dammam service for weeks; this demonstrates how sensitive air traffic in the region is to security perceptions.
