According to Iran's Statistics Center report on mehr month (September 22–October 21) consumer prices, household monthly inflation stood at 4.4%, year-on-year point inflation at 88.7%, and the 12-month average inflation at 76.8%. According to Kayhan, this means a consumption basket that cost 100 units a year ago now costs an average of 188.7 units.

According to Kayhan's report, year-on-year point inflation was 92.3% in rural households and 74.1% in urban households; price increases in rural areas ran noticeably higher than in cities. The sharpest monthly rise appeared in education spending: the education category recorded the highest monthly inflation at 23.5%, followed by transportation at 10.7% and entertainment-culture at 9.8%. In point inflation terms, tobacco products saw the highest price increases at 163.2%, followed by furniture and household appliances at 128.9% and transportation at 128%. Food, beverages, and tobacco group inflation stood at 2.4% monthly, below non-food goods and services' 5.8% increase.

According to Donya-e Eqtesad's assessment, the 1.1 percentage-point decline in point inflation—from the previous month's 89.8% to 88.7%—does not mean prices fell; it only shows that the rate of price increases slowed slightly compared to the same month a year earlier. According to Iran International, these figures also suggest that household purchasing power will continue falling as long as income growth fails to keep pace with price growth.

## Context

Inflation in Iran had resumed an upward trend in summer 2026 following the Strait of Hormuz blockade and conflict environment; [the crisis in the kalabarg (food e-coupon) payment system](../2026-10-25-kalabarg-odeme-krizi-magazalar/) was one example of how this pressure translated into households' daily shopping. The mehr month figures also reflect the school term's beginning and corresponding spike in education spending. The Central Bank has not yet announced new interest rate or foreign exchange decisions to combat inflation; analysts warn that a potential war or sanctions shock could again drive the numbers upward.

The Statistics Center's monthly reports show official inflation figures remain far above single-digit targets embedded in the government's budget proposals. The 4.4% monthly increase recorded in mehr month still points to a very high annualized rate of price growth; this aligns with field observations that low- and middle-income households continue restricting spending on non-food items. Kayhan's report did not specify whether the government will issue a separate statement for November.
