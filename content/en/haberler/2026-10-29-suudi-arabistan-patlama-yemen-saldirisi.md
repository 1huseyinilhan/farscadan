Multiple blast sounds were heard Wednesday night in eastern Saudi Arabia and in Najran on the Yemen border. According to IRNA, the Beirut-based El-Gadir satellite channel, citing Saudi media reports, said alarm sirens sounded in Najran and that according to flight tracking data, air traffic stopped at King Fahd International Airport in Dammam. Saudi sources confirmed blast sounds in the eastern region; no official statement has yet addressed whether the incidents caused damage or casualties.

The blasts came as the Saudi Arabia–led coalition escalated strikes on Yemen. According to Hamshahri, citing Yemeni military spokesman Major General Yahya Saree, the coalition conducted 101 air and missile strikes on nine provinces, including the capital Sanaa, in the past 24 hours; strikes launched from Humeys and Musait bases used F-15 and Typhoon fighters, while missiles came from Najran. The heaviest toll came from strikes on the El-Hazm marketplace and a gas station in Al-Jawf province. According to the Houthi-controlled Health Ministry, by Wednesday evening the strikes had killed 14 people, including 3 children and 2 women, and injured 15; these figures have not been independently **confirmed**. Yemeni government spokesman Omar al-Buhaiti called the strike on a civilian marketplace a "war crime" and likened it to "what happened in Gaza"—this being the Houthi side's own assessment.

According to Hamshahri's same report, the strike wave was not limited to one area: strikes on civilian homes in Tiz injured two children; the coastal city of Mocha was hit with four separate airstrikes; and the Harf Sufyan district in Amran saw seven airstrikes. In Lahij, gunfire on civilian homes wounded two children. The Saudi side neither confirmed nor denied this information.

## Context

The Saudi-led coalition has fought Iran-backed Houthi forces in Yemen since 2015; Houthi spokesman claims the total number of strikes since the beginning of the escalation has reached 3,480—a figure not independently verified. This week marked the second report of flight interruptions at Dammam's King Fahd Airport; [one blast before Wednesday also shut down flights at the same airport](../2026-10-27-dammam-havalimani-patlama-ucuslar/). The security concerns are being linked to [the Saudi stock market approaching its lowest level in years](../2026-10-28-suudi-borsasi-yemen-saldirilari-ekonomik-etki/); the Riyadh government has not yet issued detailed public statements on blast reports or casualty claims.
