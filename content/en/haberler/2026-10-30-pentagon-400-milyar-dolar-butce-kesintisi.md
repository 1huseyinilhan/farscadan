The U.S. Department of Defense (Pentagon) is reportedly preparing to cut $400 billion from President Donald Trump's requested defense budget. According to IRNA's Politico-based report, the department is evaluating whether it can make such a large reduction in its current budget request; the report was also carried by Iran's state and semi-official agencies.

According to IRNA, Politico wrote that the Pentagon was preparing to cut $400 billion from Trump's defense budget. ISNA's version of the same report notes the cut concerns "Trump's requested defense budget" and that the information was conveyed to Politico by American sources and officials. Both Iranian agencies cite Politico as the primary source. Farsçadan could not independently reach Politico's original text or another English-language source verifying this $400 billion figure, so the number and rationale for the cut are reported **at the unverified level**, cited only to Politico.

According to the report's details, the cut is under consideration in the Pentagon's internal review phase before the annual budget proposal is submitted to Congress. Which budget items would be affected, timing, or whether the cut will be finalized were not detailed in the Iranian press reports. Both agencies reported the story in brief bulletin format; details such as the Politico reporter's name, the article's publication date, or the number of sources were not specified in the Iranian agencies' versions.

Pentagon budget planning typically intensifies October-November, ahead of the next fiscal year request going to the White House and then Congress. Draft figures in the Defense Department's internal planning documents can change many times before being reflected in the final proposal, suggesting the $400 billion figure may be one option under consideration rather than a firm decision.

## Context

In the U.S., the defense budget goes through the Pentagon's internal planning process before being submitted by the White House to Congress, and its final form can change through months of negotiation; a "cut" discussion at the planning stage does not equal a definitive change in the enacted budget. The Iranian state media's emphasis on this report is notable insofar as it shows that fiscal constraints in Washington are also being debated while U.S. military commitments in the region and Iran tensions persist; however, the sources do not claim this cut is directly linked to Iran policy.

The report forms a contrast to [an earlier report that the U.S. military spent an estimated $47 billion on potential conflict with Iran, according to CENTCOM estimates](../2026-10-28-abd-iran-savasi-maliyeti-47-milyar-dolar/): on one hand Washington's estimated spending on possible Iran conflict, on the other a Pentagon-wide discussion of hundreds of billions in cuts. Iran's press reported these stories separately without linking them, but together they show multiple facets of U.S. defense spending debates. The topic should be read as an ongoing theme in U.S. defense spending and Budget negotiation between Congress and the executive.
