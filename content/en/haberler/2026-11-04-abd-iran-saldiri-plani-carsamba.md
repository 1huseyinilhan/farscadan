According to Donya-ye Eghtesad, citing the New York Times, unnamed U.S. officials said that U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM) has prepared a strike plan against Iran and Washington could implement it as early as Wednesday or sooner. According to the report, the operation could take place immediately following U.S. midterm elections and at a time when three carrier strike groups are positioned in the region.

According to Donya's account of the New York Times report, the U.S. military's optimum window for an operation of this type is only brief: after early November, one carrier must return to its home port, and early November also coincides with the low-moonlight nights the U.S. military prefers for strikes. According to the report, some cabinet members in the Trump administration want more time for the economic pressure campaign to take effect, while some officials say Iran has already weathered this pressure.

The English-language New York Times report has been corroborated and relayed by English-language outlets such as JFeed and LaPresse. According to these sources, potential targets of the planned operation include energy infrastructure, nuclear facilities, missile bases, and Revolutionary Guards command centers. Approval of the operation rests entirely with President Trump, and Trump has not yet announced a decision. When speaking with the Financial Times, Trump said there is no urgent strike plan against Iran and that "we already seem to be getting everything we want," a statement indicating that a plan's existence does not mean an imminent strike decision—and it directly contradicts the NYT report. For this reason, whether the operation actually proceeds is being reported with an "unconfirmed" caveat.

According to additional information in English sources, the three carrier strike groups stationed in the region—the first time the same number of U.S. carriers have been in the Middle East simultaneously since 2003—and reinforcing ground units are part of a preparation phase the Pentagon began months ago. According to reports, CENTCOM had been previously ordered to complete preparations for the possibility of large-scale combat operations resuming; this suggests that the current NYT report discusses not a suddenly conceived plan but a military preparation process that has been underway for months.

## Context

The New York Times report arrived during a period when U.S.-Iran conflict has continued intermittently after direct military engagement in June 2026. [In a report carried on November 3](../2026-11-03-kism-adasi-abd-saldirisi-wsj/), it was claimed, citing the Wall Street Journal, that the U.S. conducted a limited strike on Kish Island; the current NYT report raises the question of whether these limited operations are a prelude to a broader operation. On the same day, [a tanker strike was reported in the Strait of Hormuz](../2026-11-04-hurmuz-tanker-saldirisi/), indicating elevated regional tensions. No direct connection to Turkey has been reported, but a potential operation could affect energy commerce passing through the strait and regional trade routes.
