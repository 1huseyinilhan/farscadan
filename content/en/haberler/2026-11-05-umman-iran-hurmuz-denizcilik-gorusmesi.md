According to Khabaronline, Oman's Foreign Ministry said practical, political, and technical arrangements are being evaluated for safe, unimpeded resumption of maritime traffic in the Strait of Hormuz. The announcement came following a phone conversation between Omani Foreign Minister Badr al-Busaidi and Iranian Foreign Minister Seyed Abbas Araghchi.

According to Khabaronline's report, the two foreign ministers noted the importance of moving from a de-escalation phase to lasting agreements; the discussion centered on practical arrangements to ensure the security of maritime corridors and joint technical mechanisms to secure international trade and energy flows. Both sides emphasized that these steps would serve to end the state of war and prevent new conflicts in the region. The report also cited a previous statement by Iran's Foreign Ministry, noting that the two countries' foreign ministers discussed regional developments and political-diplomatic efforts to achieve peace and stability in the region.

According to Khabaronline, this is the second known phone conversation between al-Busaidi and Araghchi in recent days; in an earlier call, both sides discussed efforts to achieve regional peace. The report says the announcement's use of the term "technical arrangements" does not elaborate on specifics—such as port controls, insurance and liability regimes, or navigable corridors. This point has not been clarified. Oman has been known since the war's start as one of several countries carrying messages between Iran and various Western and regional parties, and today's conversation is presented as an extension of this mediation role.

## Context

The Strait of Hormuz, since the outbreak of regional military tensions, has repeatedly been a site of tanker strikes and navigation interruptions; [according to a report from October 8](../2026-10-08-iran-umman-hurmuz-guvenli-gecis-anlasmasi/), Iran and Oman had begun work on a framework agreement for safe passage in the strait. Today's conversation shows the technical details of that process remain under negotiation. [According to a report from November 1](../2026-11-01-fidan-hurmuz-bogazi-kuresel-ekonomi/), Turkey's Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan said the closure of the strait is imposing a heavy burden on the global economy; the European Union also announced the strait's closure would cost the continent 127 billion euros. Oman is known as one of the countries that has played a mediator role between Iran and the West throughout the war; today's phone call shows that mediation line continuing. The parties have not yet announced a signed agreement; the conversation appears to remain in the "technical arrangements" stage, with no binding outcome yet having emerged. A day later, Qatar was also [reported](../2026-11-06-abd-iran-saldiri-karari-katar-arabuluculuk/) to have offered mediation in exchange for the U.S. lifting its naval blockade and reopening the Strait of Hormuz, showing multiple diplomatic channels working simultaneously toward reopening the strait.
