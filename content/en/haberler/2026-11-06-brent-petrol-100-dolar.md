Per IRNA citing Reuters, Brent crude futures on Friday (November 6) surged more than $2 per barrel to cross $100. The price movement came immediately following a sharp decline that occurred after Democrats' decisive victory in U.S. mid-term elections.

Per Bloomberg reporting cited by Donya-ye Eghtesad, after the sharp decline following the Democrats' clear victory in U.S. mid-term elections on Thursday, Brent rebounded by approximately 4 percent in the previous session and traded near $100 per barrel. According to the report, market participants are weighing the possibility that the Middle Eastern war could end; this calculation coincided with [news reports](../2026-11-06-abd-iran-saldiri-karari-katar-arabuluculuk/) from Axios and the New York Times on the same day indicating that diplomatic options between Washington and Tehran are back on the table. Donya-ye Eghtesad also noted that despite this rebound, near-term contracts were heading toward a 15 percent decline this week—the largest weekly loss since April 2020.

According to the report, prices have fluctuated over a wide $19 range since Monday; this volatility reflects a market that instantly shifts direction based on developments in the eight-month Iran-U.S. conflict. In the post-election decline, investors appear to have priced down risk on the expectation that the Democratic congressional majority could force the Trump administration to end the war; in the subsequent rebound, news reports about the absence of a final diplomatic settlement and CENTCOM's ongoing preparations for possible new strikes drove prices up again. The IRNA report did not include this contextual analysis; it conveyed only raw price data sourced from Reuters.

This sharp volatility in energy markets reflects a pattern repeated since the start of the war: price spikes when news breaks of attacks on a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz, and rapid retreats when diplomatic progress or ceasefire prospects strengthen. This week's moves exemplify how sensitive prices have become to both scenarios; the 15 percent weekly loss despite the rebound shows that investors are not yet convinced the war risk has truly diminished.

## Context

Oil prices are moving directly under the influence of eight months of Iran-U.S. tensions and security issues over the Strait of Hormuz. EU officials disclosed in recent days that keeping Hormuz closed has cost Europe [€127 billion](../2026-11-04-vonderleyen-hurmuz-avrupa-maliyet/), and the UAE's state oil company ADNOC announced that the war had consumed [1 billion barrels from global reserves](../2026-11-03-bae-adnoc-kuresel-petrol-rezervi-uyarisi/). Friday's rebound suggests the market is pricing in diplomatic solutions cautiously; however, the magnitude of weekly losses indicates investors remain uncertain about the conflict's trajectory.
