According to IRNA's report, the U.S. Treasury Department's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) has sanctioned eight money transfer firms and exchangers based in Canada for facilitating transactions with Iran's regime and affiliated sanctioned entities. The action continues the Treasury's "Operation Economic Outcast" campaign, launched in August.

According to official Treasury statements relayed by IRNA, the sanctioned firms include Ontario-based Million Exchange (Diverse Trade Inc), British Columbia-based Amin Exchange (1380079 BC Limited and partner Top PC Technician Inc), NIA Nations Currency, Farah Exchange (2397639 Ontario Ltd), CCC Inc, B Galaxy Inc (Cryptoyal), and Darvish Exchange. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent stated: "Canada cannot be a safe haven for illegal financial facilitators providing sanctions-evasion services to the Iranian regime and its proxies." According to the Treasury statement, these firms convert Iranian rials into cryptocurrency to enable cross-border transactions; certain digital wallet addresses have conducted transactions with sanctioned Iranian crypto exchanges—Nobitex, Wallex, Ramzinex and Bitpin—and addresses linked to the IRGC. The Treasury noted the action was coordinated with FinCEN and Canada's Financial Intelligence Unit, FINTRAC, and stated that some of the sanctioned firms also facilitated multi-billion-dollar money laundering for convicted drug trafficker Ryan Wedding, who was arrested in January 2026 and moved cryptocurrency proceeds of drug proceeds across borders.

Iran International reported the action as part of a cryptocurrency-focused sanctions series that followed earlier listings: Nobitex, Wallex, Bitpin and Ramzinex were sanctioned on June 2, 2026, and Aban Tether Exchange and Shelbit on August 7, 2026. The Treasury announced the sanctions fall under Executive Order 13902, targeting Iran's cryptocurrency sector.

## Context

The sanctions represent the latest escalation in the U.S. Treasury's months-long campaign targeting Iran's sanctions-evasion networks through cryptocurrency. The Treasury has reported that hundreds of money transfer firms in Canada facilitate money flows to Iran, with some attempting to evade scrutiny by changing names and addresses. No independent statement from the Canadian government or the sanctioned firms has yet been released.
