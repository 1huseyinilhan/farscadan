According to Hamshahri, Iran's military announced it downed a U.S. MQ-9 unmanned aerial vehicle over Qeshm Island. The announcement came shortly after local reports of fighter jet sounds and explosion alerts on the island; U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM) offered no comment on the incident.

According to Hamshahri, initial reports from local sources indicated that a fighter jet had been heard over the island and that two missiles had struck it. The military's General Press Bureau then announced: "A U.S. MQ-9 type unmanned aerial vehicle was struck and destroyed over Qeshm Island's airspace by fire from the military's new air defense systems and under Iran's integrated air defense network." A separate Hamshahri report citing local sources described two enemy fighter jets breaking the sound barrier to strike a launch platform, with an advanced U.S. MQ-9 drone simultaneously targeted and destroyed by Iran's air defenses; no casualties or injuries were reported. According to a Donya-ye Eqtesad report relaying Tasnim, two missiles initially struck the island, then a fighter jet was heard breaking the sound barrier. According to reports carried by Khabaronline and Hamshahri, a CENTCOM official told Al Jazeera: "We've heard reports about Qeshm Island, the situation is relatively calm, we have no comment on this matter"; the official provided no information on the flight's purpose or possible damage.

Sources differ on the full accounting of the incident: while the official military statement mentions only a single MQ-9 drone, Kayhan reports two drones and two tanker fires; Ettelaat describes a separate attack on a launch platform alongside a drone downing. These discrepancies remain unverified.

## Context

Qeshm Island and surrounding areas experienced a similar incident on November 3, ahead of the U.S. midterm elections: according to reports citing the Wall Street Journal, the U.S. struck a launch platform on the island, Trump characterized it as a "small strike," and officials described such operations as "defensive," not a return to large-scale operations ([details](../2026-11-03-kism-adasi-abd-saldirisi-wsj/)). A day later, tanker and drone activity resumed in the Strait of Hormuz ([details](../2026-11-04-hurmuz-tanker-saldirisi/)). Qeshm Island, the largest Iranian-held island in the Strait of Hormuz, is believed to host one of Iran's missile and air defense positions; the island's strategic location makes a significant portion of global oil traffic through the strait visible to Iranian forces. CENTCOM's "relatively calm" language is a standard response pattern used in earlier similar incidents, neither confirming nor denying a strike. Whether tonight's incident represents a new U.S. strike, an Iranian defense exercise, or a false alarm has not been officially clarified.
