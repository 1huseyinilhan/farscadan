During November 2-8, the Persian press agenda centered on an alleged attack window after the US midterm elections (Nov 3). The week began with reports this window was being used (Kish strikes, CENTCOM expanding blockade to land) and ended with two tankers catching fire in Hormuz the same night. Alongside escalation, Iran sought deterrence: S-400 requests from Russia and tactical nuclear weapons discussions. This analysis draws from Farsçadan's weekly coverage, with all information attributed to source organizations.

## Week's three headlines

### 1. Post-election attack window and Hormuz's toll

The week opened with former IAEA Director Mohamed ElBaradei's warning that a new US attack would be "complete madness". According to Hamshahri citing the Wall Street Journal, the US struck Kish Island secretly one day before midterms, destroying a missile ramp. Economic daily Donya-ye Eghtesad citing the New York Times reported CENTCOM possessed a broader strike plan. Thursday: Hamshahri reported Iran's military shot down an MQ-9 drone over Kish; CENTCOM didn't comment.

Saturday night: IRNA reported the Revolutionary Guard Navy said two "violator" tankers caught fire after hitting mines; same night CENTCOM immobilized a separate merchant vessel in the Sea of Oman. Also Saturday, Iran International reported CENTCOM established "Iron Gate" task force extending naval blockade to land borders and Caspian Sea. Costs mount: ADNOC said one billion barrels drawn from global reserves; von der Leyen cited 127 billion euros cost to Europe; Brent crude exceeded $100 Friday.

### 2. Diplomacy track open

Against this, negotiations continued. Iranian delegation left Doha Sunday saying talks went "positively"; Foreign Minister Araghchi said no final conclusion. VP Vance said "progress" in Iran talks before midterms. Hamshahri citing Axios: Trump met with security team, decision on military operation coming; Qatar's Hormuz proposal on the table. Oman and Iran foreign ministers discussed practical arrangements to safely resume Hormuz shipping.

### 3. Russian weapons, nuclear escalation

Iran sought deterrence. Supreme National Security Secretary Rezai conveyed Leader Khamenei's message to Putin during an undisclosed Moscow visit. Russia agreed to supply 90-unit helicopter-launched rocket pods; Iran requested S-400 air defense systems from Putin, delivery unconfirmed.

Nuclear rhetoric hardened. Lawmakers pushed for NPT withdrawal bills. IAEA chief Grossi said Iran has no nuclear weapons; Kayhan replied "accounts unsettled". Week's end: politician Larijani stated on state TV nuclear weapons cannot target civilians but can be used "tactically" against military bases. Health Ministry denied Times claim of iodine pill distribution near nuclear sites.

## Turkey's angle

Severest Turkey-Iran development: border soldier death during illegal border crossing intervention, struck by gunfire assessed from Iranian side. Cooperation continued: Iranian and Turkish ministers agreed at Istanbul COMCEC on 24-hour border gate operations.

Late week: US Treasury added Fugo Ground Services—Turkish firm providing services to sanctioned Iranian airlines from Istanbul—to sanctions list; only Turkey-based company directly targeted Iran's file that week. Diplomacy: Erdogan told Trump in Saturday call US-Iran tension must resolve diplomatically; Ankara and Iranian media confirmed. Erdogan's Baku weekend visit struggled to contain Turkey-Azerbaijan tension under headscarf crisis.
