On Saturday evening, November 7, Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps Navy announced that two tankers it described as "violators" caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz after striking a mine; according to IRNA's account, one tanker was in the eastern section of the strait and the other was transiting the strait itself. That same night, U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM) announced it had immobilized a separate commercial vessel in the Sea of Oman for blockade violations; the relationship between the two announcements could not be independently verified.

According to the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps Navy's statement, the two "violator" tankers shut down navigation and positioning systems to attempt transit through an unauthorized route in the Strait of Hormuz and in the process struck a mine, resulting in a violent explosion and fire. According to ISNA's account, the navy reiterated that "every ship cooperating with the U.S. military and non-compliant with Iranian rules will strike a mine" and called on mariners to "disregard the fantasy words of the CENTCOM commander"; this statement served as an indirect response to CENTCOM's announcement the same night. According to Donya-ye Eqtesad, the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps' official bulletin did not identify the two tankers that reportedly hit the mine or list their flags.

According to Khabaronline's account, CENTCOM said on X (Twitter) that evening that the Saint Kitts-flagged cargo vessel "M/V Huda" had repeatedly ignored warnings and attempted to breach the U.S. naval blockade on Iran several times; therefore, a U.S. military aircraft fired a guided missile at the ship's stern, immobilizing it. According to CENTCOM, the vessel was in a port and was attempting to leave the regional waters.

Whether the two tankers the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps claimed hit a mine and the "M/V Huda" that CENTCOM said it struck refer to the same incident or represent two separate events occurring the same night remains unclear between the sources; this could not be verified. The locations also do not fully align: the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps placed the two tankers inside and east of the Strait of Hormuz, while CENTCOM's target was identified as the Sea of Oman.
