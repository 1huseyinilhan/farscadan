The Kor Mor natural gas field in the Iraqi Kurdistan Region was struck by drone attack twice in the same evening on Monday. According to ISNA, a facility in the Chamchamal area of Sulaymaniyah Province was first hit by armed drones, triggering a massive fire and a severe outage in the Kurdistan Region's electrical grid; the responsibility for the strikes has not yet been announced.

According to ISNA, the first strike by armed drones triggered a large fire at the facility and caused a severe disruption to the Kurdistan Region's electrical grid. According to Hamshahri, the Kurdistan Region Ministry of Electricity announced that approximately 2,800 megawatts of capacity went offline following the attack; the ministry did not offer an assessment of where the attack originated. In a report citing IRNA, Donya-ye Eghtesad cited a channel called Al-Ahd, which in turn cited Kurdish news agencies, claiming that the Kor Mor field suffered heavy damage and ceased operations; this detail was not verified by an independent source and remains unconfirmed. IRNA's brief report also noted explosions and a drone strike at the site, but gave no concrete figures for the scale of damage.

According to Hamshahri's account, shortly after the first strike, a second drone attack on the field was reported; news channels circulated a video claimed to show the moment of the second strike, but verification of the footage could not be confirmed independently. Hamshahri reported that no detailed explanation had yet been released regarding who carried out the second attack, the extent of additional damage, or its impact on the Kurdistan Region's energy supply.

Neither Iranian official agencies nor authorities from Iraq or the Kurdistan Region made any claim about who was responsible for the two strikes. None of the four sources—IRNA, ISNA, Donya-ye Eghtesad, and Hamshahri—offered information pointing to the attackers' identity or motives. No casualties or injuries were reported in any of these sources. For this reason, who carried out the strikes and for what purpose remains unverified at this time.

Strike reports spread rapidly through the Persian press at short intervals: IRNA issued its initial brief bulletin at 20:43 UTC, ISNA reported the same strike with details about the fire and power outage at 21:25, Hamshahri announced the second strike also around 21:25, and Donya-ye Eghtesad added the "heavy damage" claim at 21:44. While the sources' publication times were close together, none confirmed or contradicted the information provided by the others; each source reported what it had independently learned within its own framework.

## Context

**Background:**
- **April 27, 2024:** According to Rudaw, a drone strike on the Kor Mor field killed four Yemeni workers.
- **October 15, 2025:** According to Kurdistan24, the Pearl Petroleum consortium, operator of the field, increased processing capacity to 750 million standard cubic feet per day (MMscf/d) through its KM250 expansion project.
- **November 26, 2025:** According to Kurdistan24, a drone strike on the field caused approximately 80% collapse in the region's power generation, with remaining electricity diverted to priority services like hospitals and water facilities.
- **November 9, 2026:** Today's two strikes point to a similar target and method as previous attacks; however, sources did not draw a direct connection between the two periods.

**Who it is / concept:** Kor Mor (English spelling: Khor Mor) is a natural gas field in the Chamchamal area of Sulaymaniyah Province in Iraq. The field is operated by the Pearl Petroleum consortium; Dana Gas and Crescent Petroleum, both UAE-based, are 35% partners each, and OMV, MOL, and RWE are other consortium members.

**By the numbers:**
- According to Kurdistan24, Pearl Petroleum's KM250 expansion increased the field's capacity from 500 MMscf/d to 750 MMscf/d in October 2025 (Kurdistan24, October 15, 2025).
- According to Kurdistan24, the field normally supplies gas that generates over 80% of the Kurdistan Region's electricity (Kurdistan24, November 27, 2025).
- According to Hamshahri's report on today's strike, 2,800 megawatts of capacity went offline in the region's grid (Hamshahri, November 9, 2026); this figure was not independently verified by an international source at the time of writing.

**What's next:** It remains unclear whether Kurdistan Region authorities or Pearl Petroleum, the field operator, will issue a statement about the second strike's impact or repair timeline; no date has been announced for such a statement.
