Within the Trump administration, divisions over how to end the war with Iran have entered a new phase. According to Donya-ye Eqtesad (citing Ilna), while the White House officially states that the main phase of military operations has concluded, the administration has not yet reached consensus on what final form the conflict will take or under what conditions it will end; three rival approaches are competing to shape the process.

The first approach, represented by Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth, is the military line: it holds that operations should not be halted unless control and deterrence mechanisms are first put in place to prevent Iran from rebuilding its military capacity. Within this framework, the Pentagon is trying to identify facilities that require special monitoring, including ballistic missile production lines and nuclear-linked installations. The second approach, led by Vice President JD Vance and Trump's special envoy Steve Witkoff, seeks to translate military superiority into a viable political agreement. Witkoff is advancing a phased negotiation process through Qatar as intermediary. He foresees freedom of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz as the opening gambit, with nuclear inspections and highly enriched uranium stockpiles as later stages. The third approach focuses on prolonging the naval blockade and commercial and financial restrictions; its advocates believe that extending the siege rather than launching a new round of attacks could push Tehran toward Washington's terms.

According to Donya-ye Eqtesad's assessment, the Trump administration's central challenge is finding an end-state in which Iran loses its capacity to rearm. Even within the diplomatic approach, disagreement persists over when and how to lift sanctions; some officials worry that early access to Iran's frozen assets could increase Tehran's economic and military power.

This uncertainty among the three approaches is reflected in Qatar's mediation efforts. According to Iran International's report from Jerusalem Post, Qatar's prime minister said in a Tuesday meeting in Paris with the G7 foreign ministers and his Saudi counterpart that Washington and Tehran still cannot agree on the "sequencing of steps"—in particular on the order of (1) lifting the naval blockade, (2) nuclear inspections, and (3) releasing frozen Iranian assets. On the same day, Iran's Supreme National Security Council Secretary Mohsin Rezaei called Washington's response to date "deceptive and insufficient" and reiterated that the Strait of Hormuz will remain closed until the U.S. meets Iran's seven conditions. Rezaei also denied as "completely false and baseless" reports of ongoing negotiations in Qatar.
