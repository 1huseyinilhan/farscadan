On Tuesday (Aban 19), the U.S. Department of Justice unsealed a revised indictment charging three people with orchestrating roughly 20 terrorist attack attempts in Europe and the U.S. on behalf of a network linked to the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) and Iraq-based Kataib Hezbollah. According to Iran International, one of the defendants was arrested in Turkey earlier this year and turned over to the U.S.

The three individuals named in the indictment are: dual Iranian-Iraqi national M.B.S.A. (32), Iranian M.M. (48), and Iraqi M.A.H. (35). According to the U.S. Department of Justice, M.B.S.A. had been working for the IRGC and Kataib Hezbollah since at least 2017 and was brought to the U.S. under FBI custody in May (Ordibehesht 24) after being arrested at a hotel in Istanbul earlier in the year. According to his lawyer's statement, M.B.S.A. was detained in Turkey and handed over to U.S. authorities; according to CNN, officials did not disclose the exact date and manner of the arrest. M.B.S.A. was presented to the federal courthouse in Manhattan on May 14 and remanded in custody without bail to a federal detention center in Brooklyn; he has been held there since.

According to the eight-count indictment, the network claimed responsibility in the spring of 2026 for 18 attacks in Europe—seven in Britain alone—under the name of an ostensibly newly founded group called "Harakat-i Ashab al-Yamin al-Islamiya." These attacks included arson, use of explosives, and a knife attack on two Jewish men in London; one victim held dual U.S.-British citizenship. According to the New York Post's Aban 19 report, the network also attempted to set fire to a bank building in Amsterdam. According to prosecutors, despite M.B.S.A.'s detention in the U.S., he continued to relay instructions in coded language to M.M. and M.A.H. through phone calls with his mother and sister in Iraq; the indictment refers to M.M. as "Abbas" and "Seyyid Abbas," and to M.A.H. as "Ali."

The network's final operation was an attack on RAF Fairford air base in Britain, where the U.S. had stationed B-1 bombers during the Iran war. According to the indictment, M.M. and M.A.H. directed this plan from Iran; in August (Mordad), M.A.H. sent money to associates in Britain to scout the base's perimeter and photograph access routes, and procured vans, fuel cans, and metal shrapnel for use in the attack. On September 25 (Mehr 4), three vans heading toward the base were stopped at security checkpoints; British security forces detained the vehicles and occupants the following morning. Because no explosives were found, the suspects were released under strict conditions, but authorities reopened the case after flagging the possible IRGC connection in late October. The U.S. military moved some of the B-1B Lancers out of Fairford shortly after the incident to reduce risk.

FBI Director Kash Patel characterized the new indictment as evidence of "Iran's determination to carry out terrorist attacks in Europe and the U.S." U.S. Attorney Jay Clayton of the SDNY office said the defendants "coordinated successful attacks targeting civilians on behalf of Iran-backed terrorist organizations." M.B.S.A. had denied the initial charges against him. The charges in the indictment remain unproven in court, and under U.S. law all three defendants are presumed innocent until proven guilty; *the presumption of innocence applies.*

## Context

**Background:**
- **August 10, 2022:** The U.S. charged Shahram Pursafi, linked to the IRGC's Quds Force, with attempting to assassinate former National Security Advisor John Bolton.
- **May 28, 2026:** The U.S. Department of Justice SDNY office unsealed the initial indictment against M.B.S.A.
- **Late September 2026:** The network's [RAF Fairford base attack](../2026-10-19-fairford-iran-plan-rubio-ingiltere/) was thwarted by British security forces.
- **November 10, 2026:** The prosecutor's office unsealed the revised indictment, now naming M.M. and M.A.H. as well.

**Key figures / concepts:** *Kataib Hezbollah*, an Iraq-based militia backed by the IRGC, is listed by the U.S. as a foreign terrorist organization; according to prosecutors, M.M. coordinated this organization's operations in liaison with the IRGC.

**By the numbers:**
- Some of the eight counts in the revised indictment carry sentences of up to life imprisonment upon conviction (U.S. Department of Justice statement, November 10, 2026).
- The network claimed responsibility for 18 attacks in Europe in spring 2026, seven of them in Britain (Iran International, November 10, 2026, citing prosecution documents).

**The Turkey dimension:** M.B.S.A. was arrested in Turkey in early 2026—reportedly at a hotel in Istanbul—and turned over to FBI custody and transferred to the U.S. This stands as one of the rare documented examples of Turkey serving as a crucial transit and arrest point in an IRGC-linked terror investigation. U.S. officials have not disclosed the full circumstances of the arrest; no details of Turkish authorities' contribution to the operation reached the public.

**What's next:** The case against M.B.S.A., M.M., and M.A.H. is proceeding in the federal courthouse in Manhattan; Judge Colleen McMahon is presiding. The three defendants have not yet entered pleas to the charges, and trial dates will be set later. Whether M.M. and M.A.H. will be extradited from Iran and Iraq remains unclear; U.S. authorities have made no statement on this matter.
