The UK-based International Magnitsky Human Rights Award was given this year to Mohammad Javad Vafaei Sani, a former boxing champion from Mashhad and a death-row political prisoner. According to Iran International, the award organizers characterized Vafaei Sani's case as a symbol of state repression against dissidents in Iran, freedom-of-expression violations, and deprivation of fair trial rights—citing allegations of severe physical and psychological torture, prolonged solitary confinement, and repeated death threats.

Vafaei Sani was arrested for his participation in nationwide fuel-price-hike protests in November 2019 (Aban 98). Those protests spread to dozens of cities after gasoline prices tripled overnight and numerous deaths occurred during security force interventions. According to English-language sources, Vafaei Sani was convicted on charges of arson and destruction of public property linked to the allegation of "moharebeh" (waging war against the state), a capital offense, and sentenced to death three times: the first and a second sentence in 2024 were overturned by the Supreme Court, but the Mashhad Revolutionary Court's 3rd Branch issued a third death sentence in September 2024. According to his lawyer Babak Paknia's October 4, 2025 statement, this latest verdict was upheld by the Supreme Court's 9th Division; Norway-based Iran Human Rights alleges Vafaei Sani was tortured to extract forced confessions. The World Boxing Council and numerous athletes have called for his life to be spared.

The Magnitsky Human Rights Award was established in Britain in 2015 and has since been given to numerous prominent politicians and human rights defenders. The award bears the name of Russian tax lawyer Sergei Magnitsky, who died in Moscow detention in 2009; following his death, multiple countries developed "Magnitsky sanctions"—targeted-sanctions mechanisms against human rights violators.

Before his arrest, Vafaei Sani was an amateur boxing champion in Mashhad. His case has also resonated in international sports circles: the World Boxing Council (WBC) and many professional boxers have issued public appeals calling on Iranian authorities to halt the execution. Norway-based Iran Human Rights has stated that Vafaei Sani was held in prolonged solitary confinement and subjected to torture attempts to extract forced confession, classifying his case among examples carrying "risk of extrajudicial execution."

## Context

**Background:**
- **November 2019 (Aban 98):** Vafaei Sani was arrested in nationwide protests against fuel price increases.
- **June 2024:** The Supreme Court overturned Vafaei Sani's second death sentence.
- **September 2024:** The Mashhad Revolutionary Court's 3rd Branch issued a third death sentence.
- **October 4, 2025:** The Supreme Court's 9th Division upheld the third death sentence.

**Key figures / concepts:** *Moharebeh* ("waging war against the state") is a category under Iranian criminal law punishable by death; it is often applied to political charges, and human rights organizations argue the definition is so broad it is frequently used against protesters and opposition figures. *Sergei Magnitsky*, a lawyer who exposed high-level Russian corruption in 2008, was arrested and died in Russian custody in 2009; the award and sanctions regimes bearing his name address human rights defenders and violators worldwide.

**By the numbers:**
- Three separate death sentences have been issued against Vafaei Sani; two were overturned by the Supreme Court, and the third was upheld in October 2025 (HRANA, RFE/RL).
- The Magnitsky Human Rights Award has been given annually since its establishment in 2015 (Iran International, November 10, 2026).
- According to Amnesty International's July 2026 report, Iran executed at least 26 people on charges tied to January 2026 protests alone; the same report noted that over 70 people in an Isfahan prison were sentenced to death on charges related to those protests.
- A separate Iran International report found that [at least 79 people were executed nationwide during the month of Mehr](../2026-10-24-mehr-ayi-idam-istatistigi-insan-haklari/) (September 22–October 21, 2026), illustrating the scale of the climate of repression in which Vafaei Sani's case is situated.

**What's next:** Vafaei Sani's lawyer Babak Paknia and human rights organizations are closely monitoring whether the execution moves into implementation phase. According to HRANA's earlier reports, the case had previously been referred to the execution unit before the Supreme Court ordered a retrial; the awarding of the Magnitsky Prize at this stage is understood as a call to keep the case on the international agenda.
