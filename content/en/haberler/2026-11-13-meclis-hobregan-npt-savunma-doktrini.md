The Assembly of Experts, in a statement released Thursday, described a review of the country's defense doctrine as an "inevitable necessity." According to the full text published by Ettela'at, the body thanked the public for its presence in the streets during an eight-month "resistance period," and stressed the need for cultural matters (especially "modesty and Islamic dress"), economic and livelihood concerns, NPT withdrawal demands, and a re-evaluation of defense doctrine, alongside the continuation of resistance and "honorable diplomacy"; it also condemned court decisions against clergy in regional countries.

The statement noted that repeated enemy threats to Iran's nuclear capabilities show that international institutions cannot be trusted. It declared that "the question of NPT withdrawal and the creation of necessary deterrence has become an inevitable necessity and has transformed into a national, public demand." The text called on officials to heed this demand with guidance from the Supreme Leader's "wise directions." The statement also warned that should defeated enemies attempt a new military attack, Iranian forces would respond "immediately and effectively with no geographic limitation"; it called on foreign-policy institutions not to "make concessions" to the U.S., stressing that "even the smallest conciliatory approach toward the enemy would squander the great achievements gained through the blood of martyrs."

According to the text released by Ettela'at, the statement also included an economic proposal: the Assembly of Experts suggested establishing a "war economy headquarters" with "participation of different powers and high-level supervision by the Supreme Leader's office"; it described the enemy's attempts to blockade the country by air and land and damage its economic infrastructure as "part of economic warfare" and characterized solving the public's livelihood problems as officials' "most important duty." The statement alluded to the "courageous fighters" in Yemen and resistance forces in Lebanon, noting that "united fronts" strategy has proven effective and support for allies on the resistance front should remain a priority. It argued that the crises and protests in European countries, as well as the defeat of "war-mongers" in elections in the U.S. and Israel, showed the cost of "partnership with aggressors," and expressed "hope" that these countries would stop supporting "America's tyrannical regime and the illegitimate Zionist regime."

In a section devoted to cultural matters, the statement alleged that "social abnormalities" in matters of "modesty and Islamic dress" are part of the enemy's plan to implode Islamic society from within; it called on relevant institutions to take "greater than before" measures in this area, and stated that dress codes are "a religious and legal duty and a public desire." The text argued that just as the nation's territory is protected by the blood of thousands of martyrs, so too the cultural sphere must be protected against attack by "malicious enemies."

According to Iran International, the statement represents a continuation of a trend that has been unfolding for weeks following the 2026 law that mandates the government suspend cooperation with the IAEA: numerous parliamentarians and officials have called for NPT withdrawal and a change to the nuclear doctrine. The same source reported that High National Security Council Secretary Mohsen Rezaei said on November 9 that no formal decision on nuclear doctrine has yet been taken, but discussions are underway on whether existing doctrine would be maintained in any new war. Iran International also contextualized the statement's emphasis on Islamic dress: according to the source, the judiciary announced on November 10 that it had detained a group of people in Tehran's Iranshahr street on charges of "promoting indecency," and around the same time cafes in Maragheh and a bookstore in Tehran were sealed for the same charge; the number and identity of those detained were not shared with the public.

## Context

**Background:**
- **June 2026 ("12-day war"):** The Iranian Parliament passed a law requiring the government to suspend IAEA cooperation with 221 affirmative votes out of 223 voting members present.
- **November 2, 2026:** Multiple parliamentarians called for NPT-withdrawal bills to be put on the Parliament's agenda; at least ten such bills were reported to be registered ([details](../2026-11-02-iran-meclis-npt-cikis-talepleri/)).
- **November 9, 2026:** High National Security Council Secretary Mohsen Rezaei said no new decision on the nuclear doctrine has yet been made, but discussion continues (Iran International, November 2026).

**Who / concepts:** The *Majles-e Khobregan-e Rahbari* (Assembly of Experts) is an 88-member body, mostly senior clergy, with constitutional authority to elect, oversee, and if necessary remove the Supreme Leader; it should not be confused with the *Majles-e Shura-ye Eslami* (Islamic Consultative Assembly, Parliament).

**By the numbers:**
- According to Iran International, over 70 parliamentarians had previously signed a call for nuclear doctrine revision (Iran International, November 2026).
- An NPT-withdrawal bill was first introduced in September 2025, when the UN Security Council's "snapback" mechanism reimposed sanctions (Arms Control Association, 2025).
- The June 2026 law suspending IAEA cooperation passed with 221 affirmative votes out of 223 voting members ([details](../2026-11-02-iran-meclis-npt-cikis-talepleri/)).

**What to watch:** The statement called on officials to follow up on NPT withdrawal but set no specific timeline. Because the constitution vests final strategic authority in foreign policy with the Supreme Leader, it remains unclear whether an actual withdrawal decision will be made. Whether the proposed "war economy headquarters" will be established, which institutions will participate, and whether the statement's call for increased dress-code enforcement will lead to new detentions or closures in coming days are all matters to watch.
