The Revolutionary Guards Corps said Friday morning in "Statement No. 22" that in response to an "enemy air attack" on a telecommunications tower in Sirik, Hormozgan Province, it had targeted and destroyed an early-warning radar of the "aggressive Americans" on the island of Bahrain. According to IRNA's reporting, the statement says the Aerospace Force of the IRGC carried out this strike "hours before"; ISNA published the same announcement simultaneously with IRNA.

The Guards' statement opens with a religious preamble, asserting that the "courageous and glorious Iranian people's eight-month saga of historical resistance" has awakened world peoples and "drawn youth from many nations to the field." Referencing this popular support, the statement says combat units continue to gain honor in the field "with reliance on Almighty God and trust in this boundless people support," then continues: "Hours ago, in response to an enemy air attack on a telecommunications tower in Sirik, our Aerospace Force units targeted and destroyed the early-warning radar of the aggressive Americans on the island of Bahrain." The statement describes this action as a "serious warning" toward ending "air mischief and oil smuggling" in the Strait of Hormuz, declaring that the Aerospace Force stands "fully prepared for unlimited response" should violations be repeated. The statement also warns that companies that have "surrendered to the enemy" will face "heavier punishment," and closes with the assertion that "not even the slightest conciliatory approach toward the enemy will squander the great achievements gained through the blood of martyrs."

According to Hamshahri, residents of Sirik on the eastern shore of Hormozgan heard several explosions in recent hours; the nature of the blasts remains unclear, with some sources interpreting them as "warning shots at violating ships." The same report recalled that Iranian military forces had previously cited two "violating" tankers that hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz; whether these two events—tanker mines and Sirik explosions—refer to the same incident remains unconfirmed. Donya-ye Eqtesad, in a separate report citing IRNA, wrote that "official sources confirmed the attack on Sirik" but provided no details about who carried it out or with what weapons; the same site's news feed also carried separately the military's claim that "two tankers hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz," but the chronological sequence and relationship of these two reports remain unclear.

The timing is also striking: the Guards' statement announced the alleged Bahrain radar strike but did not first disclose the Sirik "enemy air attack" to the public—only the response strike was made public. For this reason, details about the Sirik attack—what target was hit, with what weapons, and when—rest solely on the Guards' own account and have not been independently verified.

In ISNA's report, the Guards' statement is noted as directly using the phrase "aggressive American military" and particularly emphasizing the radar's "early-warning" function. Neither IRNA nor ISNA provided additional information about whether the radar has been disabled, whether it can be repaired, or about any other damage.
