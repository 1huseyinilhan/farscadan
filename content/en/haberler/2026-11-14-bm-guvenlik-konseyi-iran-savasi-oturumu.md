During the UN Security Council's "Situation in the Middle East" session, Russian and Chinese representatives called Friday (November 13) for an immediate end to U.S. and Israeli military operations against Iran. According to Donya-ye Eqtesad, Iran's UN Permanent Representative and Minister Counselor Gholamhossein Darzi warned in the same session that any new U.S. attack would be met with "immediate and decisive" retaliation.

According to the full text published by Donya-ye Eqtesad, Darzi said, "Iran has never closed the door to diplomacy over the past two years" and that repeated such experiences had created deep distrust, making progress impossible without credible guarantees for renewed negotiations. Darzi used the language: "Let me be clear and direct: should the U.S. and Israeli regime resume their aggressive war, it will be met with an immediate and decisive response, with no room for doubt, targeting the source of the aggression. The U.S. representative has neither legal, political, nor moral standing to accuse Iran."

According to Hamshahri, citing Mehr, Russia's UN Security Council representative charged that the U.S. and Israel have set the region ablaze with aggression against Iran, sparking a dangerous chain of regional conflict; the representative also cited a purported missile attack on a school in Mina'bad that killed more than 150 children and an alleged assassination attempt against Iran's leadership among events that "shocked the international community." The Russian representative added that the International Atomic Energy Agency has recorded no evidence of Iran diverting nuclear material or transferring it for military purposes. None of these claims were independently verified or confirmed by independent or Western sources. According to Hamshahri, citing Tasnim, China's representative said conflicts must be halted immediately, that war has no winners, and that unilateral military action must cease; thanking Qatar and Pakistan for mediation efforts, China called for the U.S. and Iran to return to the "Islamabad Accord."
