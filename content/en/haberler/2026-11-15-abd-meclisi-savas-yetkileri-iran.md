In the U.S. House of Representatives, a bipartisan group of lawmakers is preparing a new war powers resolution to restrict President Donald Trump's ongoing military operations against Iran. According to Hamshahri's reporting, some U.S. legislators say that more Republicans could break with Trump and support this resolution.

Republican Representative Thomas Massie from Kentucky told Fox News that the new resolution on Trump's war powers against Iran could be brought to the House floor "within the next few working days of Congress or around the Christmas recess." According to Hamshahri, this would mark the fourth attempt within 2026 to restrict Trump's aggressive military action against Iran. Massie expressed hope that more Republicans would join the effort, saying "I'm building a life raft for Republicans to escape the MAGA movement."

According to reporting from IRNA, Republican and Democratic lawmakers are preparing a new push to question Trump and his "aggressive war" against Iran; Donya-ye Eqtesad headlined the story "Are Republicans joining the Democrats?" **Key concepts:** A War Powers Resolution (*War Powers Resolution*) is a type of measure under a 1973 law that can compel the U.S. President to halt military operations conducted without Congressional approval. Even if passed by the House, it requires Senate approval and the President's signature or a veto override.

**By the numbers:**
- The House conducted at least five separate votes on Iran war powers in 2026 (April, May, June, July, September); two were rejected, three passed (Al Jazeera, Arab News, 2026).
- In the September 2026 vote, seven Republicans supported the resolution, an increase from the four who supported it in June and July (Al Jazeera, September 16, 2026).

**What's next:** According to Massie, the new resolution could be taken up in the coming congressional working days or around the Christmas recess; even if passed by the House, it remains uncertain whether the resolution will pass the Senate or face a Trump veto. It should also be noted that overriding a Trump veto would require a two-thirds majority—a threshold none of the five previous votes have approached.
