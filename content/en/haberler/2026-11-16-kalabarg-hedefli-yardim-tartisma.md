Hamid Pourmmuhammadi, head of Iran's Planning and Budget Organization, reignited the targeted-assistance debate over kalabarg by saying that universal, equal payments to all support beneficiaries is "not the right method." According to Khabaronline's account, Pourmmuhammadi emphasized that the government should adjust its support based on households' needs and vulnerability levels, directing scarce resources first to the most vulnerable and neediest groups, then only after their needs are met should the government consider expanding support to other households.

According to Khabaronline's report, the most common reaction in reader comments was that the government had backed away from its promise to pay kalabarg to all income brackets. One user wrote: "So is this it? You said you'd give kalabarg to everyone, didn't you?" Another wrote: "You promised us you'd give kalabarg to everyone." Complaints also surfaced about the fairness of income-bracket classifications; one user reported that their bracket number had been raised by the government from "1" (poorest) to "9" in the previous month, with no justification given and no increase to their income. Other users noted that the real demand is not for increased kalabarg, but for controlling inflation: "We're not expecting kalabarg increases; we expect inflation to be controlled."

According to Khabaronline's report, some users questioned the bracket-classification method itself; one reader wrote that high-income individuals who distribute their wealth among family members could appear in low brackets while concealing their true financial status, resulting in unfair distribution. Another user suggested aid be given as cash rather than through the designated basket, allowing households to spend based on their basic needs for rent, food, and transportation: "Increase aid, give cash not a basket; rent, food, and taxi fares are covered accordingly."

## Context

**Background:**
- **Nov. 9, 2026:** Farsçadan reported in a previous story that roughly 10% of households eligible under the general kalabarg program would be removed from coverage that same week.
- **June 2023:** The current electronic kalabarg system was established as the "National Credit Network."
- **Nov. 16, 2026 (today):** The head of the Planning and Budget Organization elevated the targeted-versus-universal aid debate to the official level by saying universal equal payments are wrong.

**Who is who / concept:** *Bracket* (dehek in Persian) is Iran's official system for classifying households by income and asset levels, ranked from 1 (poorest) to 10 (wealthiest); the scope of kalabarg and other social assistance is determined by bracket number. *Kalabarg*, a system of electronic credit for food purchases, has detailed information in the dictionary entry.

**By the numbers:**
- According to Khabaronline, one user reported that kalabarg credit is around 500,000 tomans per month and falls short of daily expenses (Khabaronline, Nov. 16, 2026).
- According to Farsçadan's Nov. 11, 2026 report, supplemental kalabarg credit for undernourished children in the lowest-income households was around 20 million rials per child (roughly 2 million tomans).
- In the same report, roughly 10% of eligible households would be removed from coverage under the general kalabarg program (Farsçadan, Nov. 11, 2026).

**What's next:** The government's process of updating bracket classifications and clarifying the share of households to be removed continues; no final official list or date has yet been shared. Whether Pourmmuhammadi's statement corresponds to a legal regulation or merely an intent declaration will become clear through subsequent official announcements.

**Fairness note:** User comments and official statements were directly attributed to their sources; no editorial commentary or speculation about government intent was included.
