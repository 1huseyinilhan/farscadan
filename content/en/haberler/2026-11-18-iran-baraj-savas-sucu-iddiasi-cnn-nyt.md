According to Ettelaat, Iran's Foreign Ministry Legal and International Affairs Deputy Kazem Gharibabadi, in a post on X on November 17, characterized CNN's report on Iran planning to use chemical weapons in Washington as "shameful lies and historical reversal." Gharibabadi wrote that Iran is among the greatest victims of chemical weapons, and that today's accusers—especially the U.S.—have supported those who wielded such weapons against Iran.

According to Ettelaat's report, Gharibabadi stated in the same post that the New York Times had normalized an attack plan against Iran's dams by using the pretext of "proximity to alleged missile facilities," and said the newspaper could not escape responsibility for the humanitarian and legal consequences of its reporting. For Gharibabadi, targeting civilian critical infrastructure is a war crime, and normalizing such targeting "will not go unpunished."

According to Khabaronline's account, Iran's UN Permanent Mission in New York also issued a statement on X that same evening characterizing NYT's dam stories and CNN's "dirty bomb" claim ([Farsçadan](../2026-11-17-cnn-iran-washington-kimyasal-silah/)) as "a calculated disinformation and misleading information campaign designed to lay the ground for further aggression and war crimes." The Mission stated that intentional attacks on civilian dams and water infrastructure are a serious violation of international humanitarian law and constitute war crimes; those who order, plan, approve, or carry out such attacks bear criminal responsibility. The original content of CNN and New York Times's reports in question was not independently accessed for this report; the accuracy of the claims and Iran's responses could not be **independently confirmed**.

In Kayhan's coverage of the same issue, CNN and NYT's claims were presented as evidence of U.S. defeat in the war; the newspaper framed the subject together with earlier allegations about schools and hospitals under the heading "new evidence of U.S. desperation." A commonality among these three sources is that rather than conveying the specifics of CNN and NYT's original reports, they emphasize the responses given by Iranian officials; for this reason, the concrete content of the alleged attack plan against dams could not be independently confirmed for this report.

## Context

**Background:**
- **April 2026:** Gharibabadi said Trump's threats to strike power plants and bridges could constitute war crimes under Rome Statute Article 8(2)(b) (Kurdistan24).
- **June 10, 2026:** Following strikes on two water reservoirs in southern Iran, legal experts evaluated whether the strikes could constitute war crimes; U.S. military called the strikes "lawful self-defense" (Tehran Times, Guardian account).
- **A UN-backed fact-finding mission** found "reasonable grounds" that U.S. forces were responsible for strikes on a school in Minab and a sports facility in Lamerd (Tehran Times).
- **November 17, 2026 (today):** Gharibabadi and the UN Mission rejected CNN and NYT's new reports using the same "war crime" framework.

**Who is who / concepts:** *Kazem Gharibabadi* is Iran's Deputy Foreign Minister for Legal and International Affairs and also serves as Iran's Permanent Representative to the IAEA. *Article 8(2)(b) of the Rome Statute* defines war crimes within the jurisdiction of the International Criminal Court, including intentional targeting of civilian objects.

**By the numbers:**
- The UN-backed fact-finding mission found "reasonable grounds" in at least 2 separate incidents (Minab school, Lamerd sports facility) that U.S. forces were responsible (Tehran Times).
- In the June 2026 water reservoir strikes, an estimated 20,000 people in the region had their water sources affected (account by international legal experts, Guardian).

**What's next:** Iran's UN Mission announced it would exercise its right to defend itself "in accordance with international law"; no response from CNN or NYT was reported as of this report's preparation. Gharibabadi's earlier statements referencing the Rome Statute suggest Iran may be signaling intent to refer such allegations to international legal mechanisms in the future; whether such a referral will be made has not been announced as of this report.
