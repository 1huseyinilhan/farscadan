According to ISNA, the U.S. Justice Department announced on Tuesday, November 17, that a Pakistani national accused of planning an assassination of Donald Trump had been sentenced to over 27 years in prison. According to Iran International's account, the convicted person's name is Asif Merchant, and U.S. authorities describe him as an individual with IRGC (Revolutionary Guard) connections; Hamshahri's report states the sentence as 330 months, or 27.5 years.

According to Iran International's compilation, the case was decided by a federal jury in Brooklyn, and the prosecution claimed the plot was designed as retaliation for the January 2020 killing of Qasem Soleimani, commander of the IRGC's Quds Force, by a U.S. drone. According to U.S. prosecutors, the plan targeted not only then-President Trump but also then-President Joe Biden and former Ambassador Nikki Haley. Merchant testified in court that he was forced into the plot to protect his family in Tehran from the IRGC; Iran's official position rejects allegations that it targeted Trump or other U.S. officials. Neither side of these mutual allegations has been **independently confirmed**; the full text of the court decision on the precise grounds for sentencing was not accessible for this report.

According to ISNA's report, U.S. authorities made this announcement at a time when warnings about the possibility of Iran conducting terrorist acts on U.S. soil have increased. According to JNS's English account, prosecutors claimed Merchant sought out two separate contract killers, one of whom turned out to be a Secret Service informant, and that the plot was exposed through this person. Merchant's attorneys argued that their client did not intend to carry out the act and was only seeking to escape pressure from the IRGC; the jury rejected this defense.

The case is presented as one of several similar investigations publicly disclosed by U.S. authorities in recent years involving individuals linked to Iran allegedly targeting former or sitting U.S. officials. The U.S. Justice Department has previously charged Iran-linked individuals with a separate assassination plot against former National Security Advisor John Bolton. The direct connection between these cases was not explained by U.S. authorities; each case was handled separately in its own legal proceeding.

## Context

**Background:**
- **January 3, 2020:** IRGC Quds Force Commander Qasem Soleimani was killed in a U.S. drone strike in Baghdad; Iranian officials vowed retaliation from that date forward (JNS).
- **2024 (date unclear):** Asif Merchant's arrest and charges related to the assassination plot became public (JNS).
- **March 2026:** A federal jury in Brooklyn found Merchant guilty of murder-for-hire and crossing national borders to conduct terrorism; the sentencing date had not been set at that time (JNS).
- **November 17, 2026 (today):** The U.S. Justice Department announced Merchant had been sentenced to 330 months (27.5 years) in prison.

**Who is who / concepts:** *Sepah-e Quds* (Quds Force) is the unit of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) responsible for operations outside Iran. *Murder-for-hire* and *crossing national borders to conduct terrorism* are two separate federal criminal charges in U.S. law to which the defendant was subject.

**By the numbers:**
- According to the U.S. Justice Department's announcement, Merchant was sentenced to 330 months (27.5 years) in prison (Hamshahri, ISNA).
- According to JNS, the defendant faced a maximum life sentence prior to conviction.
- Soleimani's killing occurred on January 3, 2020, in a U.S. air strike (JNS, international press accounts).

**What's next:** Whether Merchant's attorneys will appeal the verdict has not been announced as of this report; Iran's official authorities issued no separate statement regarding today's sentencing.

*Information about Asif Merchant available to this report is based on a concluded jury verdict and public court records in the United States.*
