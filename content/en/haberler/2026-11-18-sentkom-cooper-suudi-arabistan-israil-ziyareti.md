According to Hamshahri, CENTCOM Commander Admiral Brad Cooper will travel to Saudi Arabia this week with more than 36 military advisors. According to Hamshahri's report citing Middle East Eye, the purpose of the visit is to assess Saudi Arabia's air defense posture and examine options to strengthen the country's defensive capacity against air threats.

According to a separate Hamshahri report, before traveling to Saudi Arabia, Cooper entered Tel Aviv on Tuesday, November 17. According to Israel's Channel 12, Cooper met in Tel Aviv with Israeli Chief of Staff Eyal Zamir and other senior military officials; the channel characterized the visit as part of "routine visits" aimed at strengthening security and military coordination between Washington and Israel. According to Hamshahri's report, this visit followed a trip by U.S. 5th Fleet Commander Kurt Renshaw to Israeli officials, in which maritime operations and military cooperation were discussed and which ended on Friday.

According to Donya-ye Eqtesad's account, IRGC spokesman Brigadier General Hossein Mohibbi posted a sarcastic message on X that same day directed at Cooper. The image posted showed a "medal" symbolizing a downed U.S. drone around Cooper's neck; the caption claimed this represented the losses in drone aircraft that Iran's Armed Forces allegedly inflicted on Cooper's command's drone fleet: 18 MQ-1s and 57 MQ-9s, totaling 75 drones. This figure was not confirmed by the U.S. or CENTCOM; IRGC drone loss claims were **not independently verified** at the time this report was prepared.

CENTCOM itself stated in an October 2026 announcement that U.S. forces and allies in the region are protected by "the world's largest air defense umbrella" (The National News); Cooper's planned visit to Saudi Arabia is being characterized as consistent with this narrative as an air defense assessment. According to Middle East Eye's account citing Western officials, the timing of the visit coincides with the latest wave of strikes the IRGC allegedly carried out against U.S. bases in Bahrain and Kuwait ([Farsçadan](../2026-11-18-bahreyn-kuveyt-pehpad-saldirisi-tirmanis/)); however, CENTCOM made no statement indicating the visit was organized in response to any specific strike.

## Context

**Background:**
- **March 2026:** According to CENTCOM's official statement, Cooper met with Israeli Chief of Staff in Tel Aviv (CENTCOM official website).
- **August 2026:** According to CENTCOM's announcement, Cooper completed a 10-day region tour covering Bahrain, Iraq, Israel, Jordan, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE, and visited USS Abraham Lincoln (Kurdistan24, CENTCOM).
- **November 17, 2026 (today):** Cooper visited Tel Aviv; announcement made that he will travel to Saudi Arabia with 36 advisors; IRGC spokesman responded the same day with sarcastic post.

**Who is who / concepts:** *CENTCOM* (United States Central Command) is the regional command responsible for U.S. military operations in the Middle East and Central Asia; its commander is Admiral Brad Cooper. *Eyal Zamir* is the Chief of Staff of Israel's Defense Forces (IDF). *5th Fleet* is the U.S. Navy unit responsible for maritime operations in the Persian Gulf, Red Sea, and surrounding areas, with its headquarters in Bahrain.

**By the numbers:**
- According to CENTCOM's August 2026 announcement, the region tour covered 6 countries and included a carrier visit (CENTCOM).
- According to the IRGC spokesman's claim, Iran's Armed Forces downed a total of 75 drones (18 MQ-1, 57 MQ-9) under Cooper's command; this figure has **not been independently verified** (Donya-ye Eqtesad).
- According to Hamshahri's report, more than 36 military advisors will accompany Cooper to Saudi Arabia.

**What's next:** According to Hamshahri, Cooper's Saudi Arabia visit will take place this week; CENTCOM had not issued an official statement with the exact date at the time of this report's preparation. How much the visit will increase U.S. support to Riyadh's air defense systems remains unclear.
