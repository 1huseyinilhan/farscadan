According to Khabaronline's report from Kayhan, the protest gathering that began in Abadan on November 14 following a bus crash that killed 13 workers has, in the newspaper's view, become a movement directed by "enemies." Khabaronline reports that Kayhan describes the crash itself as an "inexcusable error" while treating the protest's progression as a separate matter. The workers killed in the crash were employees of the NGL Dehloran refinery, traveling a daily 150-kilometer route along the Dasht-i Abbas-Abadan highway; according to Kayhan, that the contractor failed to provide adequate housing for these workers was an "inexcusable negligence," and grieving families had the right to pursue those responsible through legal channels.

According to Khabaronline's account of Kayhan's view, the gathering that initially drew some 300 people with "purely trade-union and livelihood" demands underwent a change in direction, allegedly steered by "foreign elements, Gonabadi dervishes, and groups linked to Kurdish separatists." The newspaper claims that a crowd of 1,500 was then led to march on a mosque. Kayhan states that the slogans shifted from worker rights to expressions of support for "monarchist terror organizations"; the paper also claims that opposition groups disagreed among themselves over "exploiting blood." Kayhan argues that while the majority of Abadan's population should be separated from these minority actions, the protest should not be viewed as merely a trade-union action but as a "live test" of an "Dey-month uprising model" being conducted simultaneously with "internal erosion and external threats."

Iran International reports that at the November 17 protest, demonstrators chanted "Death to the dictator" and "No more war, the Pahlavi returns," and tore down a Revolutionary Guards banner. Iran International states that in messages to diaspora opposition channels, Abadan residents recalled a symbolic "rice scattering" action they performed during the January 2026 (Dey 1404) protests; some readers viewed this gesture as a symbol of resistance. The two sources frame the events in starkly opposite ways, and **whether the demonstrators' political demands represent "foreign direction" or were spontaneous remains unconfirmed**.

## Context

**Background:**
- **November 14, 2026:** A bus carrying Dehloran refinery workers overturned; 13 were killed, and protests began in Abadan ([Farsçadan](../2026-11-18-abdanan-otobus-kazasi-protesto/)).
- **January 16, 1405 (January 2026, Dey 16 in Persian calendar):** Abadan demonstrators performed a symbolic "rice scattering" gesture during anti-government actions; the image became one of the recognized protest symbols of that period (Iran International).
- **November 17, 2026 (the day covered in this report):** Protests continued; Kayhan published commentary on "enemy direction," and opposition channels received solidarity messages.

**Key terms / concepts:** *Fifth column* (ستون پنجم), a term frequently used by Kayhan and hardline press, refers to people or groups alleged to work for foreign powers within a country; in this report, the term is used to characterize protesters as "foreign-directed."

**By the numbers:**
- According to Kayhan via Khabaronline, the gathering grew from a 300-person core to 1,500 participants.
- The death toll from the crash remains 13 according to previous Farsçadan reporting; this figure did not change in today's sources.

**What's next:** An official statement from Ilam's prosecutor's office on the crash investigation and possible arrests related to the protest has not yet been released; developments on this will be monitored. While Kayhan continues to frame the issue as a security matter of "simultaneous internal erosion and external threat," when authorities will publicly share the results of the crash investigation remains unspecified.

**Disclaimers observed:** Assessment of whether the protest was "externally directed" or spontaneous was attributed only to Kayhan without reaching a conclusion; alleged "terrorist" connections were reported only as attributed to the source, not as direct claims.
