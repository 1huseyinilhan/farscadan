According to Ettelaat's report from IRNA, the Iran human rights resolution led by Canada was approved by the UN General Assembly's Third Committee on Wednesday, November 18, New York time. The resolution passed with 76 votes in favor, while 27 countries voted against and 64 abstained, meaning the combined total of opposing and abstaining votes (91) exceeded the affirmative votes. Iran International reports the same figures: 76 in favor, 27 against, 64 abstaining. The resolution will be sent to the full UN General Assembly for final approval.

According to Iran International, the resolution text describes the response to the January 2026 (Dey 1404) protests as "unprecedented" suppression, calls for a halt to executions, an end to arbitrary detention, and accountability for human rights violators. The text further argues that internet shutdowns aim to block access to information, sever communication between people, and conceal rights violations. Iran International reports that the resolution emphasizes that use of lethal force to disperse peaceful assemblies cannot be justified in any way, demands the immediate and unconditional release of those detained for exercising their rights, calls for the removal of excessive and disproportionate sentences imposed on protesters, demands independent investigations into the crackdown, and ensures access to justice for victims. The text also calls for a halt to executions of those under 18 at the time of offense and for conversion of sentences already imposed on minors to alternative punishments. According to Ettelaat's account, Iran's UN permanent representative Gholam-Hossein Dehghani characterized the resolution before the committee as "selective and politically motivated"; in his remarks, Dehghani stated that in U.S. and Israeli military attacks on Iran, the Shajar-e Tayyeba school in Minab was struck, killing at least 168 students and teachers, that in two wars a total of over 5,000 people—including 254 children and 251 women—lost their lives, but that resolution supporters made no mention of these casualties. In a separate report, Iran International states that on the same day, Amnesty International called on the UN General Assembly to establish an international judicial mechanism to prosecute those responsible for "crimes against humanity" in Iran.

## Context

**Background:**
- **November 19, 2025:** The equivalent resolution from the previous year passed the Third Committee with 79 in favor, 28 against, 63 abstaining (data from Ettelaat's prior-year reporting).
- **January 7, 1405 (January 2026, Dey):** The anti-government protests that the resolution text references began; the crackdown on these protests is a main basis for the resolution (Iran International).
- **November 18, 2026 (today):** The Third Committee approved the resolution 76-27-64; Amnesty International issued its call for an international justice mechanism the same day.

**Key terms / concepts:** *Third Committee*, a subsidiary organ of the UN General Assembly handling human rights, social, and cultural issues; resolutions approved here are sent to the General Assembly for final approval and are typically adopted. The resolution is called the "Canada resolution" in the press because it is annually sponsored by Canada.

**By the numbers:**
- This year's vote count: 76 in favor, 27 against, 64 abstaining (total opposing plus abstaining: 91).
- According to data Ettelaat provided from last year, the same resolution passed with 79 in favor, 28 against, 63 abstaining; this year the affirmative votes decreased by 3.

**What's next:** The date for the resolution's final vote in the full UN General Assembly has not yet been specified in the sources; in previous years, this vote has taken place in December.

**Disclaimers observed:** Iran's ambassador's accusations of "selectivity" and the justifications offered by resolution supporters were reported only as attributed to their respective parties; civilian casualty figures were reported with attribution to sources, and no independent verification was attempted.
