According to Hamshahri, Yahya Saree, spokesman for Yemen's Armed Forces, announced that his forces had struck King Khalid International Airport in the "enemy Saudi capital" of Riyadh and a financial center with numerous cruise missiles. According to Hamshahri's report from the Palestinian Sama news agency, following the strikes, Jeddah airport suspended operations and approximately five aircraft were forced to hold pattern; the same agency reported that Abha, Khamis Mushait, and southern Saudi Arabia were also among the targets. Hamshahri reported that Yemen's Al-Massirah channel, citing Saudi media, stated that alerts were issued for potential threats in Abha and then Khamis Mushait.

According to a separate IRNA report, King Khalid Airport and Riyadh's financial center were hit in Yemen's missile strikes; according to ISNA, this attack also encompassed two separate targets in southern Saudi Arabia. IRNA claimed that Saudi Arabia conducted 87 airstrikes on Yemen in the past 24 hours; this figure **remains unconfirmed** by independent sources. According to Al Jazeera's October 12, 2026 report, Houthi missile strikes targeting Riyadh extended on that date to a third consecutive day; this indicates that such attacks are not new or isolated but part of a pattern spanning months. **Whether the strikes caused casualties or property damage remains unconfirmed**, as no official Saudi statement was available at the time this report was written.

## Context

**Background:**
- **Since July 2026:** Houthi missile and drone strikes on Saudi airports and energy facilities intensified (Reuters/al-Monitor reporting via Donya-ye Eqtesad).
- **October 12, 2026:** Al Jazeera reported Houthis targeted Riyadh for a third consecutive day with missiles.
- **November 15, 2026:** According to Shafaq News, Houthi spokesman Saree announced they fired two ballistic missiles at King Khalid Airport and a Riyadh financial center; the Saudi-led coalition reported it "destroyed" the missiles.
- **November 17, 2026:** According to Hamshahri, flight operations at the same airport were again suspended following a Yemen-sourced drone attack ([Farsçadan](../2026-11-17-riyad-havalimani-pehpad-saldirisi/)).
- **November 19, 2026 (today):** According to Hamshahri and IRNA, the target scope expanded, causing Jeddah airport operations suspension.

**Key terms / concepts:** *Ansarullah* (the Houthi Movement), a political-military movement based in northern Yemen that has controlled much of the country since 2014 and is assessed to be close to Iran. Yahya Saree is the spokesman who regularly announces military statements from the movement. *King Abdullah Financial District* (King Abdullah Financial District), a business zone north of Riyadh concentrating banking and financial institutions; according to Shafaq News' November 15 report, the "financial center" Houthis reference as a target is assessed to be this district, though Saree has not confirmed the name.

**By the numbers:**
- According to Donya-ye Eqtesad's Reuters report, two Western officials stated Saudi Arabia plans to continue its military operations in Yemen for at least six more months, aiming to have allied forces gain ground before negotiations begin (Reuters, November 2026).
- According to Hamshahri, approximately five aircraft at Jeddah airport were forced to hold pattern due to operations suspension.

**What's next:** According to Donya-ye Eqtesad's Reuters report, Saudi and Yemeni government forces are repositioning to defend Aden against Houthi advances; these plans may also include shifting offensive focus to the eastern front. No official Saudi statement on today's strikes has yet been released.

**Disclaimers observed:** Claims about target impact or casualties were not reported without confirmation; IRNA's claim about airstrikes was marked with "unconfirmed."
