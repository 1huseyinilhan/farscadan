According to ISNA, the U.S. Senate rejected a resolution that would have required President Donald Trump to either end his military operations against Iran or obtain authorization from Congress. The Republican majority opposed it once again. According to Khabaronline's report, the vote was 48 to 51; Pennsylvania Democratic Senator John Fetterman broke ranks with his party and voted "no" alongside Republicans. Hamshahri confirmed that the resolution was rejected.

According to Khabaronline, this was the 12th war powers vote concerning Iran in the Senate this year; in previous votes, some Republican senators (Collins, Murkowski, Paul) had supported the resolution, but this time no new Republican votes came forward. The resolution carries only symbolic weight because even if both chambers approved it, Trump retains veto power. According to a separate IRNA report, Minnesota Democratic Representative Ilhan Omar also criticized budget allocations for "endless wars" and called for cutting financing for the conflict.

The rising cost of the war underlies the vote. According to figures IRNA cited from Pentagon reports, as of November 13 the U.S. war against Iran had cost $48.3 billion. According to IRNA, Minnesota Representative Omar said in remarks from the House floor that she opposed budget allocations for "endless wars" and called for immediate termination of war financing; she argued that every dollar spent is diverted from domestic health and education budgets.

According to a separate ISNA report, American journalist Max Blumenthal attributed the war to two main factors: a massive "accounting error" and the influence of circles close to Israel on Washington's decision-making apparatus. According to Blumenthal, the stated purpose of the war was to halt Iran's nuclear program, but the real target was regime change in Tehran; this assessment was presented on ISNA as commentary/analysis, the journalist's own opinion. According to the English-language Washington Times, this vote was the first war powers vote to come before the Senate after the Republicans suffered a heavy defeat in the November 3, 2026 midterm elections; nonetheless, no new Republican senator supported the resolution, suggesting that party discipline in the Senate remained unaffected by the election outcome.

According to Roll Call, the resolution had passed the House of Representatives in September, but with the Senate's rejection it did not reach the president; therefore, the outcome of the vote constitutes a rejection, not an authorization. According to Khabaronline, the most striking detail in the vote distribution was that only one Democrat (Fetterman) broke from the party this time; in previous votes this number had varied, with defections sometimes occurring on the Republican side as well.

## Context

**Background:**
- **February 28, 2026:** The start of the U.S.-Iran conflict; according to the Washington Times, war powers resolutions have come before the Senate repeatedly since then.
- **September 2026:** A war powers resolution passed the House of Representatives, but required Senate approval to become binding (Roll Call).
- **November 13, 2026:** According to figures the Pentagon reported to Congress, the war has cost $48.3 billion (IRNA).
- **November 15, 2026:** A bid to restrict Trump's Iran war authority came before the House again ([Farsçadan](../2026-11-15-abd-meclisi-savas-yetkileri-iran/)).
- **November 19, 2026 (today):** In the Senate's first war powers vote after the November 3 midterms, the resolution was rejected 48-51.

**Key figures and concepts:** The *War Powers Resolution* (1973) gives Congress the authority to limit or end a president's use of military force against another nation. Even if both chambers passed such a resolution, the president retains veto power, so these votes are largely symbolic. *John Fetterman* is a Democratic senator from Pennsylvania who broke ranks on this vote; *Ilhan Omar* is a Democratic member of the House from Minnesota who has opposed war financing.

**By the numbers:**
- Vote tally: 48 votes "yes," 51 votes "no" (Khabaronline, November 19, 2026).
- Estimated war cost: $48.3 billion, as of November 13, 2026, according to the Pentagon (IRNA).
- Total votes this year: Over 12 (Khabaronline, November 19, 2026).

**What comes next:** The House is expected to hold another war powers vote before year's end, according to Roll Call; no date has been announced. No timeline has emerged for the Senate's next vote. The day after this vote, a separate report citing the Financial Times said CENTCOM had warned the Trump administration that the window and resources for a military operation against Iran were narrowing ([Farsçadan](../2026-11-21-sentkom-trump-iran-saldiri-firsat-uyarisi/)).
