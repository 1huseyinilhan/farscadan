According to Donya-ye Eqtesad, citing Okaz, a Saudi Civil Defense Organization spokesman said on Thursday, November 19, that one of the towers in Riyadh's financial center was struck in an attack by Ansarollah (Houthi) forces, and that the tower and surrounding buildings sustained material damage on multiple floors, though no casualties were reported. According to Ettelaat's report, Saudi authorities were forced to acknowledge the damage after hours of silence and after news broke in Western media; image distribution within the country remains restricted.

According to Iran International, a photo shared by Saudi state agency SPA, though not formally identified, showed what appeared to be the tower of Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund (PIF), Riyadh's tallest building and the financial district's signature structure. The same report noted that the Houthis claimed they struck Riyadh's financial center twice on Thursday morning and again in the evening, and that they also targeted King Khalid International Airport, the Hamis Mushait air base in the country's south, and the Sadaf petrochemical facility in the Eastern Province. Saudi authorities have not yet confirmed these additional claims.

According to Donya-ye Eqtesad, Yemen's armed forces said in statements that in recent weeks they have frequently targeted Saudi Arabia's infrastructure and civilian targets in retaliation for Saudi airstrikes on Yemeni infrastructure and civilian sites; indeed, according to IRNA and ISNA RSS summaries, that same night Saudi Arabia continued airstrikes on Yemen's capital Sanaa and Taiz province, while multiple explosions were heard in southern Saudi Arabia. According to Hamshahri's brief report, alarms sounded in both Riyadh and the Al-Hassa region near the capital; citing Arab media, the report said the attack, framed as a "strategic operation," targeted sensitive sites in Riyadh, after which large explosions were heard throughout the city.

According to Ettelaat's account, Saudi authorities acknowledged the damage only hours after the incident and only after Western media reports emerged; the newspaper noted that this delay aligns with a previous pattern in which Saudi officials have generally not quickly confirmed Houthi attacks. The Saudi government's restriction on image sharing was characterized in the same report as a form of "blockade"—a news embargo on information; this assessment represents Ettelaat's own framing; no Saudi official comment on this characterization exists.

## Context

**Background:**
- **November 8, 2026:** The Houthis launched a major strike on the Aramco building in Riyadh's financial district; Saudi authorities characterized damage as "limited" and reported no casualties (according to Al Jazeera).
- **November 15, 2026:** Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree claimed they struck King Khalid Airport and Riyadh's financial center with two ballistic missiles; the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted two missiles and two drones (Al Jazeera).
- **November 19, 2026 (today):** The same financial center's PIF Tower was struck again and this time confirmed by Saudi authorities; Houthis claimed additional targets including the airport, air base, and petrochemical facility.

**Key figures and concepts:** *Ansarollah* (Ansar Allah), commonly called the "Houthis" in English, is an armed and political movement based in northern Yemen that has controlled the capital Sanaa since 2014 and maintains close ties to Iran. The *PIF* (Public Investment Fund) is Saudi Arabia's sovereign wealth fund; the struck tower serves as its headquarters and stands as Riyadh's tallest skyscraper, located in the King Abdullah Financial District.

**By the numbers:**
- The Houthis claimed two strikes on the financial center on Thursday—one in the morning and one in the evening (Iran International, November 19, 2026).
- According to Al Jazeera's November 15, 2026 report, the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted 2 missiles and 2 drones aimed at Riyadh in the same week.
- According to Al Jazeera, strike sequences directly targeting Riyadh occurred on at least three separate days since November 8, 2026.

**What comes next:** Saudi authorities have not released damage photos of the tower, and official confirmation of the other targets the Houthis claimed (King Khalid Airport, Hamis Mushait base, Sadaf facility) remains pending; these points are **unverified**. No Saudi statement has emerged on how the coalition plans to respond to the strikes. Indeed, on November 25 Saudi jets struck Yemen's Hodeidah port and airport; in retaliation, a Houthi spokesman named all Saudi Red Sea ports, including Jeddah, as targets ([details](../2026-11-25-yemen-hudeyde-kizildeniz-saldirisi/)).
