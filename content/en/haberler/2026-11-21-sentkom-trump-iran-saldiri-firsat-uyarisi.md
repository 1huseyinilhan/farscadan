According to Hamshahri, citing Financial Times, the U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM) informed Washington that the time window for possible military action against Iran is narrowing, and that U.S. forces' financial resources and combat readiness are eroding following roughly a year of intense regional deployment.

According to Donya-ye Eqtesad, citing the same FT report, U.S. and Israeli officials pressed Donald Trump to restart military operations against Iran before the calendar year ends. In response, U.S. military planners argued that nearly a year of regional deployment has depleted military resources and reduced the military's capacity to respond to other threats. The report suggests that some accounts show CENTCOM warning Washington about constraints on the time and resources needed to sustain operations.

Donya-ye Eqtesad wrote that one of the U.S. priorities during the recent "40-day war" was resupply of naval personnel; because regional U.S. logistics bases sustained damage, the military had to transport needed supplies and personnel from thousands of miles away. FT reports that this experience has amplified concerns within the Pentagon about the costs of renewed operations.

The report notes that Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has reiterated allegations about Iran's nuclear program and renewed calls for joint military action with the U.S.; someone identified as Mossad chief "Roman Gofman" sought U.S. administration support during a Washington visit. Donya-ye Eqtesad reports that Israeli security officials have discussed not only large-scale joint strikes but also more limited operation options. According to the report, Trump has so far avoided rushing into a decision to restart the war and previously resisted Netanyahu's pressure; FT notes that Trump swings between military threat rhetoric and the necessity of diplomatic channels.

According to Donya-ye Eqtesad, the Israeli government itself is also stalled; following early elections, government formation is deadlocked. Netanyahu's opponents in Israel worry that the prime minister might escalate military tension to protect his political position. FT reports that this domestic political pressure indirectly affects the decision-making process in Washington.

## How the Persian press covered it

Hamshahri reported the story as "CENTCOM warning Trump of lost opportunity to strike Iran," while the economically oriented Donya-ye Eqtesad covered the same FT report in greater detail, also addressing U.S. domestic political costs and Israel's government formation crisis. According to Donya-ye Eqtesad, FT noted that Iran war has become a contentious domestic political issue in the U.S. and that some Republican losses in midterm elections were attributed to voter dissatisfaction with the war.

## Context

**Background:**
- **February 28, 2026:** The U.S. and Israel are reported to have launched "Epic Fury" operations against Iran (international press).
- **November 18, 2026:** Farsçadan reported on CENTCOM Commander Cooper's visit to Saudi Arabia and Israel ([CENTCOM Cooper's Saudi Arabia-Israel visit](../2026-11-18-sentkom-cooper-suudi-arabistan-israil-ziyareti/)).
- **November 20, 2026:** U.S. Senate rejected new military authority restrictions against Iran ([U.S. Senate war powers vote](../2026-11-20-abd-senato-iran-savas-yetkileri-reddi/)).

**Who and what:** CENTCOM (United States Central Command) is the U.S. Department of Defense's regional command overseeing operations in the Middle East, Central Asia, and the Horn of Africa; headquartered in Florida. It is responsible for force deployment and combat readiness in the region. The command is headed by Commander Cooper; CENTCOM assessments feed directly into decision-making at the Defense Department and White House.

**By the numbers:**
- According to the Council on Foreign Relations, citing Israeli intelligence, the U.S. military could sustain intensive air operations against Iran for only 4-5 days and limited strikes for approximately one week (CFR, 2026).
- According to Axios, CENTCOM assessed that approximately 20% of unstrikes targets would require escalation to large-scale operations as one implementation option (Axios, July 26, 2026).

**Turkey dimension:** Incirlik Air Base near Adana, a NATO key installation, witnessed missile alarm reports allegedly originating from Iran during earlier stages of the conflict; Turkey denied these claims and emphasized that it maintains full control of the base. According to TRT World, the base hosts U.S., Spanish, Polish, and Qatari military personnel; Turkey's Foreign Ministry denied claims that the U.S. sought to use the base for Iran operations.

**What's next:** According to FT, U.S. and Israeli officials expect a decision before the calendar year ends; however, the Trump administration has not yet announced an official date or decision.
