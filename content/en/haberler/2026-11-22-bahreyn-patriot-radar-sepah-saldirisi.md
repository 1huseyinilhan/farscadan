The IRGC (Sepah) announced it struck two U.S. Army Patriot air-defense radars at the Al Rafa and Avali bases in Bahrain with missiles and drones. The IRGC stated the operation was in response to two U.S. surveillance drones that violated Iran's airspace. Bahrain and the U.S. military did not confirm the claims; CENTCOM issued a separate statement denying Iran's allegations. Damage scope is therefore "unconfirmed."

According to Khabaronline's report, IRGC Aerospace Forces announced under the codename "Yom Omm al-Benin" that it targeted Patriot systems at the Al Rafa and Avali bases. IRNA stated that U.S. Navy escort operations in the region failed and experienced "heavy equipment losses." Bahrain's Interior Ministry reported that air defenses blocked attacks Saturday evening, a fire broke out at a civilian industrial facility, and no casualties were reported. While Sepah said it targeted two Patriot batteries and one radar system, and Farsnews claimed four bases were hit, Bahrain and the U.S. military did not confirm these claims. That same night, the UK Maritime Trade Operations Center (UKMTO) reported two tankers departing Hormuz were targeted with projectiles/debris in separate incidents with no casualties reported. Bahrain's Foreign Ministry called the attacks "unlawful," demanding UN Security Council Resolution 2817 be enforced with "firm steps." Concurrently, ship-tracking site MarineTraffic showed an Iranian-flagged tanker named "Hatshekan" moving near Hormuz with a 22.5-meter draft consistent with heavy crude cargo, though the cargo source and destination could not be independently verified. Deputy Foreign Ministry Spokesman Gharibabadi stated tanker convoys would head toward China, confirming this to Washington.

## Context

**Background:**
- **March 11, 2026:** UN Security Council passed Resolution 2817 condemning Iran's missile/drone attacks on Gulf states and Jordan (13 votes, Russia and China abstaining).
- **November 17-19, 2026:** Consecutive drone attacks reported in Bahrain and Kuwait, plus tanker incidents in Hormuz (see [Bahrain and Kuwait drone attack](../2026-11-18-bahreyn-kuveyt-pehpad-saldirisi-tirmanis/)).
- **November 21-22, 2026:** CENTCOM warned Trump Iran's attack window was closing (see [CENTCOM warning to Trump](../2026-11-21-sentkom-trump-iran-saldiri-firsat-uyarisi/)); same night IRGC announced Bahrain strike.

**Who/concept:** CENTCOM (U.S. Central Command) oversees U.S. military operations in the Middle East and Central Asia. UKMTO (UK Maritime Trade Operations) monitors and reports security incidents affecting commercial shipping in Hormuz and the Persian Gulf.

**By the numbers:**
- **Resolution 2817:** 13 UN Security Council members voted yes, Russia and China abstained; introduced by Gulf Cooperation Council on behalf of Bahrain (Security Council Report, March 11, 2026).
- **2 Patriot radars:** Target number IRGC claimed; Farsnews suggested 4 bases were hit — discrepancy between claims remains unverified.

**What's next:** Bahrain's Foreign Ministry requested a Security Council meeting; date has not been announced.
