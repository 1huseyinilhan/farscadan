During the week of November 16-22, three parallel currents shaped the Persian press agenda: a failed diplomatic stream running alongside mounting economic costs of the naval blockade in the Strait of Hormuz; renewed fire exchanges in Bahrain, Kuwait, Riyadh, and Jeddah that exposed a decision deadlock in Washington; and a week at home where an industrial accident became mass protest and a winter energy crisis sparked public anger. This article draws from Farsçadan's coverage throughout the week. All information is attributed to the relevant news agency and source behind each report.

## The week's three headlines

### 1. Blockade breached at Hormuz, diplomacy yields no results

The week opened with mounting tension between the blockade in the Strait of Hormuz and efforts to breach it. According to IRNA, Monday morning UKMTO reported a security incident involving a tanker near Hasab port in Oman; the vessel's engine room caught fire ([Hormuz tanker incident](../2026-11-17-hurmuz-tanker-yangin-ukmto/)). The same pattern repeated two days later: IRNA reported that UKMTO announced an unidentified object striking another vessel exiting the strait ([Hormuz unidentified tanker incident](../2026-11-19-hurmuz-pertabe-tanker-ukmto/)). According to Donya-ye Eqtesad's report, the maritime tracking firm TankerTrackers claimed Iran was preparing to restart crude oil exports despite the blockade; however, official authorities did not confirm this claim ([oil export blockade claim](../2026-11-20-iran-petrol-ihracat-abluka-iddiasi/)). By week's end, Iran International cited Wall Street Journal reporting that Abu Dhabi's ADNOC, with U.S. military support, continued moving Iranian and Iraqi crude through the strait, but according to Financial Times analysis based on Kpler data, total oil flows through the strait in the second week of November had dropped by half compared to the first week ([ADNOC shipping through Hormuz](../2026-11-22-adnoc-hurmuz-petrol-rota-abluka/)).

On the diplomatic front, the week was busy but produced no breakthroughs. According to Hamshahri, Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi stated that the seven conditions for opening the Strait of Hormuz remained valid, that the June 2026 Islamabad agreement was no longer "on the table," and that 60-percent enriched uranium would not leave the country; he added that a two-day plan conveyed to Washington through Qatar still awaited response ([Araghchi: Seven conditions for Hormuz](../2026-11-21-araghchi-hurmuz-yedi-sart-nukleer-doktrin/)). According to Ettelaat, by week's end a Qatari delegation visited Tehran to meet with Spokesman Kasem Gharibabadi; Pakistani Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar called the accord "optimistic by nature," while Qatar and Germany issued a joint statement calling on Iran to abandon nuclear weapons ([Qatar-Pakistan-Germany mediation](../2026-11-22-katar-pakistan-almanya-iran-araci/)). According to Khabaronline, Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan said an Iran-U.S. agreement was important not only for opening the Strait of Hormuz but for preventing a global financial crisis ([Fidan: Iran-U.S. agreement](../2026-11-22-fidan-iran-abd-anlasma-hurmuz/)).

### 2. Regional fire reignites in the Gulf, Washington faces decision paralysis

The military front opened with Riyadh's King Khalid Airport closing after a Yemen-sourced drone attack ([Riyadh airport drone strike](../2026-11-17-riyad-havalimani-pehpad-saldirisi/)). The next night, according to IRNA, sirens sounded in Bahrain and Kuwait; the IRGC claimed it hit a U.S. radar and reconnaissance drone, and according to Iran International's compilation, U.S. warplanes struck a missile position in Qeshm, though neither side issued official confirmation ([Bahrain-Kuwait explosions](../2026-11-18-bahreyn-kuveyt-pehpad-saldirisi-tirmanis/)). A day later, according to Hamshahri, Yemeni Armed Forces spokesman Yahya Saree announced his forces struck Riyadh's airport and financial center with cruise missiles; the attack also disrupted Jeddah airport operations ([Yemen launches missile wave at Riyadh-Jeddah](../2026-11-19-yemen-husi-riyad-cidde-misket-saldirisi/)). The next day, according to Donya-ye Eqtesad citing Okaz, Saudi Civil Defense confirmed that the PIF Tower—Riyadh's tallest skyscraper—suffered damage in a Houthi attack; according to Ettelaat, this admission came after hours of silence and reports in Western media ([Riyadh's PIF Tower targeted](../2026-11-20-riyad-pif-kulesi-husi-saldirisi/)). The week ended with Khabaronline reporting the IRGC announced it struck two Patriot radars at the Al Rafa and Avali bases in Bahrain; Bahrain and U.S. forces did not confirm, CENTCOM denied it, and Bahrain called on the UN Security Council to enforce Resolution 2817 ([Bahrain Patriot radar strike](../2026-11-22-bahreyn-patriot-radar-sepah-saldirisi/)).

In Washington, this escalation overlapped with a decision deadlock. According to ISNA, the U.S. Senate again rejected legislation limiting Trump's military authority against Iran, 51 to 48; no new Republicans voted in support, and according to the Pentagon, the war's cost has exceeded $48.3 billion ([Senate rejected war powers decision](../2026-11-20-abd-senato-iran-savas-yetkileri-reddi/)). According to Hamshahri citing Financial Times, CENTCOM warned Washington that the time and resource window for possible military action against Iran is narrowing; Israeli and American officials want Trump to restart attacks before year's end, but military planners argue that a year-long deployment in the region has exhausted resources ([CENTCOM warning to Trump](../2026-11-21-sentkom-trump-iran-saldiri-firsat-uyarisi/)).

### 3. At home: accident, protest, and winter hardship pile up

The week at home centered on an industrial accident turning into mass protest. According to Ettelaat, a workers' minibus overturned in Ilam province, killing 13 engineers; the facility manager was dismissed and protests beginning in Abadan grew to nearly 1,000 people ([Abadan bus accident](../2026-11-18-abdanan-otobus-kazasi-protesto/)). According to Khabaronline citing Kayhan, the newspaper described the protest as an initiative steered by "enemies," while according to Iran International, solidarity messages continued flowing through opposition channels ([Abadan protest: Kayhan's "enemy" claim](../2026-11-19-abdanan-protesto-kayhan-dusman/)). Meanwhile, as winter approached, economic debate continued: according to Khabaronline, the head of the Plan and Budget Organization, Hamid Pourmohammadi, said equal kalabagh (electronic food credit) payments to all were not the right approach; the statement reignited accusations that the government had broken its word ([kalabagh targeted aid debate](../2026-11-16-kalabarg-hedefli-yardim-tartisma/)). According to Iran International, President Masoud Pezeshkian's remarks—"I sleep at home in my cloak, turning on no lights"—drew sharp rebuke from citizens facing economic hardship ([Pezeshkian's cloak remarks stir backlash](../2026-11-20-pezeskiyan-kaban-enerji-krizi-tepki/)). By week's end, Iran International reported that Tehran's main electricity distribution company announced a two-hour power cut; the spokesman attributed it to "grid maintenance" but said cuts could continue in coming weeks ([Tehran power outage](../2026-11-21-tahran-iki-saatlik-elektrik-kesintisi/)).

## Frame differences

The same events acquired different frames across political camps during the week.

| Source (orientation) | Event | Frame |
|---|---|---|
| Iran International (Opposition, diaspora, citing WSJ) | Oil flow at Hormuz | Presented "blockade breached" through U.S. military support. |
| Ettelaat (Established daily, citing FT/Kpler) | Same flow data | Emphasized "flow halved," showing blockade still effective. |
| Donya-ye Eqtesad (Economic daily, technocratic) | PIF Tower strike in Riyadh | Gave the Saudi civil defense admission as brief news without attribution. |
| Ettelaat (Established daily) | Same strike | Emphasized Saudi authorities forced to admit it after "hours of silence." |
| Khabaronline / IRNA (State news agency) | Patriot strike in Bahrain | Reported the IRGC's official statement as "retaliation for U.S. aggression." |
| Iran International (Opposition, diaspora) | Same strike | Added that Bahrain and U.S. did not confirm, CENTCOM denied it. |
| Kayhan (Hardline, cited by Khabaronline) | Abadan protest | Presented as "enemy-directed" and repeat of last year's uprising model. |
| Iran International (Opposition, diaspora) | Same protest | Prioritized protesters' slogans and solidarity messages. |

The sharpest contrast appeared in reading oil data at Hormuz: Iran International, centering the WSJ's ADNOC story and highlighting the U.S. military's role, while Ettelaat processed FT's Kpler-based "flow halved" frame that week—two papers relying on different sources but mutually compatible, no contradiction. In the security file, outlets close to the IRGC presented the Bahrain Patriot strike in triumphant terms, while Iran International stressed that the same claim went unconfirmed by Bahrain and the U.S.; in domestic politics, the tension between Kayhan's "enemy direction" frame and opposition media's solidarity emphasis remained unverified.

## Turkey angle

This week's Turkey connection came largely through Hormuz diplomacy. According to Khabaronline, Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan said he had met with the Qatari Prime Minister and that Ankara was doing its part to advance the mediation process, emphasizing that an Iran-U.S. agreement could prevent a global financial crisis ([Fidan: Iran-U.S. agreement](../2026-11-22-fidan-iran-abd-anlasma-hurmuz/)). According to Hamshahri citing Financial Times, Incirlik Air Base in Adana had previously been the site of missile alert alarms allegedly sourced from Iran in earlier stages of the conflict; Turkey had denied these claims and stressed that the base remains under Turkish Armed Forces control ([CENTCOM warning to Trump](../2026-11-21-sentkom-trump-iran-saldiri-firsat-uyarisi/)). According to assessments by Ember and bne IntelliNews, the Hormuz crisis could add $14 billion in costs to Turkey through year-end; about $7.7 billion stems from increased oil imports and $6.4 billion from increased natural gas imports ([Araghchi: Seven conditions for Hormuz](../2026-11-21-araghchi-hurmuz-yedi-sart-nukleer-doktrin/)). On the security front, according to ISNA, Turkish media reported new details in a Mossad espionage case targeting Palestinians in Turkey ([Mossad espionage case in Turkey](../2026-11-17-turkiye-mossad-casusluk-sorusturmasi/)).
