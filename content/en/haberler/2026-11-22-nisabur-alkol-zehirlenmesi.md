The number of people poisoned from homemade alcohol in Nishapur, Razavi Khorasan province, has reached 33, with three deaths reported. According to IRNA, Seyyid Milad Takdisi Haydarian, Deputy Director of Treatment at Nishapur Medical Sciences University, announced that two of the deceased experienced brain death.

According to Donya-ye Eqtesad's report, Takdisi Haydarian visited the city's 22 Bahman Education-Research-Treatment Center Saturday evening to assess the clinical condition of poisoned patients and their diagnosis and treatment. The official stated that patient conditions were under continuous monitoring, specialist care was provided, and coordination among treatment teams was maintained; he also said the number of poisoning cases from homemade alcohol consumption was increasing. Whether cases stem from a single batch of alcohol or multiple sources, and which neighborhoods were hardest hit, have not been clarified in official statements; these points "remain unconfirmed."

## Context

**Background:**
- **Alcohol production, sale, and consumption are prohibited under Iran's Islamic Criminal Code;** demand is therefore met through unregulated home distillation or illegal production, creating methanol contamination risk.
- **September-October 2018:** An academic study documented 768 methanol poisoning cases with 76 deaths (AP, 2018).
- **Before 2020:** According to AP, the Forensic Medicine Institute recorded only 66 total alcohol poisoning deaths the prior year — showing how exceptional the 2020 wave was.
- **February-April 2020:** According to Iran's Forensic Medicine Institute, 728 people died when false claims circulated that methanol cured COVID-19 (AP, April 2020).
- **October 2024:** AP reported at least 26 recent deaths from alcohol drinks containing methanol in Iran.
- **Access to illicit alcohol:** The Persian press reports these poisonings largely stem from unregulated home/garage distillation without reliable production or safety oversight; methanol is used to reduce costs, lowering the price.

**Who/concept:** "Mesmumiyet-e alkol-i dest-saz" (مسمومیت الکل دست‌ساز) is the Persian press term for poisonings from homemade/illicit alcohol. Methanol is a toxic alcohol that in small doses causes blindness and death; the body converts it via the liver into substances that damage the optic nerve and central nervous system. Nishapur, in Razavi Khorasan province west of Mashhad, is a historic city known as the birthplace of poet-mathematician Omar Khayyam; the region is also known for turquoise mining and pistachio production.

**By the numbers:**
- **33 poisoned / 3 dead:** Current Nishapur poisoning and death count; two brain deaths confirmed (IRNA, November 21, 2026).
- **728 deaths:** Forensic Medicine Institute's recorded toll in the 2020 methanol wave (AP, April 2020) — not directly connected to current incident, provided for scale comparison.
- **66 deaths:** Forensic Medicine Institute's total alcohol poisoning deaths the year before 2020 (AP) — shows relative weight of 3 deaths in a single city.

**What's next:** Takdisi Haydarian said the hospital would continue clinical monitoring and treatment coordination of poisoned patients; investigation results on the poisoning source (single batch/distribution point) have not been officially released.
