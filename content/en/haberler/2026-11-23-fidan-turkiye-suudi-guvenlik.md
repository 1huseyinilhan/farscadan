Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan said in remarks to TRT News that Ankara supports Saudi Arabia's security but this support does not mean sending troops. According to Kayhan's report from Persian sources, Fidan stressed that any potential troop deployment would require the approval of the Turkish Grand National Assembly.

According to Fidan, Ankara's current policy focuses on providing defense support to Saudi Arabia and no decision has been made regarding the deployment of Turkish soldiers on Yemeni territory. The minister stated that Turkey is providing necessary support and that defense and technical cooperation between the two countries continues. He had previously spoken of Turkey's contributions in air defense and electronic warfare. According to Kayhan, citing the Mecca Joint Defense Accord between Turkey, Saudi Arabia, and Pakistan, Fidan said this alliance was not established against any specific country; any broad military operation requires approval from the Turkish parliament.

Fidan's statement came as the Houthis, based in Yemen, have intensified drone and missile attacks on Saudi Arabia in recent weeks, with Riyadh's air defense systems repeatedly activated. The article does not specify when Fidan gave this interview, and no details were provided about specific military equipment to be supplied to Saudi Arabia or timelines.

## Context

**Background:**

- **October 8, 2026:** Fidan described the Mecca Accord as "defensive" and said Turkey would not send troops to attack another country; two officials told Reuters that Turkey's support would remain bilateral and technical for now, and that the accord had not yet been formally approved by Turkish parliament (Hürriyet Daily News, October 8, 2026).

- **November 20, 2026:** Riyadh's tallest tower, the PIF Tower, was targeted in a Houthi attack, with damage confirmed by Saudis (see [Riyadh's tallest tower](../2026-11-20-riyad-pif-kulesi-husi-saldirisi/)).

**Who and what:** The Mecca Joint Defense Accord is an alliance between Turkey, Saudi Arabia, and Pakistan that mutually commits to defense; according to Fidan, it was not established against any country and includes non-aggression guarantees among members.

**Turkey's dimension:** As Houthi attacks intensify, Turkey is providing technical support to Saudi Arabia on air defense and electronic warfare; however, without parliamentary approval, troop deployment is not an option. According to two officials speaking to Reuters, Turkey's support will be primarily defensive and technical in nature, and because the accord has not yet been formally approved by parliament, support will remain bilateral for now (Hürriyet Daily News, October 8, 2026).

**What's next:** Fidan's statement included no new timeline or date for parliamentary approval; previous statements on which military delegations would determine Saudi Arabia's operational needs were not updated. Ankara's expectations regarding CAATSA sanctions and the S-400 disagreement came up in a separate statement by Fidan the previous day, but no official link was established between this file and the defense support to be provided to Saudi Arabia.
