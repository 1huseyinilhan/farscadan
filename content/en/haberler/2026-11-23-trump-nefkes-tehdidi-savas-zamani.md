U.S. President Donald Trump said Sunday evening in remarks to Fox News that military strikes against Iran could resume after the new year if a deal cannot be reached. According to Hamshahri's account, Trump repeated that Iranian ships attempting to break the naval blockade would be "sent to the sea floor."

According to Ettelaat's account, Trump said indirect negotiations are ongoing but have not produced "concrete results": "The Iranians keep talking, they're very skilled at buying time and stalling." Trump added: "If there's no deal, we can strike Iran after the new year." According to Hamshahri's report, Trump also said, "I hope Iranian tankers try to break the blockade; we'll send them to the sea floor!" and specified the threat with the words: "We'll send all of them to the depths like what happened to the rest of the navy." According to Ettelaat, Trump said that Iranian ships attempting to violate the blockade would "reach the fate of other parts of the navy, the sea floor." According to Iran International's summary, Trump expressed his desire to resume strikes after the beginning of the new calendar year if no agreement is reached and threatened to sink ships attempting to break the blockade.

## Context

**Background:**
- **April 2026:** The U.S. announced a naval blockade of Iranian ports in the Strait of Hormuz; Trump said ships violating the blockade would be "instantly destroyed" (Fox News, April 2026).
- **May 2026:** Trump said if Iran targeted U.S. ships, the country would be "wiped off the map"; the U.S. launched a convoy operation called "Project Freedom."
- **November 21, 2026:** Foreign Minister Araghchi reiterated that opening Hormuz is conditional on seven conditions (see [Araghchi: 7 conditions for Hormuz](../2026-11-21-araghchi-hurmuz-yedi-sart-nukleer-doktrin/)).
- **November 22, 2026:** Abu Dhabi-based ADNOC was reported to be attempting to pierce the Hormuz blockade with alternative oil routes (see [ADNOC pierces Hormuz blockade](../2026-11-22-adnoc-hurmuz-petrol-rota-abluka/)).

**Who and what:** "Naval blockade" (abluka-ye deryâî) means a state uses military force to cut off maritime access to another country's ports, and under international law falls within the scope of laws of war.

**By the numbers:**
- **27 ships:** Total number of ships reported turned back since the blockade began (Fox News, April-May 2026 period).
- **9 tankers:** Number of ships CENTCOM reported attempting to break the blockade and stopped (Fox News, April 2026).

**What's next:** Trump said strikes could resume "after the new year" if no deal is reached; no specific date or ultimatum was announced. When indirect negotiations will continue was also not specified.
