Iraqi Prime Minister Ali Falih al-Zeydi linked the oil fields in Diyala, Kirkuk, Salah al-Din, and Nineveh to the federal Oil Ministry following an investigation into oil smuggling; these fields had previously been under the security supervision of the Kurdistan Regional Government (KRG). According to Iran International, the decision reignited longstanding tensions between Baghdad and Erbil.

According to Iran International, citing a statement by the Iraqi Prime Minister's Office on Tuesday, Zeydi made the decision after reviewing a report from a committee investigating smuggling allegations at the North Oil Company, which manages oil and gas fields in northern Iraq. Zeydi also instructed the Border Crossing Authority to assume control of all border crossings under KRG authority and ordered the regional government to export crude oil and petroleum products only through the federal Oil Ministry. The decision included removal of the Border Crossing Authority's head; in Kirkuk, the operational commander, provincial police chief, Energy Police 1st Brigade commander and deputy, and intelligence and checkpoint officials were dismissed for failing to monitor oil smuggling. The North Oil Company's general manager and deputies were also suspended pending investigation by the Federal Integrity Commission. According to Kurdish news network Rudaw, KRG Prime Minister Masrur Barzani described Baghdad's centralization of oil resources and border crossings as "entirely unacceptable." Iran International, citing Reuters, reports that the move follows Zeydi's efforts to rein in Kataib Hezbollah, the strongest armed group close to Tehran, and could deepen historic Baghdad-Erbil tensions.

## Context

**Background:**
- **September 25, 2017:** After the KRG's independence referendum, Baghdad reclaimed control of Kirkuk and disputed territories.
- **April 2023 – September 2025:** Disagreements over oil sales without Baghdad's approval; this blocked oil flow through the Kirkuk-Ceyhan line to Turkey for over two years.
- **September 2025:** The two sides agreed to resume pipeline oil flow.
- **November 9, 2026:** U.S. special envoy Tom Barrack backed direct Baghdad-Erbil talks on the 2027 federal budget and oil revenue sharing.
- **November 24-25, 2026:** Zeydi's centralization decision and the separate responses from two Barzani family members dominate this story.

**Who and what:** The North Oil Company (Sharikat an-Naft al-Shamaliya) operates oil and gas fields in northern Iraq. The Kirkuk-Ceyhan Pipeline carries oil from Kirkuk's fields to Turkey's Mediterranean terminal at Ceyhan. Al-Hashd al-Sha'abi (Popular Mobilization Forces) is Iraq's official security umbrella that includes Iran-aligned armed groups; Kataib Hezbollah is among the strongest and most Tehran-aligned within it.

**By the numbers:** The Kirkuk-Ceyhan Pipeline has a daily capacity of roughly 1.2 million barrels (Reuters, cited by The National, March 16, 2026). Zeydi's decision covers oil fields in Diyala, Kirkuk, Salah al-Din, and Nineveh (Iraqi Prime Minister's statement, cited by Rudaw, November 25, 2026).

**Turkey dimension:** The Kirkuk-Ceyhan line is the only pipeline through which KRG oil is exported via Turkey; disputes over sales without Baghdad approval had blocked the flow from April 2023 through September 2025 (The National, March 16, 2026). Baghdad's centralization of oil resources and border crossings constrains the KRG's independent export capacity; according to The National's November 16, 2026 analysis, it also complicates the KRG's plans for independent natural gas exports to Turkey.

**Next:** U.S. special envoy Tom Barrack's brokered direct talks between Baghdad and Erbil continue on the 2027 federal budget and oil-revenue sharing, with no firm date announced (Shafaq News, November 9, 2026).
