According to Iran International, the planned Wednesday morning execution of M.A. and N.N., defendants in the Ekbatan case, was halted at the last moment following intense pressure from their families and human rights organizations. UN Special Rapporteur on Iran Human Rights Mai Sato called for the executions to be stopped immediately. The legal proceedings against M.A. and N.N. continue; the presumption of innocence applies.

According to Iran International, M.A.'s attorney said he was notified by phone around 2 a.m. Wednesday (4 Azar) that the execution had been halted—hours before it was scheduled—and immediately informed M.A.'s family. On X (formerly Twitter), the attorney wrote: "I had a horrifying week. My client was acquitted of complicity in murder; nevertheless, the Revolutionary Court sentenced him to death on charges of 'spreading corruption on earth' (mofsed-i fi'l-arz) and execution was scheduled for Wednesday morning. Hours before the execution, I was informed by phone that it had been halted, and I told his family." At the same time, the Legal Advice Center Dadban, citing an informed source, reported that N.N. had returned to general dormitory housing at Kazel Hesar Prison following a phone call with family; there was no published statement confirming an official execution halt order for N.N. According to reports, the Telegram channel Emtedad announced on Tuesday (3 Azar) that the two inmates' lawyers had been informed of execution orders for Wednesday morning; the lawyers petitioned the High Court's 39th Chamber for retrial. According to HRANA, the High Court's 9th Chamber upheld the execution orders, but for two co-defendants in the same case, the court quashed the conviction and referred their files to an equivalent chamber for retrial. According to Iran International, M.A. and N.N.'s families and relatives gathered outside Kazel Hesar Prison on Tuesday evening; a video showed some citizens chanting "Death to the dictator" throughout the night. UN Special Rapporteur Sato noted that M.A. and N.N. were arrested in connection with 2022 protests and have faced consecutive legal proceedings for over three years; she called the death sentence based on a murder charge from which they were acquitted "difficult to comprehend" and raised allegations of torture and forced confessions. Lord Alton of the British House of Lords called the death penalty risk continuing after removal of the murder charge "deeply concerning." The office of former Crown Prince Reza Pahlavi also warned on the execution risk, stating: "Let us not remain silent—before it is too late, let us be the voice of Milad and Navid."

## Context

**Background:**
- **2022 (1401):** M.A. and N.N. were arrested in connection with nationwide protests that year.
- **November 2024:** Six defendants in the Ekbatan case received death sentences; HRANA reported the verdicts.
- **May 25, 2026:** According to Iran Human Rights, M.A., N.N., and two others were sentenced to death following an unfair trial by a judge known as "Hakim-i Edam."
- **Before November 30 (9 Azar) 2026:** M.A. and N.N. were moved to solitary cells at Kazel Hesar Prison pending execution.
- **November 19, 2026:** The UN General Assembly Third Committee adopted a resolution on Iran's human rights situation ([UN Iran resolution](../2026-11-19-bm-ucuncu-komite-iran-insan-haklari/)).
- **November 25, 2026:** The planned execution was halted at the last minute following pressure from families and human rights organizations.

**Who and what:** "Mofsed-i fi'l-arz" ("spreading corruption on earth") is a broad charge in Iran's penal code that can carry a death sentence and is typically used in political and security cases. The Ekbatan case refers to trials of defendants, including M.A. and N.N., arrested in connection with 2022 protests; the name derives from the Ekbatan neighborhood in Tehran.

**By the numbers:** According to Amnesty International's July 2025 report, M.A. and N.N. are among at least nine people at risk of execution linked to the "Woman, Life, Freedom" protests. According to HRANA, six total defendants received death sentences in the Ekbatan case; two of those sentences were quashed by a separate chamber of the High Court. M.A. and N.N. have been detained for over three years from arrest to this story's writing; UN Special Rapporteur Sato described this period as "consecutive legal proceedings."

**Next:** The outcome of M.A.'s lawyer's petition to the High Court's 39th Chamber for retrial was unknown as of this story's preparation; whether an official execution halt order was issued for N.N. has also not been confirmed.
