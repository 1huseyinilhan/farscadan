According to Iran International, citing a Telegram channel, the Union of Truckers and Drivers Syndicates of Iran announced on Tuesday (3 Azar) that truckers across 15 cities nationwide had stopped hauling cargo for the third consecutive day. The union said the action continued in protest of rising tire and spare parts prices.

According to Iran International, the All-Iran Union of Truckers and Drivers Syndicates posted on Telegram: "Today Iran stands in 15 cities. Drivers have no patience left; the wheels have stopped turning; we cannot make ends meet from our pockets." The report said that in Tehran, Shiraz, Isfahan, Qazvin, Bam, Sirjan, Dezful, Bandar Abbas, Gachsaran, Chabahar, Divandarreh, Zanjan, Ahvaz, Kermanshah, and Zabol, truckers maintained the "no-cargo" action on the third day. The union highlighted the dollar exchange rate, saying: "We buy tires in dollars, oil in dollars, parts in dollars—only our hauling fees are not in dollars." The union noted that one model truck tire cost 209 million tomans a week earlier but had risen to 246 million tomans. The union said hauling fees had remained at last year's level; the government's announced roughly 22 percent increase failed to solve drivers' problems. Perviz Turani-Kazvini, a board member of the Trucking Companies Association, told Cigar-e Sanat newspaper on Monday that hauling fees do not reflect real costs and trucking has ceased to be economically viable. By contrast, Iran International reported that deputy chairman of the Truckers' Guilds Union Council, Jalal Mousavi, denied the trucker strike to Daneşcu news agency, saying: "There is currently no news of a trucker strike; all drivers are hauling cargo; if something were happening, we would certainly know." The Roads and Transportation Organization Chief, Reza Ekberi, also denied a nationwide trucker strike, stating that road transport operations were "completely normal." In separate coverage, ISNA reported that the Truckers' Guild deputy chairman confirmed that 20 liters of engine oil on the open market had risen to 22 million tomans, calling for quotaed oil distribution to continue; this represents a separate price confirmation from an official source that aligns with the union's strike justification.

Iran International's report directly contradicts official statements; the strike's scope should be considered "unconfirmed." No separate confirmation of the strike was found in state-linked agencies (IRNA, ISNA) at the time this story was prepared.

## Context

**Background:**
- **2018 and 2019:** Iranian truckers staged consecutive nationwide strikes over hauling fees and spare parts prices.
- **November 13, 2026:** The dollar rate neared the 300,000-toman band again, pressured by restrictions at the Pakistan and Iraq borders ([details](../2026-11-13-dolar-tumen-300-bin-sinir-ablukasi/)).
- **November 23 (2 Azar), 2026:** Turani-Kazvini, an association member, publicly stated that hauling fees do not reflect real costs.
- **November 23-25 (23-25 Azar), 2026:** The union announced a three-day "no cargo" action across 15 cities; official agencies denied the strike.

**Who and what:** The All-Iran Union of Truckers and Drivers Syndicates (Etehadiye-ye Teshakolha-ye Kamyondaran va Ranandegan-e Saraser-e Iran) is a non-government union representing trucking guilds nationwide. The Truckers' Guilds Union Council (Kanun-e Encomenha-ye Senfi-ye Kamyondaran) is a separate entity close to the Transportation Ministry, grouping officially registered professional organizations; the entity that denied the strike in this story. The Roads and Transportation Organization (Sazman-e Rahdari va Hamlonaql-e Cadei), under the Transportation Ministry, is the official body regulating road transportation. The "hauling index" (shakhes-e ton-kilometr) is an official annual government-set rate showing the base price for transport fees.

**By the numbers:** According to the union (Telegram channel, November 25, 2026), one model truck tire cost rose from 209 million tomans to 246 million tomans within a week. According to ISNA (November 25, 2026), 20 liters of engine oil on the open market reached 22 million tomans. The government's announced hauling increase this year was roughly 22 percent; in an earlier rate-adjustment round, the ton-kilometer index had been raised 22.6 percent, also drawing criticism from trucking circles.

**Next:** The union said it would continue the action until its demands are met; the government announced no new talks or rate-increase timeline. Whether the strike extended to day four and whether official agencies continued to deny it remained unclear as of this story's preparation.
