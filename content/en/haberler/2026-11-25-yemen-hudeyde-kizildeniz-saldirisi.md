According to IRNA, citing Yemeni sources, Saudi warplanes struck Yemen's western port city of Hodeidah and its international airport on Wednesday morning (November 25), as well as the Harf Sufyan district in northwestern Amran province—each location hit three times. Yemen's Armed Forces said that before the strikes, the coalition had called on civilians to evacuate the region.

According to IRNA, a spokesperson for Yemen's Armed Forces said that Saudi Arabia had conducted 72 airstrikes and missile attacks in the past 24 hours against civilian areas in Sanaa, Jawf, Taiz, Sa'dah, Hodeidah, Ibb, and Lahij provinces. The spokesperson had previously warned that if the port of Hodeidah were struck, all Saudi ports on the Red Sea coast would become targets for Yemen's Armed Forces. According to ISNA, the Houthi-linked al-Masirah network confirmed the airstrike on Hodeidah Wednesday morning. According to IRNA, Yemen's Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Emigrants condemned Britain's sanctions on some Yemeni officials, stating that London's "hostile" steps would not go unanswered and would not extricate Saudi Arabia from "the current crisis." According to ISNA, U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Saudi Foreign Minister Faisal bin Farhan discussed West Asia security, Yemen's attacks on Saudi Arabia, and developments in Sudan and Lebanon by phone. Opposition outlet Iran International, citing Saudi officials, reported that Houthis had also attacked a tower in Riyadh's financial district and that 11 people were wounded; however, this casualty figure could not be independently verified as of this story's preparation. According to Saudi Gazette articles from November 8 and 19, 2026, official Saudi statements on two earlier strikes in the same district had declared "no casualties"; for this reason, the wounded count from the current strike should be considered "unconfirmed." According to Saudi Gazette's November 19, 2026 article, that attack had damaged a tower in King Abdullah Financial District—the district's tallest building and headquarters of the Public Investment Fund—with nearby structures affected; another tower in the district was also struck on November 8, 2026, with no fatalities reported. Workers in the district have largely worked from home since September due to repeated Houthi attacks.

## Context

**Background:**
- **2015:** A Saudi-led coalition launched air campaigns against Houthi-controlled areas.
- **January 2024:** The U.S. and U.K. imposed sanctions on senior Houthi officials over Red Sea shipping attacks.
- **November 12, 2026:** The UN Security Council extended the sanctions regime against the Houthis through November 2027 and expanded it to include weapon components and chemical precursors.
- **November 8-19, 2026:** Houthis staged consecutive strikes on towers in Riyadh's financial district; Saudi officials announced no casualties in both strikes.
- **November 25, 2026:** Saudi jets struck Hodeidah port and airport, with Sanaa naming Saudi Red Sea ports as targets.

**Who and what:** "Ansarullah" (Houthis) is a Zaydi Shiite armed political organization controlling a wide area including Sanaa in northern Yemen since 2014; Iran describes this group as part of the "axis of resistance" but denies direct command. Al-Masirah is the Houthi-affiliated broadcast network. The Saudi-led coalition, formed in 2015 to support Yemen's internationally recognized government, includes the UAE, Bahrain, and other Arab states; it has conducted regular airstrikes against Houthi-controlled areas since then. King Abdullah Financial District (KAFD) is the business hub in Riyadh where major Saudi corporations and state investment institutions are headquartered.

**By the numbers:** According to Yemen's Armed Forces spokesperson (IRNA, November 25, 2026), 72 airstrikes and missile attacks were launched in the past 24 hours. The UN Security Council extended the sanctions regime against the Houthis through November 2027 (Gulf News, November 12, 2026).

**Next:** Yemen's Armed Forces spokesperson reiterated that if strikes on Hodeidah continue, Jeddah port will also be targeted; no specific date was given for when this may occur.
