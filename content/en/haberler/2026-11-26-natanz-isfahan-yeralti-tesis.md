According to Iran International, the Washington-based Institute for Science and International Security (ISIS) published new satellite imagery showing rapid construction of a fortified underground complex in the Behjatabad region between the Natanz and Isfahan nuclear facilities. The institute reported that the complex's final use remains undetermined.

According to Iran International's report, the new complex is located approximately 62 kilometers from each of the Natanz and Isfahan nuclear facilities. Satellite imagery dated November 24, 2026 (Azar 3, 1405) shows clear progress in underground infrastructure and administrative building construction; the security perimeter is expected to cover an area of approximately 49 square kilometers. Construction has been ongoing since last year's Azar (November-December 2025) and has proceeded at "unusual speed" during the current year. According to the source, researchers say the complex may be designed for missile systems, other weapons types, or infrastructure related to the nuclear program; however, ISIS emphasizes that enhanced security measures strengthen the military use likelihood but notes that current evidence is insufficient to determine the final purpose. The exact location of the Behjatabad region and this designation currently rests solely on Iran International's report; independent corroboration has not been obtained.

Iran International connects these new images to ISIS's previous comprehensive assessment. According to the institute's report published on November 21, 2025, in the fifth month of the 12-day war, airstrikes in June 2025 caused "widespread and in some cases catastrophic" damage to facilities at Fordo, Natanz, and Isfahan; at that time, Iran had no meaningful capacity for uranium enrichment or manufacture of large numbers of centrifuges, and progress on reconstructing destroyed capacity was limited. However, ISIS cautioned that destruction of main enrichment centers did not mean total elimination of sensitive nuclear capability; satellite imagery from that period indicated cleaning, equipment recovery, and fortification activities at some facilities.

Two facilities stand out in the institute's same assessment. The Kuh-e Kolang Gazla ("Pickaxe Mountain") complex, located south of Natanz and not struck during the 12-day war, consists of a new section linked to the centrifuge program and tunnels built in 2007; Iran announced in 2020 its plan to establish a facility in this new section capable of mounting several thousand centrifuges annually. ISIS reported that the new section's construction was approaching final stages and suggested—as an unverified possibility—that old tunnels may have been used to store enriched uranium stocks. The Taleghan 2 facility in the Parchin military complex was destroyed by an Israeli strike on October 27, 2024; 2025 satellite imagery shows the facility has been substantially rebuilt and contains a cylindrical structure associated with high-explosive testing; this structure's use remains independently unverified.

According to ISIS's assessment, the location and status of approximately 440 kilograms of uranium at 60 percent enrichment currently in Iran's possession remains an unresolved question; the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) cannot fully verify these stocks due to restricted access to facilities. The institute also reported that entrances to underground tunnels at Isfahan have been fortified, with double-layered barriers installed to prevent cruise missiles from reaching entrances directly. ISIS emphasized that satellite imagery alone cannot reveal activities inside tunnels, the exact location of uranium stocks, or the status of nuclear equipment.

According to ISIS's report, at least three facilities within SPND (the Organization for Innovation and Research in Defense—the entity associated with nuclear weaponization) showed post-strike cleaning activities; these activities could be related to equipment recovery, reconstruction preparation, or erasure of previous activities. By contrast, major enrichment facilities at Fordo, Natanz, and Isfahan—which are subordinate to AEOI (the Atomic Energy Organization), which oversees the peaceful nuclear energy program—remain largely idle; ISIS noted that no repair or reconstruction work has been observed at these three centers.

ISIS's assessment also includes concrete details of main facility damage: the Pilot Fuel Enrichment Plant (PFEP) at Natanz was destroyed and remains unrepaired since the strike; the facility's electrical infrastructure and backup generators have been disabled. At Fordo, ventilation shaft holes opened by U.S. GBU-57 bombs remain filled with soil; the institute states that the facility cannot resume enrichment operations until the ventilation system is rebuilt.

## Context

**Background:**
- **October 27, 2024 (Aban 5, 1403):** Taleghan 2 facility in Parchin was destroyed by Israeli strike (ISIS report).
- **June 2025 (12-day war):** Israel and the U.S. struck Iran's nuclear facilities at Fordo, Natanz, and Isfahan; according to ISIS, damage was "widespread and in some cases catastrophic."
- **November 21, 2025 (Aban 30, 1404):** ISIS published its comprehensive assessment in the fifth month of the war; documented construction activities at Kuh-e Kolang Gazla and Taleghan 2.
- **November 24, 2026 (Azar 3, 1405):** New satellite imagery showed rapid underground construction in what was characterized as the Behjatabad region between Natanz and Isfahan (according to Iran International's report).
- This topic connects with [IAEA-Iran monitoring dispute](../2026-11-20-iaea-viyana-iran-denetim-anlasmazligi/); Farsçadan's [NPT and JCPOA guide](../2026-11-22-rehber-iran-nukleer-npt-koep/) provides background on nuclear program terminology.

**Who and what:** The Institute for Science and International Security (ISIS—not to be confused with the armed organization sharing the acronym), founded by physicist David Albright, is a Washington-based independent nuclear disarmament research organization. *Kuh-e Kolang Gazla* ("Pickaxe Mountain") is an underground tunnel complex south of Natanz reported to house a centrifuge assembly facility. Natanz is Iran's primary uranium enrichment facility; Isfahan is a uranium conversion and fuel production center. *AEOI* (Atomic Energy Organization) is Iran's civilian body overseeing the peaceful nuclear program; *SPND* (Organization for Innovation and Research in Defense) is directly subordinate to the top military command and is associated with weaponization research.

**By the numbers:**
- The new Behjatabad complex is approximately 62 kilometers from each Natanz and Isfahan site, with security perimeter covering approximately 49 square kilometers (Iran International, November 25, 2026).
- Iran's uranium stock of approximately 440 kilograms at 60 percent enrichment has undetermined location and status (ISIS, November 21, 2025 report).
- The planned Kuh-e Kolang Gazla facility was designed for mounting several thousand centrifuges annually (Iran's 2020 announcement, as reported in ISIS report).
