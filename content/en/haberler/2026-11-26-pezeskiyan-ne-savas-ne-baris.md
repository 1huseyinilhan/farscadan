During his speeches in Khorasan Razavi, undertaken as part of the government's 21st provincial tour, President Masoud Pezeshkian said talks with the U.S. are ongoing, but Iran "will not yield to bullying." According to Donya-ye Eqtesad, Pezeshkian also shared a recollection about how the war-or-peace decision was made and noted the difficulty of planning for the future in the country's current state of "neither war nor peace."

According to Donya-ye Eqtesad, Pezeshkian traveled to Khorasan Razavi on Tuesday on a two-day program; this was the 21st stop of the government's provincial tour program. During the tour, the president discussed his administration's steps to restore public confidence and answered a question about how the war-or-peace decision was made. Pezeshkian said: "Some claim that the late Supreme Leader said the matter of war and peace needed to be resolved by us. We were with him and asked what we should do; he told us to settle this matter."

According to Donya-ye Eqtesad's news site Fararu, Pezeshkian also shared a recollection from the 12-day war: "During the war, when we came under attack, we were there; because we were covered entirely in dust and dirt, we changed our clothes and after washing, went to him who was waiting for us." Pezeshkian related what he said at that moment: "I told him that if they had killed us, we would have been relieved and wouldn't have heard so much criticism." After this account, the president said "these imbalances cannot be resolved so simply," saying "war was imposed on us and some accept this situation and some don't."

On talks with the U.S., Pezeshkian said: "We are talking with the U.S.; but we are preserving our own honor and strength and we will not yield to bullying." Emphasizing that talks are not being conducted from a position of weakness, the president stated, "We drafted the 14-point memorandum of understanding with honor; find one point where we have backed down." The memorandum's contents were not detailed in the article and **remain unverified** as an independent matter.

Pezeshkian also addressed global price increases, saying "I accept prices; our roads were limited and goods became expensive worldwide." In a separate meeting with provincial assembly members, he stressed that resources should be directed to low-income groups and deprived areas, saying the "zero inflation" plan should not be limited only to Tehran, and that the government was seeking a mechanism to support the poor and vulnerable across deprived areas.

Responding to criticism about what the government has done to restore public confidence, Pezeshkian said that the public's presence in the field under current conditions shows the importance of this solidarity: "If the government had not acted correctly, what would have happened and how would people be in the streets?" Emphasizing that trust, unity, and cohesion are principles for him, the president said, "The enemy's entire plan and design aim to create discord in the country; they want us to quarrel with each other."

While touring Khorasan Razavi, Pezeshkian also posted on social media on the International Day Against Violence Against Women: "The Prophet stood against discrimination and restored women's honor. The rise of women is a source of pride for the Islamic Republic of Iran, and today all laws and practices must ensure the rights, security, and development of both women and men."

During a speech at Ferdowsi University in Mashhad, addressing previously circulated images of him turning off a light in his office, Pezeshkian said, "I didn't turn off a light; I simply didn't turn on extra lights," emphasizing that officials should be frugal in consumption. According to Donya-ye Eqtesad, the tour also drew some scrutiny: a group gathered in front of Khorasan Razavi's governorate office to protest the government's foreign policy performance; at Ferdowsi University, a student objected to restricted speaking time, and after his objection, Pezeshkian briefly left the hall and returned shortly after mediation by the governor and officials. The article provided no details or participant numbers for these events; therefore, the scope of the protest **remains unverified**. Because only Donya-ye Eqtesad carried the speech, a "How the Persian press covered it" comparison was not conducted for this article. On the tour's second day, Pezeshkian also visited the tomb of poet Ferdowsi, the graves of musician Mohammad Reza Shajarian, and poet Mehdi Akhavan-Sales.

## Context

**Background:**
- **March 2026:** Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei died during the first week of the 12-day war; the Expert Assembly, using this authority for the first time in constitutional history, elected a new Supreme Leader that month (see [Who decides Iran's Supreme Leader](../2026-11-20-rehber-iran-kim-karar-verir/)). The new Supreme Leader's identity is not named in this report. Pezeshkian's mention of "the late Supreme Leader" refers to Ali Khamenei.
- **June-July 2026:** The region witnessed a "40-day war" in which Hayber Shikin, Emad, and Qadir missiles were used, according to Hamshahri's report (Hamshahri, November 10, 2026).
- **October 4, 2026:** Pezeshkian explained that his administration had restructured the economy in line with the "enemy's economic warfare" (see [related report](../2026-10-04-pezeskiyan-savas-ekonomisi/)).
- **November 9, 2026:** According to Saudi Gazette, Pezeshkian said Iran is open to dialogue to end the current "no war, no peace" state but wants negotiation based on mutual respect.

**Who and what:** Masoud Pezeshkian is a cardiac surgeon-turned-politician who was elected president in 2024 with support from a reformist-centrist coalition; this early election followed the death of his predecessor Ibrahim Raisi in a helicopter crash. With Azerbaijani paternal ancestry and Kurdish maternal ancestry, Pezeshkian worked as a cardiovascular surgeon at Tabriz University before taking office and served as a Tabriz member of parliament. The Farsi phrase "نه جنگ و نه صلح" (na jang-o na solh) translates to "no war, no peace," describing an interim state where active combat has halted but no formal peace exists.

**By the numbers:**
- As reported by Farsçadan on November 13, 2026, the dollar rate on the free market approached the 300,000-toman threshold.
- According to Hamshahri's November 10, 2026 report, the region experienced a "40-day war" in June-July 2026 and a "12-day war" in March 2026; Pezeshkian's recollection today pertains to the latter conflict period.
