According to Iran International, the dollar exchange rate on the free market Thursday, November 27, exceeded 295,000 tomans; messages received by the channel showed that households are struggling to meet basic needs and some shopkeepers reported closing their businesses and laying off employees. The report noted that just one day earlier, Central Bank Governor Abdolnasser Hemmati claimed the market had been in "relative stability" over the previous two months even as the dollar surged past 290,000 tomans. The currency slide coincides with the Central Bank of Iran's official consumer inflation data for Mehr (September 23–October 22): year-over-year point-to-point inflation at 88.7% and producer inflation at 120.9%.

One message Iran International received described how car repair costs had risen so steeply that a retiree hadn't used his car in months and had been riding a motorcycle in the cold; now the motorcycle had broken down and repairs would cost half his monthly pension. A retired teacher from Horasan wrote that his television had broken, he could not afford repairs or a replacement, and television was his household's only entertainment—he would need at least six months' salary for the replacement. Iran International's survey from October 30–November 3 showed that 70.9% of respondents had reduced food spending, cut basic necessities from their household budgets, or delayed medical treatment; a respondent from Tabriz reported a two-person household's monthly expenses at approximately 80 million tomans.

A student from Lahijan wrote that he was interested in electronics but couldn't afford anything because of prices; an Arduino Nano board had reached 1.3 million tomans. A clothing retailer in Karaj who closed his shop said, "With the dollar at 295,000 tomans, I have nothing left to lose—it's over"; a resident of Qeshm reported that due to sluggish sales he had closed his business, and all shopkeepers around him had either closed or abandoned theirs.

## Context

**Background:**
- **February 2026:** The U.S.-Israel-Iran conflict began; since then, the rial has accelerated its depreciation.
- **November 13, 2026:** The dollar had approached the 300,000-toman band under pressure from restrictions at the Pakistan and Iraq borders ([details](../2026-11-13-dolar-tumen-300-bin-sinir-ablukasi/)).
- **November 26, 2026:** Central Bank Governor Hemmati claimed the market was "relatively stable" even as the dollar surpassed 290,000 tomans.
- **November 27, 2026 (today):** The dollar surpassed 295,000 tomans; on the same day, the truckers' union cited the dollar rate as justification for tire and spare parts price increases ([details](../2026-11-27-kamyoncu-grevi-besinci-gun-20-sehir/)).

**Key terms:** The "toman" (tuman) is an informal unit of account used in everyday speech, equal to 10 rials; Iran's official currency is the rial. Prices in news reports and markets are typically quoted in tomans. "Point-to-point inflation" (tavrom-e nokta'i) measures this month's price level against the same month the previous year, providing a more current gauge than the average annual inflation rate commonly cited in Iran.

**By the numbers:** The Central Bank of Iran reports that point-to-point inflation in the consumer price index reached 88.7% in Mehr (October 2026); this figure is confirmed by independent forex tracking sites, which show the free-market dollar at approximately 272,000 tomans in mid-October (IntelliNews, November 2026 update)—indicating the rate has lost an additional approximately 8–9% in recent weeks. The same day, Khabaronline reported the official closing rate at 292,890 tomans, slightly below Iran International's reported daily peak of 295,000 tomans; this difference may reflect the two sources taking snapshots at different times and was not independently verified at the time this article was prepared.

**What's ahead:** No new Central Bank announcement on currency stabilization was released at the time this article was prepared. The Central Bank is expected to release November (Aban) inflation data in coming weeks.
