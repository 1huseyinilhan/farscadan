According to Ettelaat, citing Reuters and Italian media (La Repubblica, ANSA), two technicians working for the Italian unit of German arms maker Rheinmetall were killed on Thursday, November 27, in a drone attack launched by Iran against Kuwait. The report states that Rheinmetall manufactures the Skyguard air defense system, which was deployed in Kuwait, and the technicians were servicing the system's anti-drone equipment; Iran targeted this air defense system with a drone.

According to Iran International, the explosion occurred as two Rheinmetall technicians were performing maintenance on the radar antenna of the Skyguard air defense system used by the Kuwaiti military; a Kuwaiti and a Pakistani national were also wounded in the attack. Kuwait's Defense Ministry spokesperson Suaud Abdul Aziz al-Atwan called the attack a "criminal invasion" and said the target was one of the country's "vital centers," but offered no details; he stated that the wounded had been hospitalized and the attack caused material damage.

According to Khabaronline, Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi conveyed his condolences to Kuwaiti and Italian counterparts in separate phone calls and described the incident as "part of a pre-planned scenario to escalate the conflict"; he stated Iran was ready for a joint investigation with Kuwait and Italy to clarify the event. According to Donya-ye Eqtesad, Iran's embassy in Rome also issued a statement calling accusations of Iranian involvement "utterly baseless" and warning against attempts to "widen the conflict and draw Europe into the Persian Gulf crisis." Before these phone calls, Armed Forces Spokesman Abulfazl Shakerchi had also denied any attack on Kuwait, characterizing the accusations as part of "false flag operations."

According to Hamshahri, an unnamed security source alleged that Ukraine had deployed thousands of drones and micro-aircraft in an operational network across the region, including Iraqi Kurdistan; international relations commentator Muhammad Marandi suggested the attack was a "false flag" and that the Ukrainian regime wanted to demonstrate its capacity to Trump. These claims show that even among Persian-language sources, there is no clarity on who conducted the attack; Kuwait, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, and Yemen condemned the attack and attributed it directly to Iran.

According to Reuters reports (Ynetnews, IBTimes), Italy's Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani stated in the Senate that several Iranian-made drones targeted Kuwaiti military facilities, including the air defense detection station where the two technicians were working, and that Italian officials had evidence the drones were of Iranian manufacture. According to these same reports, this was the first deadly attack on Kuwait during months of conflict.

**Key terms:** Rheinmetall, headquartered in Düsseldorf, manufactures armored vehicles, ammunition, and air defense/counter-drone systems; its Italian unit, based in Rome, produces short-range air defense systems. The "Skyguard" is a Rheinmetall-manufactured, radar-based drone and air defense system; Kuwait has used this system to protect critical infrastructure since 2013.

**By the numbers:** According to Reuters reports (Ynetnews, IBTimes, November 27, 2026), two Italian citizens were killed and a Kuwaiti and Pakistani national were wounded in the attack; this is the only casualty figure independently verified in international media. Persian-language media did not report a different casualty figure; however, on the question of "who conducted" the attack, there is a direct discrepancy between Iranian official spokespersons and Kuwaiti, Italian, and international media—one that was not resolved by independent investigation at the time this article was prepared.

**Turkey dimension:** No sources reviewed reported a direct connection between this attack and Turkey (Turkish nationals affected, formal Turkish reaction) at the time this article was prepared; however, the incident can be read as one link in the chain of maritime and air security tensions in the Persian Gulf in which Turkey also participates.

**What's ahead:** At the time this article was prepared, the timeline for the joint Kuwait-Italy-Iran investigation Araghchi announced had not been announced. Whether Italy and Kuwait governments would retaliate in kind remains unclear.
