The IRGC Navy's press office announced that two tankers caught fire east of the Strait of Hormuz, near Khasab (Bandar Khassab), with one striking a naval mine. According to Hamshahri, this was the second such incident in four days, following the IRGC's announcement four days earlier of three tankers hitting mines in the same area.

According to Hamshahri's account, the IRGC Navy's press office stated that one tanker struck a naval mine off Khasab hours earlier, while the second ignited and caught fire in an "unauthorized route" east of the Strait of Hormuz. The statement indicated that these tankers, like those that exploded previously, had disabled their position-tracking systems and were attempting to covertly transit the strait using false routing. According to Hamshahri's report, the IRGC reiterated that both tankers caught fire near the southern coast and near Khasab, cautioned that the designated route was equipped with naval mines, and warned that U.S. military air support would not prevent tankers from hitting mines.

IRNA and ISNA, citing the same statement, reported the incident largely with the same details and without additional commentary. Donya-ye Eghtesad led its report with the phrase "violent explosion" and framed the incident around "two rule-violating tankers."

The IRGC Navy's statement alleged that the tankers had disabled their automatic identification systems (AIS) while transiting the strait and were following an "unauthorized" course outside official shipping lanes. According to the statement, this pattern had been observed in previously damaged tankers as well; the navy therefore describes the route as "unauthorized" and reiterates that it is equipped with naval mines. According to Hamshahri's report, the IRGC specifically emphasized that U.S. military air support in the region could not protect tankers from hitting mines—an assertion that echoed a warning in the navy's initial statement from November 24.

According to Khabar Online's report, Supreme Leader adviser Mohammad Mohber commented the same day on the Strait of Hormuz and blockade allegations, saying "neither will the Strait of Hormuz open nor will the blockade continue."
