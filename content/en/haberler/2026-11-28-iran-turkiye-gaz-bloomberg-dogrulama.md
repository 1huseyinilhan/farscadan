According to reports citing Bloomberg, Turkey's state pipeline company BOTAŞ and Iran's National Gas Company (NIGC) extended their gas export contract, which expired in July, to 2032. According to Donya-ye Eghtesad, this represents a second corroboration—from a different channel and via Bloomberg—of a claim Farsçadan had reported as "unverified" the previous day.

According to Donya-ye Eghtesad, citing "informed sources," Bloomberg reported that neither company has yet made a formal announcement and that it remains unclear whether the annual import volume or pricing formula from the previous contract (approximately 10 billion cubic meters annually) has changed. According to the report, some Turkish media outlets, citing industry sources, provided more detail: these sources indicate that the new contract contains no changes to annual volume or pricing formula. Donya-ye Eghtesad emphasized the difference between these two accounts, noting that what can currently be stated with certainty is only that the arrangement has been extended, while specifics remain uncertain.

According to Khabar Online's report, the previous contract was signed in 2001, was valid for 25 years, and expired in July of this year; however, gas flow did not stop after the contract's expiration because Turkey continued to receive volumes of gas that it had previously paid for but not yet received as make-up shipments. Khabar Online also cited an assessment from Siamak Edibi, supply analysis manager at energy consultancy FGE NexantECA, noting that the continuation of this commercial relationship is important for Tehran because large-scale bilateral trade creates opportunity for settlement of accounts if problems arise in international transactions.

According to Donya-ye Eghtesad, one source of ambiguity concerns the extension period itself: counting five years forward from July 2026 arrives at July 2031, yet Bloomberg refers to 2032. BOTAŞ's import license is also valid through April 18, 2032—which aligns with the April 18, 2032 date reported by Ettelaat yesterday. Donya-ye Eghtesad suggested that the license date and contract period may differ and that without publication of the official text, the reason for this discrepancy cannot be determined. At the time Farsçadan prepared this report, English sources (Reuters, Bloomberg's own website) yielded no text directly confirming the 2032 date; for this reason, the claim remains classified as unverified at the official level.

According to Donya-ye Eghtesad's analysis, some former BOTAŞ executives have stated that the previous contract contained a mechanism allowing it to auto-renew for another five years unless one party formally notified termination; if such a provision exists, the current "extension" report may stem not from Turkey's new, active cooperation decision but rather from both parties' failure to invoke their right to terminate. Donya-ye Eghtesad emphasized that this possibility also remains speculative absent official text and party confirmation.

## Context

**Background:**
- **2001:** A 25-year natural gas purchase agreement was signed between Iran and Turkey; the annual ceiling was approximately 10 billion cubic meters.
- **July 2026:** The 25-year contract period expired; Donya-ye Eghtesad reports that flow did not cease because previously paid-for but undelivered volumes continued to be sent as make-up shipments.
- **November 27, 2026:** Ettelaat reported, citing Anka, that the contract was extended to April 18, 2032; this claim was not independently verified that day (see [Iranian press: 2032 claim unverified](../2026-11-27-iran-turkiye-gaz-sozlesmesi-2032/)).
- **November 28, 2026 (today):** Bloomberg-citing reports reaffirmed the 2032 date through a different channel; however, BOTAŞ and NIGC issued no official statement.

**Key players / concepts:** BOTAŞ (Boru Hatları ile Petrol Taşıma A.Ş.) is Turkey's state company responsible for natural gas imports and pipeline operations. NIGC (National Iranian Gas Company) is Iran's state gas exporter. Gas is transported via the Tabriz-Ankara pipeline.

**By the numbers:** According to Donya-ye Eghtesad, Iran exported approximately 8 billion cubic meters of gas to Turkey in 2025, representing about 13 percent of Turkey's total imports of 58 billion cubic meters (Donya-ye Eghtesad, November 28, 2026). The Express Tribune's report, citing Reuters, indicated Turkey imported 7.6 billion cubic meters from Iran the previous year, also approximately 13 percent of total imports (The Express Tribune, May 24, 2026)—the two sources' percentages align. According to Donya-ye Eghtesad, Russia holds the top position in Turkey's gas import mix at 36 percent; Iran ranks fourth in this hierarchy.

**Turkey dimension:** The story directly affects Turkey's energy security. According to Donya-ye Eghtesad, Turkey's Energy Minister stated that gas planned to flow from Turkmenistan through Iran to Turkey had stopped due to U.S. pressure; this demonstrates that transit routes through Iran also face geopolitical vulnerabilities. The other dimensions of Turkey-Iran energy relations were covered in [Farsçadan's guide](../2026-11-24-rehber-turkiye-iran-iliskileri/).

**What to watch:** According to Donya-ye Eghtesad, questions to monitor in coming months include whether Iran can sustain export commitments during the winter months (Dey-Bahman), whether the contract's official text will be published, and through which channels and to what extent export revenue will reach Iran. It should be noted that neither BOTAŞ nor Turkey's Energy Ministry issued a statement confirming the 2032 claim at the time this article was prepared.
