Saudi Arabia, Pakistan, and Turkey reaffirmed their commitment to lowering tension in the region and to collective defense at a new meeting of the Political-Defense Strategic Committee of the Mecca Accord. According to ISNA's report, the three countries emphasized their commitments to defense cooperation and de-escalation in a joint statement.

According to Donya-ye Eghtesad, citing Turkey's Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan, Fidan disclosed that Turkey, alongside Pakistan and Saudi Arabia, is evaluating a roadmap for bringing new members into the Mecca Accord. Fidan stated that the process of strengthening the alliance's institutional capacity continues with the aim of contributing to regional security, and that member states have agreed on special principles for new member accession. According to Donya-ye Eghtesad, the strategic committee also issued a statement reaffirming the commitment to accelerating efforts supporting Saudi Arabia's security; the committee condemned alleged attacks by the Houthi movement (Ansarullah) in Yemen against Saudi Arabia and emphasized the three countries' dedication to defense industry cooperation, technology development, and capacity building. According to the statement, the parties decided to continue political and military consultations to support de-escalation efforts and to call for resolution of conflicts through dialogue and diplomacy. The committee also welcomed progress on the establishment of a secretariat for the alliance to be based in Saudi Arabia.

According to IRNA's account, Iran-U.S. negotiation prospects were also discussed at this meeting; IRNA attributed this to Turkey's Foreign Ministry statement. While IRNA's headline refers to this meeting as "the third meeting of the strategic committee of the tripartite defense pact members," ISNA's report called it "the second meeting"; this numerical difference between the two state agencies was not verified at the time of writing.

In a separate Donya-ye Eghtesad report, Muhammed Ali al-Houthi, a member of Yemen's Supreme Political Council, responded critically to the Mecca Alliance's statement. Al-Houthi stated that "no country can justify attacks on Yemen and the blockade of Yemen under the pretext of defending Saudi Arabia." According to Donya-ye Eghtesad's report, al-Houthi asserted that respect for countries' sovereignty requires respect for other countries' sovereignty, and emphasized that "the claim of defending Saudi Arabia can in no way justify the Saudi coalition attacks that have continued since 2015." In al-Houthi's words, "our hands are extended for honorable peace, but we will never surrender; the continuation of blockade and massacre will not go unanswered."
