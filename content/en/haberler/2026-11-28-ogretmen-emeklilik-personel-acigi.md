Iran's Ministry of Education spokesperson Ali Ferhadi announced that the majority of teachers who became eligible for retirement this year chose to continue working. According to Kayhan's report citing Mehr news agency, Ferhadi said this was the first time the ministry's records had documented such a development.

According to Kayhan's account, Ferhadi stated that over 26,000 teachers retired this year, but that 33,900 people meeting retirement qualifications chose to continue their service. According to the spokesperson, this was the first year in which the number of those choosing to remain in service exceeded those who retired. Ferhadi said the Ministry of Education thanked those teachers who chose to continue for their contribution to filling the ministry's human resources gap.

Ferhadi further emphasized that school closures and restrictions in schools are determined "according to the needs of the times and current conditions," but that the ministry's priority is ensuring schools are never closed and that in-person education continues. According to Kayhan's report, the spokesperson stated that guidelines exist for school administrators regarding situations requiring restrictions, and that the budget is planned so as not to disrupt students' educational progress.

According to Kayhan's report, Ferhadi also responded to a question about the enrollment of foreign students in schools. The spokesperson noted that, through a "religious and humanitarian" approach, every foreign child applying for enrollment—regardless of legal or undocumented status—is accepted. Ferhadi stated that more than 350,000 foreign students have been enrolled in Iranian schools to date and that work continues to ensure none are deprived of education.

Kayhan's report does not contain details from the ministry spokesperson regarding the individual motivations of teachers choosing to remain in service (such as salary, retirement pension levels, or job security); instead, the statement framed the decision directly within the context of filling staff shortages.

The phrase "first time" in Ferhadi's statement refers to the ministry's own records: in previous years, while some personnel eligible for retirement chose to end their service, this year the number choosing to remain exceeded those who retired. According to Kayhan's report, the spokesperson directly addressed teachers making this choice, thanking them for "their contribution to meeting personnel needs"; this language reflects the ministry's effort to frame the situation not as a crisis but as voluntary support. In the same interview, Ferhadi reiterated that decisions on school closures and restrictions would be made "according to the needs of the times and current conditions," but emphasized that continuity of in-person education is the priority, adding that the budget is planned to avoid disrupting students' educational process.

Kayhan's report shows that the spokesperson's statement covered three distinct topics in succession within a single interview: retirement and personnel status, school closure management, and the enrollment of foreign students.
