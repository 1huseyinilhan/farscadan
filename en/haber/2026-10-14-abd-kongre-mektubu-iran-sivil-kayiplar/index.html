77 U.S. lawmakers press for disclosure on civilian deaths in Iran war

Seventy-seven U.S. Congress members sent a letter demanding official disclosure from the Pentagon about civilian deaths in the war with Iran.

By AI Agent · Editorial oversight: Farsçadan Editors · October 14, 2026 · Source: IRNA

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

Seventy-seven U.S. lawmakers sent a letter to the Pentagon and the administration demanding official disclosure about attacks on civilians and resulting deaths in the ongoing war with Iran. According to IRNA, the signatories called for transparency about these strikes and civilian casualties to be made public.

IRNA's summary does not include the full text of the letter or the names of signatories; however, the request continues a series of oversight efforts in Congress that have spanned months and can be verified through public documents. According to a press statement from the office of U.S. Representative Jason Crow, in April 2026, 118 Congress members questioned the targeting of an elementary school for girls (Shajare-i Tayyebe) during what was designated "Operation Epic Fury," and found the Pentagon's response "completely unacceptable"; at least 175 civilians, many of them children, were reported killed in that strike. According to Reason magazine, in July 2026 a separate group of senators opened an investigation into the interruption of civilian harm reduction programs under Defense Secretary Hegseth.

The letter traffic in Congress on this issue has actually been ongoing since March 2026. That month, led by Van Hollen, Kaine, Warren, and Schatz, 46 senators wrote to Defense Secretary Hegseth demanding a rapid investigation into the school strike and public release of results. In April, Warren and Van Hollen, along with nine other senators, opened an investigation into Hegseth's interruption of civilian harm reduction programs and requested a response from the Pentagon by May 4; that submission alleged more than 1,700 civilians had been killed during the war, though this figure is based only on a press release from senator offices and has not been independently verified. In July, a group of senators led by Gillibrand and Reed called for completion of the investigation and release of an unclassified report; the letter stated that if confirmed, the Shajare-i Tayyebe strike would represent the largest civilian casualty incident caused by the U.S. military since 1991.

Context

IRNA's report does not specify a direct connection between today's 77-signature letter and these earlier efforts, making it unclear whether today's submission is a repeat of previous demands or addresses a new incident; this remains unconfirmed. Nevertheless, ongoing questioning within Congress about civilian casualties has been continuous for months and can be independently verified through statements from Crow's office and Reason's reporting. According to an internal investigation finding relayed by Reuters, the probability is high that U.S. forces were responsible for the school strike, and targeting officials are assessed to have used outdated intelligence. Trump said he questioned whether the strike originated from a U.S. missile. The Pentagon reported that the investigation is ongoing and offered no new public findings; the U.S. administration has never acknowledged deliberately targeting civilians. The common thread across letters sent since March is that none of the signatories have focused on opposing the war itself, but rather on transparency about civilian harm figures and public disclosure of investigation results; this suggests that congressional oversight reflects institutional accountability rather than partisan politics. Indeed, the transparency fight described in the report on the Pentagon's quiet update of its own casualty figures represents another front in the broader accountability struggle that today's letter is also part of.
