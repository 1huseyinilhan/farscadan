Huthis strike Aramco in Dhahran: headquarters evacuated

Saudi Arabia's Energy Ministry confirmed a missile strike on Aramco's Dhahran administrative building; four people wounded in Riyadh.

By AI Agent · Editorial oversight: Farsçadan Editors · October 14, 2026 · Source: IRNA

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

Saudi Arabia's Energy Ministry confirmed that the headquarters building of state oil company Aramco in the Eastern Province city of Dhahran was targeted on Tuesday evening; the ministry reported no loss of life. According to IRNA, the strike was part of a series of missile attacks launched by armed forces in Yemen against Riyadh, Dhahran, and Dammam.

According to Iran International, citing Reuters reporting, a ballistic missile striking Aramco's central complex in Dhahran caused fires and damage in the upper three floors of one administrative tower. ISNA and Hamshahri reported that Aramco's main building was evacuated as a precaution and that a chain of explosions occurred in three cities. Saudi Civil Defense announced separately that two cruise missiles shot down by air defense systems over Riyadh resulted in debris falling and injuries—two women, one man, and one child—and authorities stated the missiles were launched by Yemeni forces.

According to Donya-ye Eqtesad, after the strike, flight traffic at King Fahd International Airport in Dammam was suspended again. U.S. President Trump alleged that the Houthis had attacked Saudi Arabia "deliberately and likely at Iran's behest" to drive up oil prices ahead of elections; this claim is attributed directly to Trump and has not been independently verified.

Context

This strike marks a shift from earlier attacks: previous stories about Riyadh airport's third strike and Ansarallah's airport strike targeted civilian aviation; today's attack struck directly at Aramco's administrative headquarters. The Houthi movement in Yemen (Ansarallah) is characterized as aligned with Iran in the long-running conflict against the Saudi-led coalition; however, Iran's direct operational role in this particular strike has not been confirmed by independent sources.

Dhahran is where Aramco's corporate headquarters is located and forms the administrative backbone of oil exports; a direct hit there carries a heavier symbolic weight than airport strikes. Saudi Arabia's missile defense systems have reported they have largely blocked threats to Riyadh and the Eastern Province since the conflict's beginning; however, debris casualties and direct hits like today's indicate gaps remain in the defensive line. Because independent intelligence assessments of the size and range of the Houthi missile and drone inventory have not been shared with the public, whether the frequency and accuracy of strikes are increasing over time cannot be independently confirmed.
