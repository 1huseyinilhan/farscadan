Iran's top diplomat: We are seriously considering withdrawing from the NPT

A senior Iranian diplomat said Tehran is evaluating withdrawal from the nuclear nonproliferation treaty as a serious option.

By AI Agent · Editorial oversight: Farsçadan Editors · October 16, 2026 · Source: Hamshahri

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

A senior Iranian diplomat said Tehran is seriously evaluating withdrawal from the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons (NPT). According to Hamshahri, the diplomat, speaking to Lebanese news agency Yuniuz, said internal debates on nuclear doctrine are ongoing and emphasized that the Lebanon file must be an integral part of any permanent peace settlement.

According to Hamshahri's report, the diplomat stated that Supreme Leader Ayatollah Seyyed Mojtaba Khamenei has not issued a new religious ruling on nuclear weapons, but internal discussions continue about revising the country's nuclear doctrine. The diplomat also said that if Israel renews strikes on Lebanon, Iran will provide a firm response, and there is no guarantee that war will not escalate.

In the same interview, the diplomat said Iran's mediation efforts between Saudi Arabia and Yemen continue. ISNA's account of the same statement included similar language; Donya-ye Eghtesad published the news as a brief summary quoting Tasnim, emphasizing "Iran is evaluating NPT withdrawal."

Iran has mentioned the option of leaving the NPT several times in the post-war period, but today's statement is notable as a senior diplomat's direct and explicit reference to a foreign news outlet. The diplomat's remarks are significant for showing how directly Iran's process of redefining its nuclear doctrine under military and diplomatic pressure is reflected in official rhetoric.

Context

The NPT, signed in 1968 and ratified by Iran, is an international treaty aimed at preventing nuclear weapons proliferation; withdrawal by Tehran could mean the end of international inspection mechanisms over Iran's nuclear program. The diplomat's remarks come at the same time as Foreign Minister Zarif's earlier statement that Iran has not enriched uranium sufficient for nuclear weapons, and can be read as part of Iran's process of reassessing its nuclear doctrine following seven months of war. The emphasis on the Lebanon file reflects Iran's concern over renewed Israeli strikes against its regional ally Hezbollah; negotiations on Hezbollah's disarmament remain inconclusive. The IAEA's headquarters—the Vienna International Centre (UNO City), which oversees NPT implementation—would be directly affected by any Iranian withdrawal decision; the agency's inspection authority over Iran depends on NPT membership. The diplomat's identity and exact title were not disclosed in the report; therefore remains unconfirmed whether this statement reflects a decision in Iran's official foreign policy or individual assessment.
