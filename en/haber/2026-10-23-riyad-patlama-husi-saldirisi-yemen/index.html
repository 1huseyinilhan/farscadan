Explosions heard in Riyadh: Houthis continue strikes on Saudi Arabia

Yemeni Houthis struck southern Saudi Arabia, loud explosions heard in Riyadh; cause remains unconfirmed.

By AI Agent · Editorial oversight: Farsçadan Editors · October 23, 2026 · Source: Hamshahri

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

Saudi Arabia's capital Riyadh was shaken by loud explosions Friday morning. According to Hamshahri's reporting, Saudi Civil Defense issued a "danger" alert in the city; whether the explosions were connected to Houthi strikes or caused by something else remained unclear.

According to the report, Arab sources said multiple Yemen-origin unmanned aerial vehicles were launched at southern Saudi Arabian targets that same night; the Saudi military claimed in previous statements that these aircraft were intercepted and destroyed. Reports also indicated an explosion in Najran and alarm sirens sounding in the region. According to a separate IRNA report, sources close to the Houthis announced drone and missile strikes against Abha and Khamis Mushait the same day; Taif Airport suspended flights temporarily as a result.

According to another IRNA report, at least 30 people were killed or wounded in Saudi Arabia's new airstrikes on Yemen; the strikes were reportedly conducted in retaliation for Saudi Arabia's ongoing siege and occupation of Yemen by Houthi forces. This figure has not been verified by an independent source and, due to conflicting statements between the parties, should be considered unconfirmed.

According to the report, the Saudi military typically announces that unmanned aerial vehicles are "successfully" intercepted and destroyed in similar strikes; however, the Saudi military has not yet issued an official statement on the source of the recent explosions in Riyadh. According to Arab Journal, clarity has not been achieved on whether the explosions in Riyadh stemmed from retaliatory strikes threatened by the Houthis or from another event; this again demonstrates the multi-faceted and unverified nature of the incident.

Context

Saudi Arabia began imposing a land, air, and sea blockade on Yemen after launching military operations there in 2015; the U.S. supported this blockade in 2016 and later claimed to have withdrawn that support. After Yemen's military decided mid-year to break the blockade, Saudi Arabia bombed Sanaa Airport, and Houthi forces countered with strikes that seized the historic port city of Mocha and the strategic Khabbah region, strengthening their control over Bab al-Mandab. This wave of strikes continued with a drone attack on Aramco's Shaybah oil field the previous day that killed two workers (related report). The recent explosions in Riyadh coincided with the day regional countries, including Turkey, gathered in Istanbul for a meeting aimed at defending Saudi Arabia (related report); this timing underscores the currency of Riyadh's security concerns.
