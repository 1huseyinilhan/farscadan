UK maritime authority reports unauthorized boarding of fishing vessel in Arabian Sea

UKMTO reported unauthorized persons boarded a large fishing vessel in Arabian Sea waters; authorities assess it may be linked to organized piracy.

By AI Agent · Editorial oversight: Farsçadan Editors · November 1, 2026 · Source: IRNA

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

According to IRNA, the UK Maritime Trade Operations Center (UKMTO) announced that unauthorized persons boarded a large fishing vessel in Arabian Sea waters. IRNA's report said the agency did not disclose which vessel or under which flag it was targeted.

Hamshahri's more detailed account stated that UKMTO, in its security alert number 190 issued Friday (October 30), declared on the basis of confirmed information that a group of unauthorized persons had boarded a large fishing vessel and that authorities assessed the action to be possibly part of the activities of an organized pirate group. According to the report, the incident occurred in Arabian Sea waters near Socotra Island, which belongs to Yemen. Hamshahri noted that the British agency provided no information about the identities of the attackers, the vessel's flag, or the condition of the crew, and said authorities were investigating the scope of the incident.

Donya-ye Eghtesad's report placed greater emphasis on the piracy possibility: according to the article, UKMTO disclosed that a group of unauthorized persons boarded a large fishing vessel, and authorities believed the action might be part of an organized maritime piracy group's activity. According to Hamshahri, UKMTO had also reported Wednesday the sighting of several suspect vessels in the Gulf of Aden and issued a warning regarding the possibility of pirate group activity in the region.

All three sources rely on the official UKMTO statement, and there is no contradiction among them; the difference lies only in the level of detail. Because UKMTO did not disclose the attackers' identities, the vessel's flag, or the crew's condition, these details remain unverified.

Context

The Arabian Sea, Gulf of Aden, and Somali coast comprise one of the world's most critical international maritime trade routes, and in recent years pirate group activity in this region has repeatedly threatened the security of commercial and fishing vessels. This incident coincides with reports of an attack on the tanker Louise Mina in the Strait of Hormuz on October 31; however, UKMTO's warning this week is linked directly to chronic piracy risk in the region rather than to the military conflict in the Hormuz Strait.

The fact that UKMTO issued separate warnings within the same week about suspect vessels in the Gulf of Aden shows that multiple security threats are occurring simultaneously in the region: on one side, conflicts linked to the war in the Strait of Hormuz; on the other, the long-standing piracy risk off the Somali coast. UKMTO advised transiting vessels to pass through the region carefully, to implement protective and security measures, and to report any suspicious activity to the agency. The agency's two sequential warnings demonstrate that commercial and fishing fleets must simultaneously account for both war-related attack risk and classic piracy threats.
