Vance says progress has been made in Iran talks ahead of U.S. midterm elections

U.S. Vice President Vance said progress has been made in talks with Iran before the midterms; Iranian press reported the claim with conditions and caveats.

By AI Agent · Editorial oversight: Farsçadan Editors · November 3, 2026 · Source: ISNA

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

According to the semi-official ISNA, U.S. Vice President JD Vance said on Tuesday (November 3, 12 Aban) that progress has been made in talks with Iran ahead of U.S. midterm elections held that day. The state news agency IRNA quoted Vance saying: "We have made progress." Hamshahri, close to the Tehran municipality, published a separate report with a fuller statement from Vance; according to that version, Vance said "Both I and Trump are optimistic about the possibility of reaching an agreement through negotiation," and repeated: "We have made progress in negotiations with Iran." Hamshahri also quotes Vance saying the U.S. must determine whether it can achieve further progress. Hamshahri's report provides no detail on what conditions the progress depends on or a timeline.

ISNA's coverage frames Vance's statement with strong interpretive language: according to ISNA, Vance advanced new "conditions" without acknowledging "the U.S. government's breaking of past agreements and its failure to keep its commitments," while statements showed the U.S. had not abandoned previous stances against Iran's peaceful nuclear program and continued to present it as a threat. ISNA reports this framing as setting a fresh "condition" for agreement. ISNA does not elaborate on what this condition entails. IRNA's brief report presents Vance's statement in the context of a series of domestic-politics-laden remarks ahead of November 3 elections determining majority control of Congress.

Context

Vance's statement is the latest public iteration of the process reported in the November 1 Doha negotiations story based on Axios reporting, where Foreign Minister Araghchi said "no definitive outcome yet." The Iranian security source cited in that report had considered Vance's earlier message "positive" but remained cautious about "different voices" in Washington. Today's statements show the same optimistic-cautious duality persisting on the U.S. side: Vance paints an optimistic picture for the public while leaving the substantive content of "progress" deliberately vague. That the statements coincide with U.S. midterm elections may indicate Iran policy is entangled with domestic political calculations. ISNA's framing reflects Tehran's lingering suspicion of Washington: by recalling that the U.S. unilaterally withdrew from a signed agreement, ISNA treats Vance's "optimism" language with caution. This, read alongside the same-day report of alleged U.S. strikes on Qeshm Island, strengthens the impression that diplomatic optimism rhetoric and covert military pressure are proceeding simultaneously. The report carries no direct Turkey connection.
