Turkish soldier killed at Iran border; fire assessed to have come from Iranian side

Turkish soldier killed at border in gunfire from Iranian side during illegal crossing response; both countries launched investigations.

By AI Agent · Editorial oversight: Farsçadan Editors · November 5, 2026 · Source: Hamshahri

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

According to Hamshahri, Turkey's Defense Ministry announced that a soldier died from gunfire assessed to have come from the Iranian side while responding to illegal border crossing in Hakkari's Yüksekova district. The ministry said investigations are being conducted in coordination with Iranian authorities to clarify all aspects of the incident.

According to Hamshahri's report, private Yusuf Sait Ünlü was wounded on October 31 (November 9 by the Iranian calendar) while responding to illegal border crossing in the area of responsibility of the Engin Mine border post. According to Turkey's Defense Ministry, Ünlü was airlifted to Hakkari State Hospital but died despite treatment efforts. The ministry announced that legal and administrative investigations were immediately opened, that a meeting with Iranian authorities was held at the scene on November 3, 2026, and that Iran had informed Turkey it was launching its own investigation into the incident. The statement said, "Efforts to clarify all aspects of this incident are ongoing in coordination with Iranian authorities and with great care." Condolences were offered to the soldier's family, the Turkish Armed Forces, and the nation. There is no independent verification of who opened fire or the incident's circumstances; this point has not been confirmed.

How the Persian press covered it

The economic daily Donya-e Eqtesad carried the same incident with broader source research, citing Anadolu Agency, Rudaw, and "Turkish Gazette" to add that the incident occurred in the 7th Border Brigade's area of responsibility, in the Esendere position on the border with Iran's West Azerbaijan province. Donya's report also noted that Turkish media and the Defense Ministry used the term "martyrdom" for the soldier's death. Hamshahri's headline framed the matter cautiously with the question "Did the fire come from the Iranian side?"

Context

Turkey and Iran share three official land border crossings: Ağrı-Gürbulak, Van-Kapıköy, and Hakkari-Esendere; the mountainous and sparsely populated terrain in the region has long been a site of smuggling and illegal crossing attempts. According to a report from November 4, the two countries had agreed to keep joint border crossings open 24 hours to increase trade; today's fatal incident occurred during a period of this commercial rapprochement. According to Turkish Minute's report, the incident came after a series of missile interdiction events between Iran and Turkish airspace in the first weeks of the war that began with U.S.-Israeli strikes in February; whether the earlier incidents are connected to today's death remains unclear. The fact that Turkish and Iranian officials held a joint meeting at the scene suggests that border security dialogue between the two countries continues despite tensions; however, no official determination has yet been announced on who fired and for what purpose. The same day, the U.S. Treasury Department added Istanbul-based Fugo Ground Services to its sanctions list for servicing blacklisted Iranian airlines, showing tension and rapprochement both playing out on the Turkey-Iran axis the same day.
