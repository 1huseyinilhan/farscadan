Shooting spree in Afyonkarahisar: eight killed across three villages

Armed attacks across three villages in western Turkey's Afyonkarahisar province left eight dead; suspect arrested in Eskişehir.

By AI Agent · Editorial oversight: Farsçadan Editors · November 6, 2026 · Source: ISNA

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

Per ISNA's account, a series of armed attacks across three villages in Afyonkarahisar province in western Turkey on Thursday left eight dead and one severely wounded. The gunman fled the scene and was arrested in the neighboring province of Eskişehir following a brief operation.

ISNA's initial report stated that the attacks occurred in Afyonkarahisar province, with victims found at multiple locations and inside three separate vehicles. Reports citing Turkey's state news agency Anadolu Agency—corroborated by English-language sources including Arab News and Business Standard—indicate the attacks took place in three villages: three were killed in Ayazini village, three in Kıyır village; two women shot in Yukarıtandır village died after being taken to hospital. The Afyonkarahisar governorate confirmed eight deaths and one severely injured.

Local sources say police closed roads to the affected villages and launched a broad search operation to locate the gunman. Security camera footage showed a man in a black jacket, white pants and sunglasses standing beside a red van with a long-barreled weapon. According to BBC's account of the Milliyet newspaper report, the vehicle believed to have been used by the gunman was found stuck in mud; the suspect had fled on foot.

The suspect was arrested in the neighboring province of Eskişehir after his escape. According to Arab News citing Anadolu Agency, the suspect fired on police officers during the arrest attempt; officers incapacitated and arrested him, with two security personnel lightly wounded.

The motive for the attack remains unclear. Authorities note that the victims did not know one another and are investigating whether the incident is connected to an earlier incident involving a woman seriously wounded in a parked vehicle in the Osmangazi district of Afyonkarahisar center. The detail in ISNA's report about "two teachers among the victims" was not corroborated in English-language sources; this detail remains unverified. Similarly, there is a minor discrepancy among sources regarding the exact date of the attacks: some English reports list the incident as Wednesday, most as Thursday; this point remains unverified.

The Afyonkarahisar governorate announced that the investigation is being pursued by multiple teams simultaneously and that the suspect's statement was taken following his arrest. According to local media, the suspect moved on foot for some time through rural terrain before arrest; gendarmerie and police special operations units conducted drone-assisted searches in the area. The incident is considered one of the deadliest mass shootings in rural Turkey in recent years by the number of casualties.

Context

Afyonkarahisar is a largely rural, agriculture-based province in western Turkey in the transition zone between the Aegean and Central Anatolia regions. Mass killings in villages are rare in Turkey but draw significant public attention when they occur. This report's appearance in the Persian press exemplifies Iranian media's practice of summarizing and relaying significant security incidents from abroad for its readers; the incident has no direct connection to Iran or regional tensions. Turkish authorities are continuing the investigation; no official explanation of the suspect's motivation or connection to any prior incident has been released. Armed violence stemming from disputes over land, family matters or inheritance occasionally makes headlines in Turkey's rural regions, but the scale of this incident and its scope across three different villages sets it apart from similar cases.

The presumption of innocence applies. Information about charges remains unclear, and responsibility is subject entirely to investigation authorities' findings.
