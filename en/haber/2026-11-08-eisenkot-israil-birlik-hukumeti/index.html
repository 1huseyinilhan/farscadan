Eisenkot, Netanyahu's rival, tasked with forming government in Israel

Herzog tasked Netanyahu's fiercest rival, Gadi Eisenkot, with forming a new government; 67 Knesset members had nominated him.

By AI Agent · Editorial oversight: Farsçadan Editors · November 8, 2026 · Source: IRNA

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

According to IRNA, Israeli President Isaac Herzog tasked Gadi Eisenkot, widely regarded as Benjamin Netanyahu's fiercest rival, with forming a new government on Saturday evening; the task will be formally conveyed to Eisenkot on Sunday at an official ceremony. According to reports based on Hebrew media, 67 of 120 Knesset members nominated Eisenkot, while 46 nominated Netanyahu.

According to the report, this decision came after Netanyahu released a video message the day before. According to ISNA and Hamshahri, Netanyahu warned against the possibility of a government dependent on Arab party support, calling for a "national camp" and a national unity government including all Zionist parties. Netanyahu said such a government would "permanently eliminate the nuclear threat of the Islamic Republic of Iran." According to Donya-ye Eghtesad's report, Eisenkot rejected any "rotational government" formula hours after Netanyahu's statement and conditioned his participation on removing what he called extremist parties from the coalition.

According to Khabaronline, before this decision, Israel's public broadcaster Kan reported that Herzog would task Eisenkot with forming the new cabinet on Sunday. According to the same report, Herzog said Sunday evening, "I am tasking the head of the Yesh Atid party with forming a government," and asked Eisenkot to be present at an official ceremony at the presidential residence. The report also noted that Avigdor Liberman, leader of Israel Our Home party, contacted Eisenkot to discuss the conditions of a possible coalition. According to ISNA and Hamshahri, after the task is formally conveyed, Eisenkot said "the time has come to form an emergency government"; the announcement contained no details about which parties would be included, and this remains unconfirmed.

According to Al Jazeera, this development resulted from a process begun after the November 1 elections: when no bloc secured the 61-seat Knesset majority, Herzog launched consultations with party representatives on November 4-5 about who would form a government. According to the same source, during these consultations Eisenkot emerged as the strongest candidate, while Netanyahu's team continued efforts to assume power without explicitly challenging the election results.

Context

Background:

  • October 28, 2026: Exit-poll figures in the Israeli election conflicted between channels; it was not confirmed that either the Netanyahu or Eisenkot bloc had reached a clear majority (details).
  • November 1, 2026: In a phone call between U.S. President Trump and Netanyahu, renewed strikes on Iran were discussed (details).
  • November 4-5, 2026: According to Al Jazeera, Herzog accepted the election results and began consultations with Knesset members on government formation; no bloc had reached the 61-seat majority.
  • November 7, 2026: Netanyahu issued a video call for a national unity government; hours later Eisenkot rejected the rotating-government proposal, and Herzog tasked him that same evening.

Who: Gadi Eisenkot is a former chief of staff of the Israeli Defense Forces; in the 2026 elections, he emerged as Netanyahu's strongest rival through the Yesh Atid party. Avigdor Liberman, whose Israel Our Home party is mentioned in coalition talks, is a secularly-nationalist movement.

By the numbers:

  • 67 of 120 Knesset members nominated Eisenkot, 46 nominated Netanyahu (Khabaronline, November 7, 2026); Al Jazeera's November 5 report indicated a similar distribution.
  • The person tasked with forming a government has 28 days by law; an additional 14 days can be granted if needed (per international media, carried by Al Jazeera).
  • The religiously observant United Torah Judaism party, nominated neither candidate, remaining neutral (per international media); this shows the 120 seats are not neatly divided between the two blocs.

What's next: Khabaronline reports Eisenkot is expected to conduct coalition negotiations with party leaders within the allotted time; talks with Liberman had begun before the task was formally conveyed. According to international media, the new Knesset is scheduled to be sworn in on November 10, 2026; this date will also determine the government-formation timeline. If Eisenkot cannot secure a 61-seat majority within the deadline, whether the task passes to another Knesset member remains unannounced.
