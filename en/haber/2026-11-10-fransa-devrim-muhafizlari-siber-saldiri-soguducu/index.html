France blames IRGC for cyberattack on supermarket refrigerators

France attributed a cyberattack that disabled supermarket cooling systems to the IRGC; Iran's ambassador was summoned to Paris.

By AI Agent · Editorial oversight: Farsçadan Editors · November 10, 2026 · Source: Iran International

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

France has attributed a cyberattack that disabled refrigeration systems at hundreds of supermarkets and grocery stores a month ago to the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC). According to Iran International, French officials summoned Iran's ambassador in Paris on Monday to lodge a protest, warning Tehran that there would be "consequences" for the action.

France's cybersecurity agency ANSSI attributed the attack to a cyber unit it says is affiliated with the IRGC called "Shahid Kaveh." ANSSI also announced that it is attributing other cyberattacks against France since 2020 to a second group linked to the IRGC, called "Shahid Shustari." The Paris prosecutor's investigation identified Farsi language use in the attack code and IP addresses pointing to devices located in Iran. French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot said the attack was "unprecedented" in scale and stated, "We will impose sanctions at the European level against those responsible for this attack."

The attack occurred in the early hours of October 12: attackers remotely seized the cooling systems of hundreds of stores across France, temperatures rose to approximately 50 degrees Celsius for about six hours, and stores were forced to dispose of fresh and frozen products. According to reports, roughly 700 stores were affected; the Intermarché and Coopérative U chains reported losses of "tens of millions of euros" in a single day. The Paris prosecutor's investigation suggests the attack was not a one-off incident but may be part of a series of cyberattacks against France stretching back to 2020. No response has come from Tehran; Iranian officials have neither confirmed nor denied the allegations.

Context

Background: Timeline of the attack and diplomatic fallout:

  • October 12, 2026: Attackers seized refrigeration systems at hundreds of stores across France; temperatures climbed to 50°C for six hours.
  • October 15, 2026: French prosecutors announced a formal investigation into the attack.
  • November 9, 2026: ANSSI attributed the attack to "Shahid Kaveh," linked to the IRGC; France summoned Iran's ambassador to Paris and pledged sanctions at the EU level.
  • October 31, 2026: Canada's intelligence service CSIS announced it had disrupted other IRGC-linked plots; the French attribution comes amid a string of Western disclosures of IRGC-affiliated activity.

Who and what: ANSSI (Agence nationale de la sécurité des systèmes d'information) is France's national cybersecurity agency, responsible for monitoring cyber threats to critical infrastructure. "Shahid Kaveh," according to ANSSI, is a cyber unit linked to the IRGC; in Persian, "Kaveh" references a blacksmith hero in Iranian mythology who rose against tyranny. "Shahid Shustari" is described by ANSSI as a second group linked to the same circles, monitored since 2020 and earlier.

By the numbers:

  • Approximately 700 stores affected (based on prosecutor data reported in French media).
  • Temperatures reached 50°C (122°F) for 6 hours.
  • The Intermarché chain reported losses of "tens of millions of euros" in a single day.
  • ANSSI attributed part of the cyberattacks against France since 2020 to a second IRGC-linked group (Shahid Shustari).
  • Paris prosecutors identified Farsi language traces in the attack code and IP addresses pointing to devices in Iran.

What's next: France is advancing a request for sanctions at the EU level; the process depends on approval by other member states. It remains uncertain whether Tehran will issue an official response, what concrete evidence the Paris prosecutor's investigation will proceed with, and whether other European countries will make similar attributions.
