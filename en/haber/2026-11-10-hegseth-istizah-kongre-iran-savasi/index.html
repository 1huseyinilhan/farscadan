Eight-count impeachment resolution against Hegseth introduced in Congress

Republican congressman Massie filed an eight-count impeachment resolution against Defense Secretary Hegseth over Iran war crimes; vote set for Wednesday.

By AI Agent · Editorial oversight: Farsçadan Editors · November 10, 2026 · Source: Khabaronline (AP and CNN)

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

Republican Representative Thomas Massie of Kentucky has filed an eight-count impeachment resolution against U.S. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. According to Khabaronline's report citing AP and CNN, the first article accuses Hegseth of waging unlawful war or military action against Iran, Yemen, and Venezuela; a vote is expected by Wednesday.

The resolution contends that Hegseth knowingly carried out orders to wage war against a foreign nation without Congressional authorization and without an imminent threat, and that his remaining in office would endanger civilians, the legality of U.S. military operations, and the integrity of the armed forces. Massie further accuses Hegseth of disregarding rules minimizing civilian casualties, participating in extrajudicial killings, and suppressing freedom of expression. According to AP, the resolution repeatedly references the fact that Trump never sought Congressional authority for war against Iran.

Hegseth said Monday evening, when asked about Republican support in Congress, "We are not concerned about that," and indicated he would hold a Pentagon press briefing Tuesday morning—his first since May. According to CNN, Hegseth is not backing down, and Trump has expressed full support for his Defense Secretary. Following the midterms, in which Republicans suffered historic losses, GOP representatives have blamed Hegseth. While a vote in the House requires only a majority, removal from office demands a two-thirds majority in the Senate, making it unlikely to reach that stage. According to a prior assessment by Axios, after the midterm defeat, the "blame game" in the House peaked, and the Iran war became the chief target of Republican anger; Hegseth is seen in Congress as the symbol of that war.

Context

Background: Timeline of impeachment efforts against Hegseth:

  • September 15, 2026: Massie filed his initial impeachment resolution against Hegseth; House Speaker Mike Johnson moved to adjourn early to delay the vote until after the midterms.
  • September 2026: Massie pledged to reintroduce the resolution when Congress returned after the midterms.
  • November 3, 2026: Republicans suffered a historic loss in the midterms; this result increased the number of GOP representatives blaming Hegseth (details).
  • November 9, 2026: Massie reintroduced an eight-count resolution.

Who and what: Thomas Massie is a Republican Representative from Kentucky known more for rule-following than party loyalty; he will not be returning to Congress next term after losing a primary to a Trump-backed opponent. "Impeachment" is the constitutional process by which the House of Representatives votes on charges against a federal official, with final judgment resting with the Senate by a two-thirds majority vote.

By the numbers: The resolution contains eight counts; a vote must occur by Wednesday (due to the resolution's "priority" status). Removal from office requires at least 67 of the Senate's 100 members (a two-thirds majority); absent almost all Democrats and a handful of Republicans besides Massie, such a majority appears remote.

What's next: Hegseth's Pentagon press briefing Tuesday morning and the House vote Wednesday will be closely watched. Reports indicate Senate Democrats have conditioned Hegseth's removal on cooperation over critical defense legislation; this situation may shape how the process proceeds after the vote.
