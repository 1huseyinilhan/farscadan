Iran grants 347,000 malnourished children free food basket credits via Kalabarg system

Iran's Labor Ministry allocated free food basket credits via Kalabarg to 347,647 malnourished children in households; part of anti-poverty pledge.

By AI Agent · Editorial oversight: Farsçadan Editors · November 11, 2026 · Source: Khabaronline

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

Iran's Ministry of Labor, Cooperatives, and Social Welfare announced that as of Aban 18 (November 9, 2026), free food basket credits would be allocated via the Kalabarg system to families of 347,647 malnourished children. According to the ministry's Deputy Secretary for Welfare and Economic Affairs, Yakub Andayesh, this represents the government's commitment to combating poverty. According to Khabaronline (citing Mehr news agency), this marks the eighth implementation round in 1405 (2026) of support targeting children aged 5 months to 59 months with malnutrition.

According to Andayesh's statement, the support is being implemented in two tiers based on household economic status: for 286,714 children in very poor households, 20 million rials (approximately 2 million tumans) in credit; for 60,933 children in moderately needy households, 12 million rials (approximately 1.2 million tumans) in credit. The credit is assigned via the Kalabarg electronic system to the household head's national identification number. The credit can be spent within one month on basic food items—rice, meat, chicken, eggs, and dairy products—approved by the Ministry of Health.

The official stated that eligible children are identified through height-weight measurements at health clinics (anthropometric monitoring); data is compiled through medical schools and relayed to the Labor Ministry through a means-testing process based on the Welfare Database of Iranians. Andayesh noted that war, sanctions, and recent severe economic fluctuations have heightened the need to prevent child malnutrition, emphasizing that the period from birth to age five represents a "golden window" in which over 90 percent of brain development occurs and that unresolved malnutrition during this window can cause permanent brain damage, disability, and intergenerational poverty cycles.

This child-targeted support is a separate and additional application from the general Kalabarg household credit program. Within the same week, another Khabaronline report noted that approximately 10 percent of households eligible for the general Kalabarg program would be removed from coverage and that clarification was underway over who would be added; this is being framed as the government's effort to redirect scarce resources to the most-needed households. The credit is not a physical card or paper coupon; it is an electronic balance tied to the household head's national ID number and can be used directly at checkout in partner supermarket chains.

Context

Background:

  • 1980s: Paper food-coupon programs spread during the Iran-Iraq War.
  • June 2023: The current Kalabarg electronic system was established as the "National Credit Network" and began operations under the Labor Ministry.
  • Throughout 1405 (2026): Child nutrition support is reloaded monthly; the November 2026 application is the year's eighth round.

Key figures / concepts: Kalabarg is Iran's government-administered electronic credit system for household purchases of basic food items; fuller details are available in Farsçadan's glossary entry. Under the system, coverage is determined by household income rank, classified into income brackets ("dahak") from one to nine.

By the numbers:

  • Total 347,647 children are benefiting: 286,714 receiving 20 million rials and 60,933 receiving 12 million rials in credit (Labor Ministry statement, Khabaronline, November 10, 2026).
  • According to Rudaw reporting, the government is planning to remove approximately 15 million people classified as having "better" material conditions from the general Kalabarg program to increase the amount allocated to the most needy households.
  • According to Al Jazeera's 2025 analysis, when food-coupon programs like Kalabarg are evaluated alongside indirect tax increases implemented during the same period, the net effect on low-income households remains contested.

What's next: The Labor Ministry stated it will maintain the monthly reload schedule for the program; no separate clarification has been issued on how unused credit within one month will be handled. Whether the scope reduction in the general Kalabarg program will affect child-nutrition credits remains unspecified. Andayesh confirmed the program will continue monthly throughout 1405, with future implementation rounds updated using the same means-testing method; however, no specific date was given for when the broader scope reduction takes effect.
