NYT: U.S. effort to convince Turkey, Iraq, Pakistan to close Iran land border failed

New York Times reported that U.S. effort to convince Turkey, Iraq, and Pakistan to close land borders with Iran failed.

By AI Agent · Editorial oversight: Farsçadan Editors · November 12, 2026 · Source: Khabaronline

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

The New York Times reported that the U.S. spent weeks in intensive diplomacy trying to convince Turkey, Iraq, and Pakistan to shut down land border crossings with Iran, but the effort failed. According to Khabaronline's account of the Times reporting, officials from all three countries told Washington that closing the borders would damage their own economies; communities in border regions depend on trade and cross-border transit.

These countries also warned that border closures could trigger unrest and protests in frontier areas. In this context, Iraq's Border Crossings Organization chief Omer al-Wali announced that the Shaib border gate reopened on November 10 after technical procedures were completed; the same official added that the Mandali crossing would temporarily close starting November 13 for maintenance. Earlier, Iraq's Foreign Minister Fuad Hussein said the U.S. Treasury Department had requested that Baghdad close all but two land crossings with Iran, but he noted that implementing this demand would be difficult.

Khabaronline's report cites the NYT through the Mehr news agency, showing that details reached Iran's press secondhand; while emphasizing the three countries' unified stance against U.S. pressure, the article does not include a separate statement from Turkey about its own border policy.

Context

Background:

  • September 20, 2026: U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced that Iranian airports serving Iranian airlines could face sanctions; this threat ran parallel to land-border pressure.
  • November 8, 2026: CENTCOM announced it had established a task force called "Task Force Iron Gate" to broaden the land blockade of Iran; the unit's goal was to cut off weapon transfers, sanctioned goods, and illicit revenue crossing Iran's borders.
  • October 21, 2026: Iraq closed the Shaib border gate citing smuggling concerns; the gate reopened November 10 after technical procedures finished.
  • Mid-September 2026: According to reports, the number of trucks waiting at Turkey's border reached seventeen thousand, concrete evidence that Turkey did not fully close commercial border crossings.

Who and what: Task Force Iron Gate (in English sources "Task Force Iron Gate"), a task force established by U.S. Central Command to oversee the land blockade of Iran; it operates under CENTCOM Commander General Brad Cooper. According to English-language reporting, the unit's mandate is to cut off weapon transfers, sanctioned goods, and illicit revenue across Iran's land borders and across the Caspian Sea.

By the numbers:

  • Iraq and Pakistan closed or temporarily halted some land crossings with Iran (Shaib, Gabd, Taftan) in October–November 2026 (harici.com.tr and Newsweek, November 2026 compilations).
  • In mid-September, the number of trucks waiting at Turkey's border was estimated at 17,000 (Newsweek, September 2026).

Turkey factor: According to the NYT account, Turkey, Iraq, and Pakistan together resisted the U.S. closure demand; English-language compilations describe Turkey as the blockade's "weakest link" because Ankara has not committed publicly to fully shutting commercial border crossings. This reflects a situation where Iran's land trade routes are not entirely closed, but U.S. pressure has narrowed Tehran's room to maneuver.

What's ahead: Iraq's Mandali border gate will temporarily close starting November 13 for maintenance; no timeline has been announced for when or how the U.S. Task Force Iron Gate will tighten enforcement at land borders.
