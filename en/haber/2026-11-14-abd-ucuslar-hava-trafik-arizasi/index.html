U.S. air traffic crashes: 7,000 flights affected, cyber attack ruled out

FAA halts all U.S. flights due to communications failure; officials say no signs of malicious attack.

By AI Agent · Editorial oversight: Farsçadan Editors · November 14, 2026 · Source: Donya-ye Eqtesad

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

The U.S. Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) temporarily grounded all passenger flights nationwide Friday after a communications failure at air traffic control centers. According to Donya-ye Eqtesad, the FAA officially announced the closure of all airports, and approximately 7,000 flights were cancelled or delayed.

According to ISNA's report, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the failure was "a communications issue affecting all air traffic control centers" and that FAA technicians were investigating; Duffy also warned that the current navigation system is "very outdated." According to Ettela'at, an Interior Security official, Markovin Molin, stated: "There are currently no signs indicating a malicious attack; it appears to be only a remote communications outage." According to Iran International's more detailed report, the FAA halted all airport departures Friday around 4 p.m. local time after communications problems emerged at several regional air traffic control centers; by 7:05 p.m., 670 flights had returned to their departure airports and 157 had been diverted elsewhere, according to FlightAware data. According to data firm Cirium, cited by the Associated Press, around 11,000 flights carrying more than 1.5 million passengers and scheduled to depart after 4 p.m. were affected.

Trump reportedly rejected the possibility of either a cyber attack or an AI-related malfunction. According to Iran International, investigators are also examining whether there was a compatibility issue between the FAA's legacy communications equipment and a newly deployed AI-powered traffic management tool it launched recently; the tool's developer denied any connection between the system and Friday's outage. According to Hamshahri's live reports, passengers at Chicago O'Hare and other major airports learned of delays from signs at security checkpoints; many sports teams were forced to reschedule their travel plans accordingly.

State and semi-official sources largely framed the outage as a technical glitch, while Ettela'at raised the cyber attack question in its headline before reporting the official denial. No source confirmed the exact cause of the failure; this point remained unverified.

Context

Background:

  • September 11, 2001: The first nationwide grounding of all U.S. flights occurred.
  • January 11, 2023: The FAA halted domestic flights for approximately two hours due to a failure in its pilot notification system (NOTAM), marking the second nationwide halt on record.
  • November 14, 2026: Communications failure at air traffic control centers triggers the third nationwide flight halt since 2001.

Key terms: FAA (Federal Aviation Administration), the U.S. civil aviation regulator managing air traffic control centers nationwide. NOTAM (Notice to Air Missions), an electronic alert system providing pilots and airports with real-time hazard and restriction notifications.

By the numbers:

  • The FAA employs more than 14,000 air traffic controllers managing approximately 44,000 daily flights from U.S. airports (Ettela'at, November 13, 2026, citing Airlines for America data).
  • Friday's outage affected approximately 11,000 flights carrying more than 1.5 million passengers scheduled to depart after 4 p.m.; by 9 p.m., more than 6,000 flights had been cancelled (Iran International, citing Axios/FlightAware data, November 14, 2026).

What's next: Duffy said technicians are investigating the outage's source; a possible connection to the FAA's digital control tower project, launched last week in Alaska and Florida, is also being examined. Experts say hours of delays could create a domino effect on U.S. and global aviation through the end-of-year holiday period—a forecast not yet realized that was reported as analysis.
