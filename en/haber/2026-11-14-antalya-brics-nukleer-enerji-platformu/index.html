BRICS nuclear energy platform convenes in Antalya with Iran delegation

BRICS member states' Nuclear Energy Platform held its annual meeting in Antalya; Iran delegation attended.

By AI Agent · Editorial oversight: Farsçadan Editors · November 14, 2026 · Source: IRNA

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

The annual meeting of the BRICS member states' Nuclear Energy Platform took place Friday in Antalya, Turkey. According to IRNA, Iran's delegation attended the meeting; the news agency reported that discussions focused on developing nuclear interactions among BRICS nations.

According to IRNA's brief report, the meeting was held as part of World Atomic Days activities and constituted the platform's annual gathering. The agency did not detail the full agenda, identify Iran's delegation members, or indicate whether a specific agreement was signed. According to the World Nuclear Association's event calendar, the Antalya program ran November 12-13 for two days; the first day covered new nuclear power plant projects and general sector issues, while the second addressed "green transition," small modular reactors (SMRs), and the BRICS Nuclear Platform's annual conference. The calendar lists Elsie Pule as the platform's lead coordinator; IRNA's report contained no details about her or other speakers. No other Persian-language source published detailed coverage of the meeting, so a second Persian source framework was not added to this report.

The platform's previous annual conference took place in Moscow in September 2025 during "World Atomic Week," where Iran participated and the platform's first strategic document was approved; according to Iran Daily's coverage at that time, a "BRICS nuclear platform strategy" was adopted at the meeting. The Antalya meeting's specific follow-up steps toward implementing this strategy were not specified in IRNA's report.

The BRICS Nuclear Energy Platform was established as a mechanism to promote civil nuclear technology, personnel training, and regulatory cooperation among member states. The platform's previous annual conference was held in Moscow in September 2025 during World Atomic Week; participants approved the platform's first strategic document and discussed 2026 plans. Iran joined BRICS as a full member at the beginning of 2024; thus the Iran delegation's presence in Antalya demonstrates Tehran's continued participation in the BRICS nuclear cooperation network.

Context

Background:

  • January 2024: Iran joined BRICS as a full member alongside the UAE, Egypt, and Ethiopia.
  • September 26, 2025: BRICS Nuclear Energy Platform's first annual conference was held in Moscow during World Atomic Week; participants approved the platform's first strategic document.
  • November 12-13, 2026: The platform's second annual conference took place in Antalya, Turkey, as part of World Atomic Days.

Key terms: BRICS Nuclear Energy Platform, a coordination mechanism led by Russia that brings together member states' nuclear agencies (Iran's Atomic Energy Organization, Russia's Rosatom, and others) to promote cooperation in reactor construction, fuel cycles, and human resources training; not a binding treaty.

By the numbers:

  • BRICS expanded to 10 full members in January 2024 (Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa, Iran, UAE, Egypt, Ethiopia, Saudi Arabia — World Nuclear Association, October 15, 2026 update).
  • The Antalya meeting coincided with the second day of World Atomic Days' two-day program on November 12-13, 2026 (World Nuclear Association event calendar).

Turkey dimension: The meeting's location in Turkey shows that Ankara can host such international nuclear cooperation platforms despite not being a BRICS member. Turkey's own nuclear program overlaps with this ecosystem: the Akkuyu Nuclear Power Plant under construction by Rosatom in Mersin's Akkuyu district is the largest concrete example of Turkish-Russian nuclear cooperation and evidence of Rosatom's presence in the region as a dominant BRICS actor. IRNA's report did not mention Akkuyu directly; this connection is background information added by Farsçadan.

What's next: IRNA did not provide a date for the conference's final communique or follow-up steps. Aside from information that the platform's previous annual conference (Moscow, 2025) discussed 2026 plans, the Antalya meeting's concrete outcomes have not yet been shared with the public.
