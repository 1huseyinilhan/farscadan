UN Security Council debates Iran war—Russia and China call for immediate ceasefire

UN Security Council's Middle East session called for ending war with Iran; Iran warned of immediate retaliation to new attacks.

By AI Agent · Editorial oversight: Farsçadan Editors · November 14, 2026 · Source: Donya-ye Eqtesad

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

During the UN Security Council's "Situation in the Middle East" session, Russian and Chinese representatives called Friday (November 13) for an immediate end to U.S. and Israeli military operations against Iran. According to Donya-ye Eqtesad, Iran's UN Permanent Representative and Minister Counselor Gholamhossein Darzi warned in the same session that any new U.S. attack would be met with "immediate and decisive" retaliation.

According to the full text published by Donya-ye Eqtesad, Darzi said, "Iran has never closed the door to diplomacy over the past two years" and that repeated such experiences had created deep distrust, making progress impossible without credible guarantees for renewed negotiations. Darzi used the language: "Let me be clear and direct: should the U.S. and Israeli regime resume their aggressive war, it will be met with an immediate and decisive response, with no room for doubt, targeting the source of the aggression. The U.S. representative has neither legal, political, nor moral standing to accuse Iran."

According to Hamshahri, citing Mehr, Russia's UN Security Council representative charged that the U.S. and Israel have set the region ablaze with aggression against Iran, sparking a dangerous chain of regional conflict; the representative also cited a purported missile attack on a school in Mina'bad that killed more than 150 children and an alleged assassination attempt against Iran's leadership among events that "shocked the international community." The Russian representative added that the International Atomic Energy Agency has recorded no evidence of Iran diverting nuclear material or transferring it for military purposes. None of these claims were independently verified or confirmed by independent or Western sources. According to Hamshahri, citing Tasnim, China's representative said conflicts must be halted immediately, that war has no winners, and that unilateral military action must cease; thanking Qatar and Pakistan for mediation efforts, China called for the U.S. and Iran to return to the "Islamabad Accord."

Context

Background:

  • November 12, 2026: G7 foreign ministers issued a statement after their Paris meeting calling on Iran to guarantee free passage in the Strait of Hormuz.
  • November 13, 2026 (Friday local time): UN Security Council held a session titled "Situation in the Middle East" addressing the Iran-U.S.-Israel war.

Key terms: Gholamhossein Darzi, Iran's ambassador and minister counselor at its UN Permanent Mission, representing Iran's permanent delegation in New York. The UN Security Council's "Situation in the Middle East" agenda item is an open session format for regularly discussing regional security crises.

By the numbers:

  • G7 foreign ministers' joint statement issued November 13 called for all UN member states to implement UN sanctions that were reinstated through the "snapback mechanism" in September 2025 (Donya-ye Eqtesad's November 14, 2026 report on the G7 statement).
  • The "Islamabad Accord" referenced by China's representative was described as a negotiation framework that emerged through Qatari and Pakistani mediation; its date and contents were not detailed in Persian sources.

What's next: Darzi said Iran would continue to maintain its deterrent and respond to any new attack; no specific timeline or Council resolution was announced. The content of the U.S. and Israeli representatives' statements and whether the Council will produce a draft resolution remain unknown.

Key terms

  • Tasnim and Fars news agenciesTwo Iranian news agencies known for their closeness to the IRGC, founded in 2003 (Fars) and 2012 (Tasnim).
  • Principlists (Osulgarayan) and reformistsThe two main political camps of the Islamic Republic. Principlists defend the revolution's principles; reformists advocate political opening.
  • Strait of HormuzThe strait linking the Persian Gulf to the Gulf of Oman, through which about a quarter of the world's seaborne oil trade passes.
  • Snapback (trigger mechanism)A provision tied to the 2015 nuclear deal that automatically restores UN sanctions on Iran. It was triggered in September 2025.
