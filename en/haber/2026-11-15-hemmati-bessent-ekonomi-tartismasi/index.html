Hemmati responds to Bessent: 'We've secured $33 billion in foreign exchange; it's early for winter talk'

Central Bank Governor Hemmati responded to U.S. Treasury Secretary Bessent's 'tough winter' warning with claims of $33 billion in foreign exchange secured.

By Farsçadan Editors

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

Central Bank Governor Abdolnassar Hemmati responded during a television interview to recent statements from U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent about Iran's economy. According to Khabaronline's reporting from the Sima news outlet, Hemmati said: "The Treasury Secretary still hasn't counted the chicken I mentioned; let him count it first, then speak about winter; it's too early."

According to reporting from Ettelaat from the same interview, Hemmati added: "Thank God the situation is good; we've secured approximately $33 billion in foreign exchange during this period. As we promised, we're also providing medicines and essential goods." He continued: "Hopefully there will be no problems; all our efforts are toward ensuring there are no problems." Hemmati's statement is based on his own claims, which the U.S. Treasury Department has not independently verified; the time period covered by the $33 billion figure and the breakdown of sources remain unclear and unconfirmed.

The statement Hemmati was responding to came Friday, November 13, when Bessent stated that Washington would maintain economic pressure on Iran and that Trump no longer saw any obligation to reach an agreement with Iran's government, saying "Iranians face a long and cold winter" (according to reporting compiled by Iran International). Bessent's comment continues a campaign he has been leading since August 2026, which he termed "economic D-Day"; according to Al Jazeera, on August 31 Bessent claimed Iran's economy could collapse within months.

Bessent's August campaign was not limited to rhetoric. According to Semafor's reporting, on August 24 the Treasury Department imposed sanctions on 60 individuals, institutions, and vessels targeting digital assets, gold, technology, aviation, and maritime sectors; Bessent the same day provided a "cleanup period" for countries with commercial ties to Iran and expanded secondary sanctions threats. According to Al Jazeera's August 20 reporting, Bessent described this package as "economic D-Day" and said the new measures would "collapse" Iran's economy.

According to Investinglive, Bessent also claimed Iran's leadership "feels the economic pressure," and in another Fox News statement suggested that Trump's "economic fury" combined with naval blockade was "strangling" the regime. Hemmati's response today represents the latest link in this months-long chain of mutual statements; when Hemmati first used the "count the chicken" metaphor remains unclear in this report.

Hemmati's assessment that "the situation is good" can also be compared with independent estimates Farsçadan has previously reported: the International Monetary Fund, in its October 2026 report, projected Iran's economy would contract 7.6 percent in 2026, while Tehran's own statistics projected a steeper 10.1 percent decline (see details). Hemmati did not directly reference these projections in today's interview; the gap between the two assessments has not been independently reconciled.

Context

Background:

  • August 20, 2026: Bessent said new economic measures would "collapse" Iran (Al Jazeera).
  • August 24, 2026: Treasury Department imposed sanctions on 60 individuals, institutions, and vessels in digital assets, gold, technology, aviation, and maritime sectors; Bessent defined a "cleanup period" for countries with commercial ties to Iran (Semafor).
  • August 31, 2026: Bessent said Iran's economy "could collapse within months" (Al Jazeera).
  • November 13, 2026: Bessent reiterated his "long and cold winter" warning; Hemmati responded in today's interview.

Key concepts: Scott Bessent is U.S. Treasury Secretary and has served as the spokesman for the secondary sanctions campaign against Iran since August 2026. Abdolnassar Hemmati is Governor of Iran's Central Bank (Bank-e Markazi); the expression "count the chicken at the end of fall" is a Persian idiom meaning "don't declare victory prematurely."

By the numbers:

  • According to Hemmati's statement, the Central Bank has secured approximately $33 billion in foreign exchange during this period; this figure has not been confirmed by independent sources (Khabaronline/Ettelaat, November 15, 2026).
  • The Treasury Department's August 2026 sanctions package targeted 60 individuals, institutions, and vessels (Semafor, August 24, 2026).

Turkey angle: According to Semafor reporting, when Bessent expanded secondary sanctions threats in August 2026, he identified China, Turkey, and the UAE as Iran's largest trading partners and called on these countries to sever commercial ties with Iran; Turkey's specific response or actions taken in response to this call have not been publicly reported as of this story.

What's next: As the winter season Bessent referenced approaches, the coming months will test his claims about Iran's economy through concrete indicators—foreign exchange rates, export volumes, and energy revenues—but no timeline has been announced.
