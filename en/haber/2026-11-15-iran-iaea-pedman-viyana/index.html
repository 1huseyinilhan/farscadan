Iran's Vienna response to Grossi: Safeguards cannot resume to normal under extraordinary conditions

Iran's Vienna mission rejected IAEA demands, saying safeguards won't resume to normal under wartime conditions.

By Farsçadan Editors

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

Iran's mission to international organizations in Vienna responded to International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) Director General Rafael Grossi's "one-sided report" on Iran's peaceful nuclear program. According to Kayhan's reporting from Tasnim, the mission stated that safeguards implementation cannot be returned to normal under extraordinary conditions.

According to the mission's statement, the IAEA's reports on Iran are technically and legally flawed, were misused in the approval process, and their findings are based on Governing Board resolutions that exceed legal authority. The statement said: "As Grossi himself has acknowledged, unprecedented attacks on Iran's safeguarded nuclear facilities created an extraordinary situation, disrupted inspections, forced the Agency to withdraw inspectors, and necessitated addressing Iran's security concerns." The mission further argued that while the U.S. removed the word "aggression" from biased resolutions, this cannot erase the consequences from reality, and stated: "Normal safeguards implementation cannot be resumed under extraordinary conditions; the Governing Board must address the roots of today's situation."

Behind this statement lies the IAEA's complete withdrawal of inspectors from Iran following the June 2025 U.S.-Israeli strikes, and the inability to gain access to attacked facilities since that date. According to Yeni Şafak/AA reporting, on October 27, 2026, Grossi briefed the Security Council, requesting immediate access to verify previously declared nuclear material—including 440 kilograms of uranium enriched to approximately 60%—and described this as Iran's legal obligation. According to Grossi, safeguards issues have persisted for more than five years. According to Manorama Yearbook's October 28, 2026 reporting, Iran refused IAEA access to the bombed nuclear facilities.

The same dispute continued in the UN Security Council meeting on November 13. According to Farsçadan's reporting on that meeting, France's representative emphasized the uncertainty surrounding the fate of 440 kilograms of uranium enriched to approximately 60% and called on Iran to grant IAEA inspectors access to all nuclear facilities; Greece also urged Iran to cooperate fully with the Agency and support independent verification (see details). Iran's Vienna mission's statement today directly answers these demands: by arguing that "normal safeguards implementation cannot resume under extraordinary conditions," the mission implies that full access as requested by France and Greece is not a prerequisite—a direct contradiction between the two sides that has not yet been resolved by a third party.

The statement also complained that IAEA access to the Bushehr Nuclear Power Plant was "restricted," but suggested the Agency had not answered the question of what it was actually searching for at the bombed facilities. This claim has not been verified by independent sources beyond Iran's own account.

The IAEA maintained its access request in its October 27 Security Council briefing, while Iran's statement defends that full access cannot be provided "under extraordinary conditions"; the legal interpretation difference between the two sides has not been verified.

Context

Background:

  • June 2025: Following U.S.-Israeli strikes, the IAEA completely withdrew inspectors from Iran (Yeni Şafak/AA, October 2026).
  • February 28, 2026: The second wave of U.S.-Israeli conflict with Iran began; from this point forward, Iran further restricted safeguards access.
  • October 27, 2026: Grossi briefed the UN Security Council requesting access to verify 440 kilograms of enriched uranium.
  • October 28, 2026: Iran refused IAEA access to the attacked facilities (Manorama Yearbook).
  • November 13, 2026: UN Security Council meeting on the Middle East where France raised the 440-kilogram uranium question (see details).

Key concepts: Safeguards (Persian pâdman, protection/security) refers to the Comprehensive Safeguards Agreement framework under which the IAEA, under the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (NPT), has the right to inspect member states' nuclear facilities. Rafael Grossi is an Argentine diplomat who has served as IAEA Director General since 2019.

By the numbers:

  • According to the IAEA, Iran possesses 440 kilograms of uranium enriched to approximately 60%, and verification of this stock has been impossible for months (Yeni Şafak/AA, October 27, 2026).
  • The IAEA has been unable to send inspectors to the attacked facilities since June 2025; safeguards issues have accumulated over more than five years (Yeni Şafak/AA, October 27, 2026).
  • All IAEA inspectors were withdrawn from Iran during the 12-day conflict in June 2025; no return to the attacked facilities has been achieved since then (Yeni Şafak/AA, October 27, 2026).

What's next: Iran's Atomic Energy Organization is reported to be in "constructive" contact with the IAEA regarding security equipment for transferring spent fuel at Bushehr to dry storage, with the transfer expected early in 2027; the IAEA Governing Board's reconsideration of the issue at its next meeting and how Iran will respond to France and Greece's demand for full access are matters to be monitored.

The presumption of innocence applies.
