Forty-one foreign nationals and 2 smugglers arrested in Salmas near Turkey border

Border guards in Salmas arrested 41 foreign nationals and two smugglers in three separate operations near Turkey.

By AI Agent · Editorial oversight: Farsçadan Editors · November 15, 2026 · Source: IRNA

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

In Salmas, a town on Iran's border with Turkey in West Azerbaijan province, border guard forces conducted three separate operations in which they arrested 41 foreign nationals and 2 human smugglers who facilitated illegal border crossings. According to IRNA reporting, the Commander of the Salmas Border Guard Unit announced that the operations were conducted within the unit's area of responsibility.

In IRNA's report, details such as the nationalities of the arrested persons, the date they were apprehended, or the specific location where they were stopped were not shared; these details remain unconfirmed. The report also does not clarify whether the ultimate destination was Turkey or another country; however, Salmas's geographic location makes the town a known waypoint on irregular migration routes from Iran to Turkey and from there to Europe.

These types of operations are not unique to today in Salmas. A similar pattern is visible on Turkey's side of the border: according to Daily Sabah reporting, in Van province, which shares Iran's longest border with Turkey, security forces regularly stop migrant groups attempting to cross the Iran border and vehicles transporting them. According to Rudaw, in April 2026, Turkey conducted simultaneous operations in 24 provinces that resulted in the arrest of 132 suspected migrant smuggling organizers. These figures suggest that today's arrest in Salmas should be understood not in isolation but as part of ongoing smuggling pressure on both sides of the border; however, whether Iranian and Turkish authorities are conducting joint or coordinated operations on this issue has not been disclosed in this report.

IRNA's report contains no details about smuggling methods; in previous operations in the region, smugglers have been known to attempt to move migrants on foot across mountainous terrain or in secret compartments in vehicles across the border, but which methods were used in Salmas's three operations today was not specified. The processing that border guards will apply to the 41 apprehended persons—whether they will be deported or held in administrative detention—is also among the unaddressed points in the report.

Context

Background:

  • April 2026: According to Rudaw reporting, Turkey arrested 132 suspected migrant smuggling organizers in simultaneous operations across 24 provinces.
  • Previous years: According to Daily Sabah reporting, Van province, which shares Turkey's longest border with Iran, is among the locations where the highest security measures against irregular migration are implemented; migrant groups attempting to cross the Iran border are regularly apprehended in the province.

Key concepts: Salmas (Persian Salmâs) is a town center in West Azerbaijan province, located southeast of Lake Van and near the Turkish border; the region is historically known as one of the land border crossing points between Iran and Turkey. Border Guard Unit (Hang-e Marzbani) is one of the border security units under Iran's Ministry of Interior.

By the numbers:

  • According to IRNA's report today, 41 foreign nationals and 2 smugglers were arrested in three operations in Salmas (IRNA, November 15, 2026).
  • According to Rudaw reporting, Turkey arrested 132 suspected smuggling organizers in April 2026 operations across the country (Rudaw, April 25, 2026).

Turkey angle: Salmas is located in West Azerbaijan, one of Iran's provinces closest to Turkey's border, and the region is a known waypoint on irregular migration routes where migrants attempt to reach Europe through Turkey. According to Daily Sabah reporting, similar arrests are regularly reported on Turkey's side in Van province; whether operations on both sides of the border are coordinated has not been disclosed in this report, and this detail remains unconfirmed. This stretch of border also drew attention on November 5, when a Turkish soldier was killed by gunfire believed to have come from Iran (details).

What's next: IRNA's report provided no timeline regarding the judicial process the arrested persons and smugglers will undergo; whether similar operations will continue at the border and whether attempted crossings will increase during winter months due to mountainous terrain conditions are issues to be monitored.
