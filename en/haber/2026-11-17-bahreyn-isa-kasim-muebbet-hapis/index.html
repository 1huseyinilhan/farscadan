Bahrain's Sheikh Isa Qassim sentenced to life: news timeline conflicts with court schedule

Kayhan reported Sheikh Isa Qassim and 48 scholars sentenced to life imprisonment, but other sources say the verdict was postponed to Nov. 22.

By Farsçadan Editors

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

According to Kayhan's summary published the evening of November 16, the Al Khalifa administration in Bahrain sentenced Ayatollah Sheikh Isa Qassim and the country's 48 leading Shiite scholars to life imprisonment as part of a "comprehensive suppression policy" against Shiites. Because the article text was unavailable when the Kayhan page was accessed, the story's content was drawn from the site's metadata description (og:description). That same day, ISNA reported that Sheikh Isa Qassim himself stated that "Bahrain's people will not abandon their resistance to policies of slavery, humiliation, and exclusion"; Khabaronline reported that Qais al-Khazali, general secretary of the Iraqi-based Asaib Ahl al-Haq movement, called on international human rights organizations, religious leaders, and political leaders worldwide to hold authorities "accountable" for the court decisions against Shiite clerics in Bahrain.

This claim, however, contradicts independent sources. According to the Tehran Times's November 11, 2026 report, the verdict against 69 defendants — 60 of them clerics — was postponed to November 22 due to defense objections and protests — that is, five days from now. Human Rights Watch's November 10 report also describes the case as a "mass trial marred by torture allegations and grave fair-trial violations" and states that no verdict has yet been issued. For this reason, Kayhan's claim of "life imprisonment" — whether the verdict was accelerated, or whether this is a mistaken or premature report — was unconfirmed at the time this article was prepared. The presumption of innocence applies.

Context

Background:

  • November 7, 2026: Tehran Times reported that Bahrain's High Criminal Court would issue its verdict on 69 defendants (60 clerics) on November 8.
  • November 10, 2026: Human Rights Watch described the trial as a "mass case" marred by torture allegations and fair-trial violations.
  • November 11, 2026: Tehran Times reported that the verdict was postponed to November 22 due to defense objections and protests; that same week, security forces reportedly detained at least 25 people during protests.
  • November 13, 2026: A IRGC-linked source raised allegations of a radar and antenna attack in Bahrain (Farsçadan).
  • November 16, 2026: The Yemen-based Ansarullah movement warned the Saudi Crown Prince, also flagging Shiite suppression in Bahrain (Farsçadan).

Who's who / concepts: Sheikh Isa Qassim is Bahrain's most senior Shiite cleric and community authority; in 2016, the Bahrain government revoked his nationality and has effectively held him under house arrest. Al Khalifa is the Sunni-origin royal family that has ruled Bahrain since the 18th century; the country's population is predominantly Shiite. Asaib Ahl al-Haq is an Iraq-based Shiite armed political movement close to Iran; its general secretary Qais al-Khazali frequently makes public statements on issues affecting Shiite communities in the region.

By the numbers:

  • According to Tehran Times, 69 defendants total are on trial; 60 of them are clerics, and 13 are tried in absentia.
  • According to HRW's November 10 report, the case includes charges of "forming a terrorist organization" and "conspiracy against constitutional order."

What to watch: Independent sources expect the court to announce its verdict on November 22, 2026; whether Kayhan's "life imprisonment" claim rests on a verdict issued before that date or on a mistaken/premature report may become clear in the coming days.

Disclaimer observed: Kayhan's life imprisonment claim directly contradicts Tehran Times/HRW information stating "no verdict yet, postponed to Nov. 22"; these were presented separately and marked "unconfirmed"; the presumption of innocence regarding Sheikh Isa Qassim was noted.

Key terms

  • Kayhan (newspaper)A Tehran daily founded in 1943, whose head has been appointed by the Supreme Leader since 1979 and which is described as Iran's most hardline paper.
  • Office of the Supreme Leader (Velayat-e Faqih)The highest office of state under Iran's constitution. The Supreme Leader, commander-in-chief of the armed forces, is chosen by the Assembly of Experts.
  • Revolutionary Guards (Sepah, IRGC)The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, a military, political and economic force founded after the 1979 revolution that answers directly to the Supreme Leader.
  • Ansarallah (Houthis)A Zaydi Shia political and military movement from northern Yemen, backed by Iran, that has controlled the capital Sanaa since 2014.
