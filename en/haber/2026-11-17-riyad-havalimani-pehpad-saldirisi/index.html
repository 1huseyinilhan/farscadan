Flights halted at Riyadh airport; drone attack from Yemen reported

According to Hamshahri, flight operations at Riyadh's King Khalid Airport were suspended following a reported drone attack from Yemen.

By Farsçadan Editors

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

According to Hamshahri's "breaking" report published the morning of November 17, flight operations at King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh were suspended following a drone attack from Yemen. According to Hamshahri's account, media outlets reported hearing explosion sounds in Saudi Arabia's eastern region. ISNA also confirmed the same attack in a brief report and stated that flight operations at the airport had been suspended.

Hamshahri's report also stated that following retreats suffered by the Arab coalition in Yemen, the Saudi royal court appointed Fiyad al-Ruwaili as joint forces commander through a decree, retaining his current position; the report did not claim direct causation between these two developments but reported them in sequence. According to Hamshahri's framing, this appointment signals a reorganization of the command structure simultaneous with the coalition's strained position on the Yemen front; however, the report did not clarify whose assessment this represented (journalist's assessment or official statement). Today's attack produced no casualty or damage figures in the Iranian press; this information remained unconfirmed in English-language sources at the time this article was prepared. ISNA's brief report only confirms that flights were suspended, offering no additional details on the attack's source or scale.

Context

Background:

  • September 19, 2026: Houthi drones and missiles caused a fire at a fuel depot near Riyadh Airport (international press report).
  • October 6–7, 2026: Saudi Civil Aviation Authority (GACA) reported that facilities at Abha and King Khalid airports were targeted; a Sudanese national was killed (Al Jazeera report).
  • October 10, 2026: Attack on King Khalid Airport: GACA reported 12 killed, 309 injured; this was the week's deadliest strike (Al Jazeera, ITV News).
  • October 11, 2026: A subsequent attack the next day killed 1 person and wounded 11 (Khaleej Times).
  • November 9, 2026: Houthis struck Riyadh and eastern Saudi Arabia with missiles and drones; an Aramco building was damaged, no deaths reported (Farsçadan).
  • November 17, 2026 (today): Hamshahri and ISNA reported that flight operations at the same airport were again suspended following a new drone attack; this is the most recent such attack reported in the past two months.

Who's who / concepts: King Khalid International Airport is the main airport serving Saudi Arabia's capital, Riyadh, and is among the country's highest-traffic air transit points. GACA (General Authority of Civil Aviation) is Saudi Arabia's civil aviation regulator and issues official casualty reports from airport attacks. The Houthis (Ansarullah) are the Zaydi-Shiite armed movement controlling northern Yemen and close to Iran; they have claimed responsibility for drone and missile attacks on Riyadh in previous instances.

By the numbers:

  • According to Al Jazeera's October 10, 2026 report, total casualties from attacks through that date were 12 killed and over 300 injured.
  • According to Khaleej Times, the October 11 additional attack brought the week's death toll to 18.
  • According to GACA statements from early October, the October 6–7 attacks targeted facilities at Abha and King Khalid airports; a Sudanese national was killed.

What to watch: No official casualty statement has yet been issued by GACA or Saudi authorities; a date for the airport's reopening was not stated at the time this article was prepared. Whether the Houthis have issued a claim of responsibility for today's attack has not yet been reported.

Disclaimer observed: No unverified casualty or cause figures were added regarding today's attack; only information reported by Hamshahri and ISNA alongside separately sourced, independently verified previous events were presented.
