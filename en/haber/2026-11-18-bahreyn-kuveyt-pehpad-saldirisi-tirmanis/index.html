Explosions in Bahrain and Kuwait: U.S.-Iran escalation cycle intensifies

According to IRNA, alarm sirens sounded in Bahrain and Kuwait; IRGC and U.S. made mutual strike claims as tensions escalate.

By Farsçadan Editors

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

According to IRNA, on the night of November 17–18, alarm sirens sounded repeatedly in Bahrain, with the Interior Ministry instructing citizens to move to the nearest safe area. That same night, multiple explosion sounds were reported in Kuwait and air defense systems were activated. According to IRNA's account, the events occurred during a period of renewed escalation between the U.S. and Iran.

According to a statement by the IRGC released through Kayhan, in response to the U.S.'s "aggressive" drones continuing to violate Iranian airspace, IRGC fighter jets targeted a U.S. radar system at Sheikh Isa Air Base in Bahrain. In the same statement, the IRGC Aerospace Force claimed to have downed another U.S. MQ-1 reconnaissance-attack drone south of Qeshm Island, and claimed this was the "18th MQ-1 destroyed." The IRGC Navy separately claimed that a supertanker (carrying over 2 million barrels of crude oil) and an LPG vessel transiting east of the Strait of Hormuz, with their navigation systems reportedly disabled, hit sea mines and caught fire.

According to Iran International's compilation, in response to the IRGC's strike on the Bahrain radar, U.S. warplanes targeted an IRGC missile launch position on Qeshm Island; these tit-for-tat strikes occurred while diplomatic efforts to end the war continue. According to Hamshahri, an Iraqi media outlet called "Naya" claimed that drones had struck a U.S. base in Bahrain directly and precisely; Arab users shared images of a large fire in northern Kuwait. Hamshahri's report also includes a quote attributed to one of the soldiers reportedly targeted in the strikes: "We were not prepared for air strikes; the commanders had no plan to protect the soldiers." The source and authenticity of this quote has not been independently confirmed. As of the time this report was prepared, no official statement from U.S. or Bahrain-Kuwait authorities was found addressing these claims.

Context

Background:

  • March 2, 2026: IRGC announced striking Ali al-Salem Air Base where U.S. personnel were also stationed (The National).
  • June 3, 2026: Kuwait Airfield struck by missiles and drones; 1 killed and 63 wounded at Kuwait Airport; CENTCOM said the strike "did not achieve its objective" (Khaleej Times, The National).
  • July 18, 2026: In the 18th phase of IRGC's "Nasr-2" operation, Arifjan Base and Ali al-Salem radar facility in Kuwait were allegedly targeted; Kuwait airspace was closed (newsonair.gov.in).
  • November 13, 2026: IRGC announced it struck a U.S. early-warning radar in Bahrain in response to a "hostile air strike" on the town of Sirik in Hormozgan (Farsçadan).
  • November 17–18, 2026 (today): New wave of alarms and explosions in Bahrain and Kuwait; U.S. said to have struck Qeshm in response.

Who is who / concepts: CENTCOM (United States Central Command) is the regional command of the U.S. Department of Defense responsible for the Middle East, Central Asia, and Red Sea region. Sheikh Isa Air Base is a Royal Bahraini Air Force installation where U.S. forces are also stationed.

By the numbers:

  • According to Maritime Executive, 3 U.S. service members were killed in earlier Iranian strikes in Kuwait (2026, specific date not provided).
  • According to Khaleej Times' June 3, 2026 report, that day's strike on Kuwait Airport killed 1 and wounded 63.
  • According to Kayhan's claim, the drone downed today was the "18th MQ-1" destroyed by the IRGC; this figure has not been independently verified.

What's next: According to Iran International, diplomatic contacts aimed at ending the war continue; how today's mutual strike claims will affect ceasefire efforts remains unclear.

Key terms

  • Revolutionary Guards (Sepah, IRGC)The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, a military, political and economic force founded after the 1979 revolution that answers directly to the Supreme Leader.
  • Kayhan (newspaper)A Tehran daily founded in 1943, whose head has been appointed by the Supreme Leader since 1979 and which is described as Iran's most hardline paper.
  • Strait of HormuzThe strait linking the Persian Gulf to the Gulf of Oman, through which about a quarter of the world's seaborne oil trade passes.
  • Iran InternationalA London-based Persian-language TV channel and news site founded in 2017, critical of the Iranian government, which Tehran calls a "terrorist" group.
  • Principlists (Osulgarayan) and reformistsThe two main political camps of the Islamic Republic. Principlists defend the revolution's principles; reformists advocate political opening.
