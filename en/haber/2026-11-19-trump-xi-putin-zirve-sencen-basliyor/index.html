Trump-Xi-Putin trilateral summit in Shenzhen begins; Trump says he doesn't need their help on Iran

Trump, Xi, and Putin to hold trilateral talks in Shenzhen within APEC; Trump says he doesn't need their aid to end Iran war.

By Farsçadan Editors

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

According to Iran International's report from Reuters, U.S. President Donald Trump arrived in Shenzhen, China on Wednesday, November 18 to attend the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) leaders summit and was met at the airport by Xi Jinping. Iran International reports that Xi embraced Trump at Bao'an International Airport and the U.S. national anthem was played; heavy security measures were in place around the airport and extending to the Langham Hotel, with the intersection leading to the hotel closed to vehicle traffic.

According to ISNA's analysis titled "A new Yalta or strategic stalemate?," the trilateral meeting between Trump, Xi, and Putin scheduled for Thursday, November 19 within the APEC framework is taking place during a period when competition between global powers has become an erosive stalemate. Iran International reports that Trump stated he does not need Xi and Putin's help to end the war with Iran; this statement appears to contradict earlier reports (see Background) suggesting Iran war might be on the summit agenda, with Trump indicating he intends to keep the matter separate from trilateral discussions. According to Al Jazeera, the summit's primary focus is expected to be nuclear arms control agreements; the Ukraine war's inclusion on the agenda is assessed as unlikely.

According to Iran International, in the opening APEC speech, Xi called for a halt to commercial protectionism and urged countries to avoid disrupting global production and supply chains, announcing that China will cut tariffs and facilitate market access. In his remarks, Putin described Western powers as "old hegemons" and said Europe is experiencing a "humiliating collapse"; he argued that Western sanctions on Russia may have deterrent effect but cannot halt rival economies' growth or even collapse them.

Iran International reports in a separate story that before departing for China, Trump announced in an official lunch in the Philippines that Washington and Manila had reached a trade agreement, though he provided no details; the Philippine ambassador to Washington stated there was no signed document yet. Trump's journey included meetings with leaders of India, Japan, and Australia; the possibility of a bilateral meeting with North Korea's Kim Jong-un at the APEC margins has been raised, though neither side has confirmed this yet.

Context

Background:

  • November 6, 2026: China's Foreign Ministry for the first time publicly confirmed it was discussing the possibility of a trilateral meeting (Korea Times/AP).
  • November 14, 2026: Farsçadan reported the summit would take place Thursday in Shenzhen, including a White House official's statement that talks would focus primarily on arms control.
  • November 16, 2026: Farsçadan reported that Beijing formally confirmed the summit's date and location.
  • November 18, 2026 (yesterday): Trump arrived in Shenzhen and was met at the airport by Xi.

Key terms / concepts: Shenzhen, located in Guangdong province in southern China just north of Hong Kong, is a technology city known since the 1980s as China's first "Special Economic Zone" and for rapid industrialization; this city should not be confused with Xinjiang (Sinkiang) in northwest China, which appears in some previous reports.

By the numbers:

  • According to Al Jazeera, this will be the first trilateral summit with all three leaders present simultaneously.
  • According to Business Today, the summit takes place on the margins of the November 18-19, 2026 APEC leaders meeting.

What's next: The trilateral meeting is scheduled for Thursday, November 19; according to Al Jazeera, a bilateral Trump-Xi meeting is also expected—the third of this year. Whether the meeting produces concrete results remains uncertain due to the parties' different priorities (nuclear disarmament/Ukraine/Iran).

Disclaimers observed: Assessments of the summit's impact on Iran war were attributed to their sources; no conclusions or intent analysis were offered.

Correction (November 19, 2026): In earlier Farsçadan reports dated November 14 and 16, 2026 on this summit, the city's name was incorrectly listed as "Xinjiang"; the correct name is Shenzhen. Correction notes have been added to those reports.
