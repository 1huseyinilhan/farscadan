Iran strikes back at Vienna: won't return to pre-war inspection terms

Iran's IAEA representative criticized the agency's silence on U.S.-Israeli strikes; U.S. and three European nations demanded inspectors return.

By Farsçadan Editors

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

According to Ettelaat, Iran's ambassador to international organizations in Vienna, Reza Najafi, sharply criticized the International Atomic Energy Agency's (IAEA) Board of Governors in a pointed rebuke, condemning the agency's silence over U.S. and Israeli military strikes against Iran's nuclear facilities under IAEA safeguards. Najafi said the agency's director general's reports about Iran's loss of focus on its nuclear materials and facilities could not be evaluated without acknowledging the U.S.-Israeli strikes that caused it.

According to Ettelaat's report, Najafi noted that the strikes occurred less than 24 hours after the Board approved a U.S. proposal in June 2025 and during ongoing negotiations, creating an "unprecedented" situation in IAEA inspection history; he reminded the audience that the agency had not even formally condemned the strikes. The ambassador stressed that Iran's cooperation and transparency would not revert to pre-strike conditions, that the scope of access and Iran's policy on accepting inspectors would be reviewed, and that Iran could exercise its right to suspend safeguards obligations on security grounds.

According to Ettelaat's account, Najafi compared the current silence to the IAEA's response to Israel's 1981 strike on Iraq's nuclear facility: at that time the Board and General Conference passed roughly 10 condemnatory resolutions; this time, despite strikes that were more extensive and directly targeted operational facilities containing nuclear material, the agency's response was "deadly silence." Najafi said the agency could not remain silent on military strikes while demanding Iranian cooperation and transparency, and that attributing the loss of focus to Iran rather than to the strikes was "placing effect in place of cause, blaming the victim instead of the aggressor."

According to Ettelaat's report, the ambassador noted that Board resolutions adopted after the strikes did not condemn the strikes but instead, ignoring security circumstances, demanded Iranian access to all nuclear sites. He labeled the September 2026 resolution "a clear example of abuse of authority and procedure" and declared it unlawful and illegitimate. Najafi also pointed to the U.S. Treasury's announcement of million-dollar bounties for information on Iran's nuclear scientists, personnel, and facilities, saying that sharing sensitive information in such an environment would do nothing but lay the groundwork for new attacks. He added that Iran would continue to defend its right to peaceful nuclear energy in all its dimensions, including national fuel cycle development, and flatly rejected claims about "reviving" expired UN Security Council resolutions.

In response, Iran International reported that the U.S., UK, France, and Germany issued a joint statement at the same Board meeting calling on Iran to provide information on its nuclear facilities and materials and to resume inspections; the four nations stressed their support for the agency's independent and impartial role and called on Tehran for "urgent cooperation." The statement referenced the September 2026 resolution, which facilitated reporting Iran's non-compliance with safeguards obligations to the UN Security Council and General Assembly; the European Union separately backed the same point. France read the statement on behalf of the four countries at Thursday's session in Vienna.

Context

Background:

  • June 18, 2025: The IAEA Board of Governors approved a U.S. proposal on Iran's safeguards obligations; U.S.-Israeli strikes on Iran's nuclear facilities followed shortly after (according to Ettelaat).
  • November 13, 2026: The IAEA called on Iran to take "step-by-step" measures to restore inspection access; Director General Rafael Grossi noted that strikes had limited cooperation (Al Jazeera).
  • November 15, 2026: Iran ruled out a return to routine inspections under current conditions; Farsçadan covered this same day (Farsçadan).
  • November 19, 2026 (today): Najafi's sharp remarks and the four-nation joint statement occurred sequentially at the same Board meeting.

Key figures and concepts: Safeguards (pâdîman) is the IAEA's system of inspection and reporting to verify that member states' nuclear materials are not diverted to non-peaceful purposes. The Board of Governors comprises 35 member states and makes policy decisions for the agency. Reza Najafi is Iran's ambassador to international organizations in Vienna and Iran's permanent representative to the IAEA Board of Governors.

By the numbers:

  • According to Najafi, 16 consecutive IAEA reports had verified Iran's full compliance with 2015 nuclear agreement (JCPOA) obligations (Ettelaat).
  • Najafi cited agency data showing that inspection activities in Iran increased 152% between 2013 and 2017 (Ettelaat).
  • Najafi noted that after Israel's 1981 strike on Iraq, IAEA bodies passed roughly 10 condemnatory resolutions; this time, no condemnation passed (Ettelaat).
  • The September 2026 Board resolution referred the issue to the UN Security Council and General Assembly (Iran International).

What comes next: According to Al Jazeera, Grossi said inspectors' return depends on "returning to the table," technical work is complete, but talks remain stalled; no new negotiating date has been announced. A day later, Foreign Minister Araghchi repeated in an interview that the debate over changing nuclear doctrine and Iran's red lines on its 60%-enriched uranium stockpile continued (Farsçadan). The legal framework behind terms like safeguards, the Additional Protocol and snapback is covered in detail in Farsçadan's guide to the NPT and the JCPOA.
