Iran tries oil exports through blockade? TankerTrackers claim lacks confirmation

TankerTrackers claimed Iran is preparing to resume crude oil exports despite naval blockade; no official confirmation received.

By Farsçadan Editors

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

According to a report from Donya-ye Eqtesad's "site reading" section, maritime tracking organization TankerTrackers claimed that Iran is preparing to resume crude oil exports despite the naval blockade. The report notes that this claim has not received any response from Iran's official authorities. The same article suggests that in the wake of escalating Tehran-Washington tensions, the idea of Iran resuming oil exports and threatening to strike oil facilities—especially those of the United Arab Emirates—if its export fleet is attacked has gained traction.

According to Iran International, a Telegram channel linked to the Revolutionary Guards called Saberin News posted an article labeled "unofficial," claiming that dozens of Iranian tankers are ready to move toward China. The same report cited a statement by Samir Madani, one of TankerTrackers' founders, to Reuters saying that on Thursday a tanker in the Persian Gulf reportedly received Iranian crude via ship-to-ship (STS) transfer from another vessel; according to Madani, if this tanker breaks through the blockade, it would be Iran's first successful seaborne crude export since late July. Madani declined to name the vessel but said it is not on the U.S. Treasury sanctions list and entered the region without drawing attention; the destination of the cargo was not disclosed.

According to Iran International's report, the Revolutionary Guard-affiliated Fars News Agency also published an article on Thursday calling on the government to send a tanker convoy to China; according to the agency, the possibility that Iran would retaliate against UAE, Saudi, and Qatar oil terminals could deter Washington from firing on the tankers. As Donya-ye Eqtesad notes, this amounts to a string of unconfirmed claims from unofficial sources; Iran's government has yet to make any statement on the matter.

According to UANI (United Against Nuclear Iran), the U.S. Navy blockade as of October 2026 continues to severely restrict Iran's seaborne crude oil exports; the organization reported that Iranian-linked tankers have made attempted ship-to-ship transfers near the Shinas port off Oman, but the secondary vessel's attempts to cross the blockade line have largely failed. According to the same report, some tankers have been anchored for weeks off Sri Lanka and Malaysia; this circumstance raises caution about what scale today's "resumption" claims could realistically achieve. Despite the reports from Donya-ye Eqtesad and Iran International, the focus is on a single tanker's possible passage; thus whether the claim represents an isolated incident or a broader policy shift remains unclear.

Context

Background:

  • Late July 2026: The U.S. Navy tightened the naval blockade; according to UANI reports, Iran's seaborne crude exports have remained near zero since then.
  • November 13, 2026: Reports noted the dollar-toman rate approaching the 300,000-toman threshold and growing land-border blockade effects (Farsçadan).
  • November 19, 2026 (today): TankerTrackers' claim and Saberin News/Fars' "tanker fleet" calls came to light the same day.

Key figures and concepts: TankerTrackers is an independent maritime intelligence firm that uses satellite imagery and vessel tracking data to monitor crude oil tanker movements; it is frequently cited as a source by Reuters and international media. Ship-to-ship transfer (STS) is the loading of cargo from one tanker to another at sea; it is a commonly known method for obscuring the origin of sanctioned cargo.

By the numbers:

  • According to UANI's November 10, 2026 report, Iran continues to load oil on tankers even if crossing the blockade is unsuccessful.
  • If the tanker in question breaks through, Madani said it would be the first successful seaborne crude export since late July (roughly four months), according to Reuters as cited by Iran International.
  • According to UANI reports, some tankers attempting to breach the blockade have remained anchored off Sri Lanka and Malaysia for weeks.

Turkey angle: In an earlier Farsçadan report, citing an TBMM Research Center note, it was reported that roughly 20% of Turkey's crude oil needs come from Gulf countries through the Strait of Hormuz (Farsçadan). Iran's attempts to breach the blockade could indirectly affect security and shipping costs on this route, though today's claim contains no direct data on this dimension.

What comes next: Madani said it remains unclear whether the vessel will breach the blockade; it has not been confirmed whether Iran's government will issue any statement in response.
