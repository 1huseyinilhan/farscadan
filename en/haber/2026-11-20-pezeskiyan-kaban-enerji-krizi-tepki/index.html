Pezeshkian says "I sleep in a coat," gets sharp backlash from struggling citizens

Iran's President Pezeshkian said he sleeps in a coat without heat as an example of energy conservation; struggling citizens attacked the remark on social media.

By Farsçadan Editors

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

According to Iran International, Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian said at the "Pioneers of Salvation" congress held at the University of Tehran on Thursday, November 19, that at home he sleeps in a coat and clothes without turning on any lights or heat. Pezeshkian's exact words were: "I am president, but at home I sleep in a coat and clothes, I don't turn on any lights." According to the report's details, these remarks came during a time of acute natural gas supply crisis, heating problems in schools, and ongoing economic hardship.

According to a separate Iran International report, Pezeshkian's remarks sparked angry responses on social media from viewers—by the president's own account, "Iran International's viewers"—who criticized the government's resource management shortcomings and the passing of crisis costs to ordinary people. The report notes that viewers pointed to Iran's vast natural gas reserves, the energy supply crisis, cold in schools, and economic hardship. Pezeshkian added at the event that he does not live the way people do and said he was willing to show his house to others.

One response Iran International compiled, from a viewer in Mashhad, read: "We sold our coats so that our wives and children could have bread at night." A viewer from Tehran wrote: "You sleep in a coat, meaning you at least have a coat. Do you know how many citizens don't have money for a coat? I wish you had dignity." Another viewer asked: "Do you know the price of a child's coat? Do you know how many families fill their refrigerators so they can sleep on a full stomach?"

According to Iran International, the common thread in these responses was that they pointed to the gap between Pezeshkian's personal example of frugality and the deprivation citizens actually face; audiences read the president's words not as a suggestion but as an admission that laid bare the distance between his living standards and those of ordinary Iranians. According to the report, these responses accumulated during a winter when Iran faces natural gas supply problems, heating shortages in schools, and widespread economic hardship; Pezeshkian's remark that "I do not live the way you do" drew an explicit line between his living conditions and those of the population.

How the Persian press covered it

This report relies on a single source (Iran International); because no other media outlet covered the same event in today's flow, a comparative framing table was not compiled. Iran International's opposition/diaspora orientation should be kept in mind; the scope of citizen responses has not been independently verified.

Context

Background:

  • October 2025: Pezeshkian remarked that "we sleep on gold but the people go hungry," effectively acknowledging that his administration's economic policies had contributed to inflation; at that time, a Revolutionary Guard-linked newspaper called on him to shift focus from "hunger" to inequality. At the same time, two senior government officials called on citizens and civil servants to avoid public criticism of the government, characterizing the current situation as a "state of war" (according to Iran International's English archives).
  • May 2026: Pezeshkian urged citizens to reduce consumption of water and electricity, saying difficult economic conditions would persist after the war.
  • November 16, 2026: Farsçadan reported that the head of the Plan and Budget Organization said equal cash payments were not the right approach for the kalabarg (food e-coupon) system (Farsçadan).
  • November 19, 2026 (today): Pezeshkian's "coat" remarks and the resulting backlash emerged the same day.

Key figures and concepts: Masoud Pezeshkian has been Iran's president since 2024; he is a cardiac surgeon by training and a politician. The "Pioneers of Salvation" is a congress/event held at the University of Tehran mentioned in the report.

Why the coat became a symbol: In the audience responses cited by Iran International, the word "coat" transformed into a reference point that symbolized both the cost of winter heating and access to basic food. In the messages Iran International compiled, "coat" appears in three different interpretations: one viewer from Mashhad said he had to sell his coat to buy bread, one from Tehran said having a coat was itself a privilege, and a third pointed to the disproportion between the price of a child's coat and household food budgets.

What comes next: No response from the government or Pezeshkian's office to these reactions has emerged; it remains unverified whether any statement is expected.
