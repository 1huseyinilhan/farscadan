U.S. Treasury's dual move: Singapore-Malaysia warning and sanctions on 4 Iranians

U.S. Treasury warns Singapore and Malaysia about Iran-linked shell companies; sanctions 4 Iranians including football federation head's brother.

By Farsçadan Editors

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

According to Iran International, citing Reuters, U.S. Treasury officials met this week with government authorities in Singapore and Malaysia, including regulatory and national security institutions in both countries. The officials warned banks and law firms in these nations about the risks of doing business with Iranian shell companies. On the same day, Treasury added four Iranian nationals to the sanctions list, including Majid Taj, brother of Iran's football federation head, citing oil sales or related support.

According to an unnamed Treasury official cited by Reuters, Washington wants tighter oversight of "high-risk" sectors such as maritime shipping, aviation services, and supply networks; the aim is to prevent these channels from being misused as proxies for the Iranian government. The official told Reuters, "We will target these vital arteries, and when we identify a vital artery, this is not a negotiation." The report notes that oil brokers have long used Malaysian, Singaporean, and Indonesian waters, stopping to transfer cargo between vessels to obscure the oil's origin; Malaysia tightened controls on these transfers last year and detained two tankers along with their cargo. Reuters verified records showing that Iran Airtour has conducted regular flights on the Tehran-Kuala Lumpur route through at least November 14.

In a second action announced the same day, Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) added, alongside Majid Taj, Mohammad Javad Bavand (former Iranian Revolutionary Guard Intelligence Organization official), Mohammad Hossein Tharwati (son of IRGC Brigadier General Mohsen Tharwati), and Mojtaba Arabnejad (son of sanctioned Mahan Air CEO Hamid Arabnejad) to the list. According to Iran International, Treasury stated that Bavand manages efforts to recover oil proceeds in third countries and has delegated this work to trusted individuals like Taj; the designations fall under Executive Orders 13902 (petroleum sector) and 13224 (counterterrorism).

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent stated that no Iranian oil cargo has been loaded onto vessels since August 24 under the "Operation Economic Outcast" campaign, and no shipment outside the blockade has been left behind; Tehran may never recover the revenue from its previous sales. State Department Spokesperson Tommy Pigott added separately that the four individuals financed Iran's armed forces through illegal oil sales and that Mahan Air was used by the Quds Force for transporting weapons and personnel.

According to OFAC's statement, all U.S.-based assets of these four individuals have been frozen; because they are under secondary sanctions, banks and individuals conducting certain types of transactions with them also face sanctions risk. While Qatar's Prime Minister Muhammad bin Abdulrahman Al Thani pursues mediation efforts, Trump stated at a press conference the same day that Tehran wants to reach a deal with Washington but refuses what the U.S. considers "acceptable" terms.

Context

Background: The U.S. targeting Iranian sanctions-evasion networks is not new.

  • August 24, 2026: Secretary Bessent announced from the White House the launch of "Operation Economic Outcast" campaign.
  • September 8, 2026: Treasury added dozens of companies based in Turkey, UAE, Malaysia, Kazakhstan, and the United Kingdom to the sanctions list for allegedly supporting Mahan Air.
  • October 16, 2026: Treasury sanctioned 16 individuals and 10+ institutions targeting Iran's foreign exchange and oil revenue transfer networks.
  • November 20, 2026: Singapore-Malaysia warning and new four-person sanctions wave.

Who and what: OFAC (Office of Foreign Assets Control) — a Treasury Department unit that prepares and enforces sanctions lists. Shell company — an entity that appears on paper to conduct ordinary business but actually operates on behalf of a sanctioned person or institution.

By the numbers: OFAC added 4 individuals to the sanctions list in this round (Iran International, November 21, 2026). Treasury's separate aviation sanctions in September covered dozens of companies across five countries, including Turkey (U.S. Department of the Treasury, September 8, 2026). Iran Airtour's Tehran-Kuala Lumpur flights were confirmed by Reuters flight records to continue through at least November 14, 2026.

Turkey dimension: On September 8, 2026, the U.S. Treasury sanctioned companies based in Turkey — along with those in UAE, Malaysia, Kazakhstan, and the UK — for allegedly supporting sanctioned Mahan Air in its aviation supply chain; one firm was identified as a general sales agent coordinating U.S.-origin parts shipments for Mahan Air (U.S. Department of the Treasury). Today's Singapore-Malaysia warning represents a continuation of that aviation-focused earlier wave.

What's next: According to Treasury's statement, the campaign continues; new sanctions will be added as officials identify what they term "vital arteries." Reuters reported that the Malaysian government did not respond to a request for comment, while Singapore stated it remains "committed" to preventing institutional abuse.
