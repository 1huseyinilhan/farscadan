Araghchi: Strait of Hormuz reopening requires 7 conditions, 'neither war nor peace' must end

Foreign Minister Araghchi says 7 conditions apply to Strait of Hormuz reopening; states 60% enriched uranium will not leave Iran.

By Farsçadan Editors

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi told Hamshahri newspaper that the 7 conditions previously outlined for reopening the Strait of Hormuz remain in effect. Araghchi stated that the June 2026 Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding is no longer "on the table," that 60% enriched uranium will not leave the country, and that "neither war nor peace" conditions must end.

According to the full interview published by Hamshahri, Araghchi explained that Tehran conveyed first a 7-day plan and then a 2-day plan to Washington via Qatar: if the U.S. fulfilled its commitments on the first day (lifting the naval blockade, suspending oil sales sanctions, etc.), Iran would open Hormuz on day two and nuclear negotiations would begin. Araghchi said the offer was conveyed before the U.S. elections but Washington has yet to respond, and Trump has recently lost interest in a deal. He characterized allegations against Iran — such as "chemical bomb attacks on Washington" or "missiles hidden beneath dams" — as "baseless and ridiculous," though he noted that some political leaders, not American public opinion, take them seriously. As an example, he cited Treasury Secretary Bessent moving his residence to a military base — this claim remains unconfirmed.

On the 400-kilogram stockpile of 60% enriched uranium, Araghchi defined two red lines: the material will not be exported and will be used according to Iran's own defined needs. The final decision will be made by the Supreme National Security Council's negotiations committee and approved by the council. The minister noted that nuclear doctrine revision discussions are ongoing domestically, that attacks on facilities strengthen arguments of those favoring doctrine change, but such a change would carry "significant costs." He added that Russia and China oppose doctrine revision, but Iran's official position has not yet changed.

Context

Background:

  • June 2026: Iran and the U.S. agreed on the "Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding" via Qatar; both presidents signed the text.
  • Early October 2026 (Mehr month): During a New York visit, Araghchi conveyed a 7-day Hormuz plan to Washington via Qatar; with elections approaching, the timeline was shortened to 2 days.
  • October 4, 2026: Reuters reported that Iran will not reopen Hormuz without 7 conditions being met (see Iran oil export blockade claim).
  • November 19-20, 2026: While tanker allegations and IAEA Vienna inspection disputes persist (Hormuz tanker unconfirmed claim, IAEA Vienna inspection dispute), Araghchi reiterated that conditions have not changed.

Who and what: Abbas Araghchi is Iran's senior diplomat and Foreign Minister; chief negotiator in nuclear talks. "Neither war nor peace" (na jang, na solh) describes the interim state in Iranian media where the war has not formally ended but active fighting is not ongoing.

By the numbers:

  • 400 kg: The quantity of 60% enriched uranium Araghchi says Iran wants to keep (Hamshahri, November 21, 2026).
  • 7 conditions / 2 days: The number of conditions for reopening Hormuz and the final proposed implementation timeline; according to Reuters, conditions include lifting the naval blockade, suspending oil sales sanctions, and a ceasefire covering Lebanon (The National/Reuters, October 4, 2026).

Turkey dimension: The Strait of Hormuz blockade directly affects Turkey's energy import costs. According to an Ember and bne IntelliNews assessment from June 2026, the crisis could add a $14 billion burden to Turkey by year-end 2026; approximately $7.7 billion from oil price increases and $6.4 billion from natural gas import cost increases (Turkey's annual energy import bill under the baseline scenario is around $60-65 billion).

What's next: Araghchi said they are ready for expert-level talks if concrete proposals come from the U.S. regarding how the 7 conditions would be implemented; no new scheduled session has been announced beyond this.

Key terms

  • Principlists (Osulgarayan) and reformistsThe two main political camps of the Islamic Republic. Principlists defend the revolution's principles; reformists advocate political opening.
