Two-hour power blackout in Tehran: Officials blame 'grid maintenance'

Tehran Electric Distribution announced 2-hour blackout for November 21; officials attribute it to grid maintenance as winter energy crisis deepens.

By Farsçadan Editors

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

Tehran's Major Electric Distribution Company notified some subscribers via text that a two-hour power blackout is planned for November 21 (Aban 30). According to Iran International, Ahsan Zabihzade, spokesman for the electricity sector, attributed the blackout to "grid maintenance" and said planned blackouts could continue in coming weeks.

According to Iranian press reports, the company sent messages titled "Energy Management" asking subscribers to check the "Berk-e Men" (My Electricity) system to find their blackout times and restoration. Some areas of the capital, such as the Farabi electricity district and Hazine neighborhood, were notified of their November 21 blackout and reconnection times. Zabihzade stated that the messages concern grid maintenance work, that the final schedule for planned blackouts is not yet ready but could begin in coming weeks. The spokesman said that thermal power plants in Iran generate approximately 70% of annual electricity using natural gas, but attacks on South Pars gas fields in late March 2026 reduced gas production capacity, complicating this winter's fuel supply. He said that last week, as temperatures dropped, the plants did not receive adequate gas, forcing them to switch to liquid fuels, and that significant portions of winter fuel reserves had been depleted. Mustafa Receb-i Meshedi, deputy minister of electricity and energy, stated on November 14 that the country has lost 130 million cubic meters per day of gas production capacity "due to war" and that this winter's fuel supply will be harder than in previous years.

Tehran's temporary Friday prayer leader Muhammad Javad Hajjali stated on November 20 that conservation is the main way out of the crisis: "If the people take seriously conservation in gas, electricity, gasoline, and bread, by God's grace we can overcome this livelihood crisis." A day earlier, President Masoud Pezeshkian said he sleeps in a coat in his home and uses no lights or heaters (our earlier report). Iran International readers responded harshly to these remarks, pointing to Iran's vast gas reserves, speaking of cold classrooms and families bearing hardship, and criticizing the government for passing the bill for resource mismanagement to ordinary people. Iran International reported that while officials ask people to conserve, they have not fundamentally resolved structural and administrative problems in electricity, gas, and water sectors; temporary school and office closures had been used as stopgap measures in prior periods.

Context

Background:

  • March 2026 (late Esfand 1404): Attacks on South Pars gas fields reduced production capacity.
  • November 14, 2026 (Aban 23): Energy Ministry deputy Receb-i Meshedi disclosed the war-related loss of 130 million m³ daily gas production.
  • November 20, 2026: President Pezeshkian's "sleeping in a coat" statement — our earlier report.
  • November 21, 2026 (Aban 30): Tehran announces two-hour blackout.

Who and what: Berk-e Men (برق من, "My Electricity") — the online system through which Tehran's Major Electric Distribution Company notifies subscribers of blackout schedules. Khomushi (خاموشی, blackout) is the general Persian term used in media to describe planned or emergency power outages.

By the numbers:

  • Approximately 70% of Iran's annual electricity is generated from thermal plants using natural gas (Ahsan Zabihzade, Iran International, November 20, 2026).
  • War-related loss: 130 million m³ per day of gas production capacity (Mustafa Receb-i Meshedi, November 14, 2026).
  • According to Al Jazeera's November 16, 2026 report, the government expects a daily gap of approximately 430 million m³ at peak winter demand; 11 of 31 provinces (especially Mazandaran, Golestan, North Khorasan) fall in high-risk category.

Turkey dimension: Iran supplied approximately 13% of Turkey's natural gas imports last year; a long-term contract providing up to 9.6 billion m³ annually ended in July 2026. After attacks on South Pars in March 2026, Iran suspended shipments to Turkey for a period. Turkey's Energy Minister Alparslan Bayraktar said in September 2026 that storage facilities were full for winter and no supply security issues were foreseen; however, Iran's announced domestic demand gap this winter now raises questions about whether the contract will be renewed.

What's next: Electricity sector spokesman Zabihzade said the final schedule for planned blackouts is not yet ready and will be announced in coming weeks.
