Abu Dhabi's ADNOC bypasses Hormuz blockade with U.S. support, oil flow halved

WSJ: ADNOC carries oil from Hormuz with U.S. military support; FT reports flow dropped 50% in second week of November.

By Farsçadan Editors

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

According to the Wall Street Journal, the Abu Dhabi National Oil Company (ADNOC) continues to move both its own and Iraq's crude oil through the Strait of Hormuz with U.S. military support, despite over 20 of its tankers being targeted since April. On the same day, Financial Times reported, citing Kpler data, that oil flow through the strait dropped 50% in the second week of November compared to the first week.

Iran International, citing the WSJ report, notes that ADNOC tankers transfer their cargoes to other vessels after passing through Hormuz and return for reloading; this allows private companies reluctant to navigate the strait due to attack risk and insurance difficulties to take delivery south of it. ADNOC-operated vessels came under attack Tuesday night, with 11 crew members wounded and 1 missing; company management characterized these attacks as "a warning from the Iranian government." According to HawkEye data, UAE-linked crude and condensate exports fell from 2 million barrels daily pre-war to 11,300 barrels in March, then rose to 2.1 million by mid-year; in October, total exports including pipelines reached 3.74 million barrels daily (pre-war roughly 3 million). Iraq sustains exports from Persian Gulf terminals by offering discounts to buyers unable to access them; ADNOC transported 20 million barrels of Iraqi crude in August and another 14 million by mid-September. According to Financial Times, Rystad Energy's Jorge León states that average 1-1.5 ships come under attack daily in the strait, with many incidents unreported; small damage repairs take less than a week, while bridge or engine damage can take far longer. León says that at this rate, serious tanker supply problems may emerge and the issue could reach broader scale within 2-3 months — this is León's assessment, not a confirmed development. The International Maritime Organization (IMO) reported 138 vessel attacks in the Middle East region between March 1-November 17, 2026, with 25 seafarers killed. Iran announced it had blacklisted ADNOC-operated vessels for allegedly violating transit rules.

Context

Background:

  • March 2026: UAE-linked crude exports fell to 11,300 barrels daily (HawkEye data).
  • August-September 2026: ADNOC transported 34 million barrels of Iraqi crude unable to use Persian Gulf terminals.
  • November 19-20, 2026: Consecutive tanker incidents reported in Hormuz (see Iran oil export blockade claim).
  • Second week of November 2026: Strait flow halved compared to first week (Kpler/Financial Times).

Who/concept: ADNOC (Abu Dhabi National Oil Company) is the UAE's state oil firm and ranks among the world's top 12 oil producers. Kpler and HawkEye are independent maritime analytics firms tracking tanker movements via satellite data; energy market analysts and news outlets regularly use this data as source material.

By the numbers:

  • 1.42 billion dollars: Net profit ADNOC earned from tankers (first 9 months of year); prior year same period was 122 million (WSJ).
  • 3.74 million barrels/day: ADNOC's total crude exports in October 2026 including pipelines; pre-war level roughly 3 million barrels/day (HawkEye).

What's next: ADNOC executives reportedly consulted with U.S. officials on leasing permits for sanctioned tankers; WSJ reported no permits had been announced when the article appeared.
