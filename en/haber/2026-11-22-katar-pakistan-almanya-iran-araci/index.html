Qatar mediation continues: delegation in Tehran, Pakistan and Germany-Qatar joint statement

A Qatari delegation visited Tehran; Pakistan's FM hopeful on deal; Qatar-Germany issued joint statement on Iran.

By Farsçadan Editors

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

Deputy Foreign Minister and Spokesman Kazim Gharibabadi confirmed that a Qatari delegation arrived in Tehran Friday evening, met with Iranian officials, and returned to Doha. The same day, Pakistan's Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar said he is "by nature optimistic" about an Iran-U.S. agreement, while Qatar and Germany issued a joint statement calling on Iran not to pursue nuclear weapons.

According to Ettelaat's report, Gharibabadi told reporters "we are in virtual and in-person contact with mediators, including the Qataris; we are evaluating whether diplomacy can be given another chance." The deputy minister termed the naval blockade a "warfare act" and said Iran will take "every necessary step" to break it; when asked whether certain oil tankers heading to China were part of this, he did not directly answer. Gharibabadi stated that the diplomatic plan Iran conveyed to Oman's Sultan was identical to the previously public "2-day" Hormuz plan and that no different offer was on the table. Pakistan's Foreign Minister and Deputy Prime Minister Ishaq Dar, in an interview with Euronews broadcast by PTV News, said "by nature I am an optimist; sooner or later, hopefully in the shortest time, I believe we will reach this goal," and warned that the world cannot sustain a prolonged conflict in West Asia. The joint Qatar-Germany statement called for Iran not to develop nuclear weapons alongside demands that Houthis cease attacks on Saudi Arabia and that Iran cut support to them; the statement reaffirmed commitment to Yemen's unity and sovereignty but did not separately condemn military attacks on Iran — Khabaronline criticized this asymmetry as a "Western-Israeli-axis one-sided stance."

Context

Background:

  • June 2026: Iran and U.S. agreed on "Islamabad Accord Memorandum" through Qatar mediation.
  • October-November 2026: Araghchi proposed a 7-condition plan for opening Hormuz; U.S. did not respond (see Araghchi: 7 conditions for Hormuz).
  • November 21 evening, 2026: Qatari delegation in Tehran for approximately two hours of talks, departed same night for Doha.

Who/concept: Kazim Gharibabadi is Iran's Deputy Foreign Minister for International and Legal Affairs and spokesman; previously headed Iran's Permanent Mission to the IAEA. The phrase "breaking the blockade" (شکستن محاصره / shekestan-e mohasere) is used in Iranian press to describe efforts to break the U.S. naval blockade. Ishaq Dar is Pakistan's Foreign Minister and Deputy Prime Minister; played a mediation role in the June 2026 Islamabad accord.

By the numbers:

  • March 11, 2026: UN Security Council passed Resolution 2817 condemning Iran's Gulf attacks; passed 13-0 with Russia and China abstaining (Security Council Report).
  • ~2 hours: Duration of Qatari delegation's talks in Tehran (Gharibabadi's press conference, November 21, 2026).

What's next: Gharibabadi said mediation channels continue but announced no new meeting date. U.S. President Trump, in a Fox News interview, said he wants a "permanent, nuclear weapons-preventing" deal with Iran but was in no hurry to decide on further military action; this is Trump's assessment and was not confirmed by Iran. Trump claimed Iranian leadership is the "third group" negotiating with him, "probably more rational but very hard to negotiate with" and "agrees one day and breaks the deal the next" — also Trump's assessment, unanswered by Tehran.
