Session canceled in Iranian parliament: tablet malfunction or brawl?

Iranian parliament's Sunday session was canceled; Kayhan blamed tablet malfunction, one deputy blamed rocking and physical fighting.

By Farsçadan Editors

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

Iran's parliament did not hold its Sunday session (November 22). According to Kayhan's report, the session was canceled due to Tehran deputy Hamid Resaei's protest over his voting tablet not being activated; however, other sources describe the incident differently.

According to Kayhan, before the session began, a group of deputies protested Resaei's tablet not functioning and threatened to disrupt the session if the problem was not resolved. According to Iran International's account, the news site Asr-e Iran confirmed the same reason: Resaei, Mehdi Kuçekzade, Ali Hazriyan, and Hamed Yezdiyan protested Resaei's tablet not activating. Resaei had been sentenced to imprisonment by a private plaintiff and has been on leave since last week.

Tehran deputy Mucteba Zarei, however, offered a different account on his X account. According to Iran International's account of Zarei's post, he wrote that those who canceled the session were the "notorious Shiryan group" and their leader's and supporters' "thuggery" and physical fighting; in his view, the issue was not a tablet malfunction but this group's conduct. According to Ettelaat's account, Ali Keshvari, a member of the Constitution's Article 90 Commission, said he did not view the incident as an "ordinary political disagreement"; the conduct of the four deputies disrupted the natural flow of the session, and he called for the Deputies' Conduct Oversight Board to investigate.

How the Persian press covered it

Source (orientation)Headline / framing
Kayhan (Hardline)Links the session cancellation solely to tablet malfunction protests, presenting deputies' demand as a legitimate procedural concern.
Ettelaat (Established daily, moderate-traditionalist)Through Keshvari's words says "not an ordinary political disagreement," frames the incident as a more serious disciplinary problem, without naming names.
Iran International (Opposition, diaspora)Bases itself on Zarei's X post; reports harsh language accusing a specific group of "thuggery and physical fighting."

The three sources describe the same session's cancellation differently: tablet malfunction, the "conduct" of four unnamed deputies, and a group's "thuggery." Which account fully reflects the facts remains unconfirmed.

Context

Background:

  • Hamid Resaei's situation: A Tehran deputy convicted in a case brought by a private plaintiff; he has been on leave since last week (Iran International, November 22, 2026).
  • November 19, 2026: Farsçadan published a guide examining polarization in the Iranian press and how outlets reflect these camps (see Iranian press map).
  • November 20, 2026: Farsçadan published a guide explaining parliament's role in Iran's decision-making mechanisms (see Who decides in Iran?).

Who and what: The Islamic Consultative Assembly (also known as Baharistan) is Iran's unicameral legislative body, composed of 290 deputies, and sessions are conducted using digital voting tablets. "Lât-bâzî" (لات‌بازی) is a term used in the Persian press to mean use of crude force or thuggery.

By the numbers:

  • 4 deputies: According to Kayhan and Iran International, the number of deputies named in connection with the tablet issue or the incident: Resaei, Kuçekzade, Hazriyan, Yezdiyan (Iran International, November 22, 2026).
  • 290 members: Total membership of the Iranian parliament (parliament official website).

What's next: According to Ettelaat, Keshvari called for the Deputies' Conduct Oversight Board to examine documents and clarify the incident's scope, and for the parliamentary presidium to announce measures to prevent recurrence; an investigation date has not yet been announced.
