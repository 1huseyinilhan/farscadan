U.S. Treasury sanctions Iran shadow banking: 8 individuals, 22 companies targeted

Treasury designates 8 Iranians and 22 companies under Operation Economic Outcast; FinCEN identifies $14.7 billion in suspicious transactions.

By Farsçadan Editors

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

According to Iran International, the U.S. Treasury Department on Monday, November 23, added eight Iranian nationals and 22 companies tied to Iran's shadow banking network to its sanctions list under Operation Economic Outcast. The Treasury also announced that its Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) had identified approximately $14.7 billion in suspicious financial flows linked to this shadow network.

According to the Treasury's Foreign Assets Control Office (OFAC) official list, the sanctioned entities are located in Iran (4), the UAE (16), Turkey (1), and Hong Kong (1). Eight individuals were designated for acting on behalf of Bank Sarmayeh representatives, the company "Rahbar Bazargani Nesim Baharan Qeshm," and foreign exchange bureaus called "Mina Fashki and Partners Currency Exchange" and "Mellat Currency Exchange": Sayyed Mohammad Jalali, Valiollah Hemmati, Ali Kalhor, Mina Fashki, Farzad Fashki, Mahmoud Akhlaghi, Parviz Fazlju, and Mohammad Hossein Abbasi. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said, "Iran's economy is drowning and its leaders are in panic; shadow banking is the dying regime's last lifeline," and vowed that the Treasury will continue to dismantle these networks "until we cut off the head of the snake."

Among eight UAE-based companies listed are Grand Target, Recaz Star, Meethale Raw Materials, Hemraj Trading, Ewan Al Madeena, OGA Global, Dexterous Global (Zana International), and Khore Al Hilal. Treasury noted that Khore Al Hilal had funneled millions of dollars from the sanctioned Opal and Omid Sepah exchange bureaus to Bank Sepah. Turkey-based "Bogazici Gumrukleme" and Hong Kong-based "Grandez Trading" were also designated; the Treasury alleges Grandez served as a front for the sanctioned "Mohsen Khandan and Partners" exchange bureau. OFAC added the Iran Currency and Gold Exchange Center to the list, which the Treasury says annually intermediates tens of billions of dollars in trade between exporters and importers; the Treasury stated that the Central Bank uses this center's mechanisms to facilitate trade in rubles and yuan without using dollars. The Treasury warned that companies violating these sanctions risk losing access to the U.S. financial system and face secondary sanctions.

Context

Background: Since August 2026, the Treasury has been rolling out successive sanctions waves under Operation Economic Outcast, targeting Iran's hard currency and oil revenues.

  • November 21, 2026: Treasury sanctioned four Iranians while warning Singapore and Malaysia against shell companies (Dual Treasury move).
  • October 15, 2026: Treasury announced a separate sanctions round targeting Iraq-based banks and shadow banking networks.
  • November 23, 2026: The new wave of 8 individuals and 22 companies was announced, the subject of this report.

Who is who / concepts: "Shadow banking" refers to transferring money for a sanctioned country through currency exchanges, trade shell companies, and third-country intermediaries instead of official banking systems. By issuing this statement, OFAC warned of "secondary sanctions," meaning foreign companies could also lose U.S. financial access. OFAC (Office of Foreign Assets Control), attached to the Treasury, prepares and enforces sanctions lists. FinCEN (Financial Crimes Enforcement Network) is a separate Treasury unit that tracks money laundering and suspicious financial activity.

By the numbers: According to OFAC's official registry (ofac.treasury.gov/recent-actions/20261123), sanctioned entities are distributed as: Iran (4), UAE (16), Turkey (1), and Hong Kong (1)—matching Iran International exactly. FinCEN identified suspicious transactions totaling $14.7 billion (U.S. Treasury Department, November 23, 2026).

What's next: The Treasury stated the operation will continue "with European allies and regional partners," but no specific timeline for new sanctions waves was given. Earlier Treasury announcements suggest similar sanctions rounds may occur weekly; this report contained no new timeline commitment.
