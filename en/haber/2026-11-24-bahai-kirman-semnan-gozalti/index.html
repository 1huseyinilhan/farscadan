Three Bahai citizens detained in Kerman; one in Semnan called in for interrogation twice

Intelligence Ministry officials detained three Bahai citizens in Kerman; a Bahai in Semnan was called in for questioning on consecutive days.

By Farsçadan Editors

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

According to Iran International citing a source, Intelligence Ministry officials detained three Bahai citizens in Kerman—Kimia Mostafavi, Ruha Imani, and Human Imani—and also summoned Lida Pirasteh, a Bahai from Semnan, for questioning on consecutive days. According to the source, Mostafavi and Ruha Imani were detained on the evening of Sunday, November 22, and Human Imani on the morning of Monday, November 23. After searching their homes and seizing personal belongings, officials transferred the three to an undisclosed location. As of the time this report was prepared, no information was available about the status or whereabouts of those detained.

According to Iran International, Pirasteh was questioned by Intelligence Ministry officials on Sunday, November 22, and called to the Semnan Intelligence Office again on Monday, November 23. Intelligence officials had previously, on November 15, stopped Pirasteh's car on the street and taken her home; after a house search, they confiscated books related to the Bahai faith, laptops, and phones belonging to Pirasteh and her child.

No official statement has been issued regarding these detentions; Iranian authorities had not commented on the matter as of this report's preparation. The story rests on a single source—an anonymous contact speaking with Iran International's correspondent—and no independent verification was available at the time of writing. Iran International typically reports similar detention cases by speaking directly with Bahai families or lawyers, or via notifications from diaspora Bahai organizations; this report did not specify the source's identity or connection to the detainees.

No formal charges against the three detained or Pirasteh were mentioned in this report. Iranian law does not explicitly list Bahai membership as a crime, but courts have previously tried Bahai defendants under general charges such as "propaganda against national security" or "espionage." Whether such accusations were made against those detained here is unknown; accordingly, these potential charges were not stated as fact.

According to a Human Rights Watch report from December 2025, Iran's judiciary plays a "leading role" in persecution of Bahais, with courts often convicting them through general criminal statutes based on religious activities. In April 2025, the European Union imposed sanctions on parts of Iran's judiciary and certain judges and prosecutors for human rights violations against minorities including Bahais.

Context

Background: Human rights organizations report that detentions of Bahai citizens have increased following January 2026 protests and the war that began in February 2026.

  • January 2026: The Center for Human Rights in Iran (CHRI) reported waves of raids and detentions on Bahai homes in multiple cities following the protests.
  • December 2025: Human Rights Watch released a report stating that Iran's judiciary played a leading role in persecution of Bahais.
  • November 15, 2026: Intelligence officials searched Lida Pirasteh's home in Semnan.
  • November 22-23, 2026: Three individuals were detained in Kerman; Pirasteh was called in again.

Who is who / concepts: Bahaism (Bahaiyyat) is an independent religion that emerged in 19th-century Iran and is considered heretical by Shiite clergy. Iran's constitution does not recognize Bahaism among recognized religions, and practitioners' open practice of their faith has no legal protection. The "Vezarat-i Ettilaat" (Ministry of Intelligence) is the body conducting these detentions and interrogations; it is responsible for internal security and intelligence operations.

By the numbers: According to AP reporting via WTOP, in a country of over 90 million people, Iran is home to approximately 300,000 Bahais (WTOP, July 2026). Human rights organizations report that since January 2026 protests and the ensuing war, dozens of Bahai citizens have been detained or arrested in cities like Shiraz, Kerman, Mashhad, and Yazd (CHRI, cited by WTOP, July 2026).

What's next: The whereabouts and any potential charges against the three detained were not disclosed as of this report's preparation; the status of Pirasteh's investigation was also unspecified. Bahai families and diaspora organizations typically report that information flow is limited in the first days of such detentions, with details emerging days later; updates on this case may therefore become available in coming days.
