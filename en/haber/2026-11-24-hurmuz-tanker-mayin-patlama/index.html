Three tankers hit mines in Strait of Hormuz, burst into flames; stern warning from IRGC Navy

IRGC Navy announced three tankers struck naval mines and caught fire in Strait of Hormuz; U.S. air support failed to prevent the incident.

By Farsçadan Editors

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

According to Hamshahri, Iran's Revolutionary Guards Navy announced that three tankers in Strait of Hormuz waters struck naval mines and caught fire severely. In a statement from the IRGC Navy, it said that in recent hours, three tankers traveling in "unauthorized routes" hit mines and burst into flames following the explosions.

The statement claimed that one tanker off the southern coast and two others in the eastern strait struck mines "while under intense U.S. military air support and escort." The IRGC Navy stated that U.S. air escort proved unable to prevent the events and announced that images of the explosion and fire would be released "in coming hours." The statement, addressed to ship owners, captains, and maritime companies, said: "The Strait of Hormuz is not a place where unauthorized routes are attempted or maritime warnings are ignored." It emphasized that U.S. military escort cannot replace compliance with the instructions of the IRGC Navy's traffic control center and maritime regulations. The statement concluded by warning that unauthorized transit and ignoring warnings would be counted as "violations" and "collusion with hostile forces," and would be met with "resolute response." The IRGC Navy did not specify who laid the mines; as of the time this report was prepared, no independent maritime monitoring organization or third party had confirmed the incident.

All four sources drew from the same IRGC Navy statement, so no contradictions exist; differences appear only in which sentences were highlighted. No independent source outside the IRGC confirmed the incident, so the source of the mines and exact damage toll remain unverified.

Donya-ye Eghtesad also shared a map showing the incident location and recalled that U.S.-backed maritime traffic continues and that strait flow has already declined compared to previous weeks. No source disclosed, as of this report's preparation, the names, flags, or crew status of the tankers.

Context

Background: The Strait of Hormuz has regularly seen tensions since the U.S. and Israel launched military operations against Iran in February 2026.

  • November 19, 2026: An unidentified object reportedly struck a tanker off Oman's coast (New incident in Hormuz).
  • November 22, 2026: The Wall Street Journal and Financial Times reported that Abu Dhabi's ADNOC company continues transporting oil through the strait with U.S. backing, while overall strait flow dropped by half in the second week (ADNOC ships oil from Hormuz).
  • November 24, 2026: The IRGC Navy announced that three tankers struck mines and caught fire.

Who is who / concepts: "IRGC Navy" (Niru-ye Daryayi-ye Sepah), the naval branch of the Revolutionary Guards, claims to operate its own "traffic control center" in the Strait of Hormuz and sets passage rules through it. This center is distinct from and separate from the international UKMTO (United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations) warning system.

By the numbers: According to Reuters reporting tracked by satellite data, Iran's crude oil exports fell to near-zero levels over recent months as trackable by satellite (Iran International, November 23, 2026). The Financial Times reported that total oil flow through the strait dropped by half comparing the second week of November to the first (see November 22 report). Brent crude dipped below $89 on November 23 for the first time since August 28 (Hamshahri).

What's next: The IRGC Navy promised to release images of the explosion and fire "within coming hours." Tehran has tied the strait's full reopening to "an end to hostilities, lifting the blockade, and cancellation of sanctions"; no timeline has been set for when these conditions might be met.
