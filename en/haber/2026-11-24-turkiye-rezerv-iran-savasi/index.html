Central Bank chief: Our reserves eroded by $51 billion since Iran war began

Central Bank chief Karahan announced gross reserves fell from $210 billion to $159 billion since war began—a $51 billion decline.

By Farsçadan Editors

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

According to Hamshahri, Turkey's Central Bank (CBRT) Governor Fatih Karahan announced that since the U.S. and Israel launched war against Iran, Turkey has experienced a $51 billion decline in gross international reserves. According to Karahan, combined gold and forex reserves fell from $210 billion at the war's start to $159 billion by November 13; approximately $32 billion of this decline stems from losses in the value of gold reserves.

The statement carried by Hamshahri contained no other details from Karahan about the causes of reserve erosion or measures the CBRT took in response; the report was brief financial news and rested on a single source—the CBRT governor's statement. At the time this report was prepared, the $51 billion figure was not directly cross-checked against CBRT's official weekly data bulletin. Separate estimates in English financial media with different dates provide similar trends but different magnitudes, so the exact total was not independently confirmed.

Hamshahri's report is part of a series of stories in Iran's official or semi-official press on the war's economic costs to the region and world; such reports typically support Tehran's framing that "the enemy also paid a heavy price." Hamshahri's report contained no explicit commentary directed toward that frame; it merely summarized Karahan's statement.

Context

Background: Turkey's reserves have eroded in waves since the U.S.-Israel-Iran conflict began in February 2026; during this period the CBRT attempted to defend the lira through forex sales and gold operations.

  • March 2026: Bloomberg reported that Turkey's forex reserves experienced their largest monthly decline to that date, driven by emerging-market sales triggered by war and pressure on the lira.
  • April 2026: Fitch Ratings downgraded Turkey's outlook from positive to stable, citing the significant reserve decline since the war began and estimating forex-market interventions had exceeded $50 billion.
  • November 1, 2026: Foreign Minister Fidan warned that the prolonged closure of the Strait of Hormuz was placing a heavy burden on the global economy (Fidan: Hormuz closure burdens global economy).
  • November 13, 2026: CBRT Governor Karahan announced gross reserves had fallen to $159 billion (the figure carried by Hamshahri).

Who is who / concepts: Fatih Karahan, serving as CBRT Governor since June 2023, is an academic who previously worked as an economist at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York. "Gross reserves" refers to the total forex and gold held by the central bank; "net reserves" means the amount remaining after subtracting short-term liabilities (including swaps)—the two can decline at different rates in different periods. English financial media sometimes cite "net reserves" and "net reserves excluding swaps" separately; accordingly, different reports' totals should not be conflated.

By the numbers: Fitch Ratings estimated the CBRT's forex-market intervention since war began exceeded $50 billion (Intellinews, April 2026). Bloomberg reported March saw Turkey's fastest monthly reserve decline to that date (Bloomberg, May 13, 2026). According to Karahan, as of November 13, gross reserves stood at $159 billion, with approximately $32 billion of the loss attributed to gold value depreciation (Hamshahri).

Turkey dimension: This report directly concerns Turkey's economy. By Fitch's assessment, reserve erosion weakens Turkey's buffer against external shocks and negatively affects its credit outlook. According to Bloomberg, the central bank resorted to forex sales and gold operations to defend the lira; this preserves short-term exchange stability while eroding the reserve buffer—a costly trade-off. Per Intellinews reporting, Turkey had managed the war's economic shocks "so far" without it becoming a full crisis; however, this assessment predated November's new reserve losses.

What's next: The CBRT's upcoming weekly data bulletins will show whether reserves followed the same trajectory through November. Karahan's statement included no new timeline or relief package; the CBRT's monetary policy committee's next meeting date and any potential rate decision were also absent from this announcement and from Hamshahri's coverage.
