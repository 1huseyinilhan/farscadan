Baghdad denies claims of troop movements toward Kurdistan Region

Iraq's government denied reports of troop movements toward the Kurdistan Region after the Federal Court ruling on border authority; tensions remain high.

By Farsçadan Editors

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

Iraq's government spokesman Haydar al-Abudi denied reports of military unit movements toward the Kurdistan Region on Wednesday evening. According to IRNA, al-Abudi said that Prime Minister Ali Falih al-Zaydi is focused on supporting the region's population and preventing the misappropriation of the region's resources. The denial came amid escalating Baghdad-Erbil tensions following the federal government's centralization decision on oil fields and border crossings.

According to ISNA and Khabaronline, al-Abudi characterized reports of military movements toward the Kurdistan Region as "false" and reiterated that Zaydi is focused on supporting the Kurdish people and preventing the region's wealth from flowing into private pockets. According to Iran International, citing the Iraqi official news agency (VAA), al-Abudi made this statement on Iraqi state television Al Iraqia Al Ahbariyya and emphasized that Kurdish rights are guaranteed in the Iraqi Constitution, noting that KRG salaries should not be affected by existing disputes. Al-Abudi also said the government would continue efforts to pursue oil smugglers, dismantle their networks, and identify smuggling cases across different provinces; according to him, "tens of billions" of smuggling proceeds flow into private accounts and special accounts without any benefit to the Kurdistan Region's population. According to Hamshahri, citing Al Jazeera, al-Abudi similarly said, "We will not remain indifferent to oil smuggling; we will pursue those involved in this across different provinces."

According to Iran International's report citing Reuters, this denial followed a decision by the Iraqi Federal Supreme Court on November 25; the court confirmed the central government's authority to deploy military force on all Iraq's international borders, including the Kurdistan Region, to control border institutions, and affirmed the Oil Ministry's authority to oversee and account for oil and gas fields. However, the court also emphasized in the same ruling that the rights of regional governments and relevant provinces to participate in customs affairs and management of produced oil and gas must be preserved. The details of this court decision were not independently verified by Farsçadan in this session using English sources; the information is being reported as attributed to Iran International.

According to the same source, the KRG characterized statements in an Iraqi army spokesman's remarks as "military rhetoric and threats" and rejected them; however, it stated its readiness to cooperate with federal authorities in combating corruption and called for ending oil smuggling across Iraq. The KRG Cabinet on November 25 characterized Baghdad's centralization decisions regarding oil fields and border crossings as a step violating the region's authorities and called for implementation of Article 140 of the Iraqi Constitution, which regulates the status of disputed areas. According to Iran International, citing Reuters, a senior Iraqi security source said Baghdad does not prefer direct military confrontation with KRG forces but indicated the possibility of the federal government deploying additional forces in areas near oil fields in the coming days.

According to Reuters, Zaydi on November 24 instructed federal institutions to take control of border crossings under KRG administration and ordered the Oil Ministry to assume management of oil fields in Kirkuk, Nineveh, Diyala, and Salah ad-Din—located outside Kurdistan Region boundaries but under KRG security force control. According to an intelligence report cited by Reuters from Iraq's official news agency, in addition to Kirkuk oil operated by the North Oil Company, oil from the KRG in Sulaymaniyah has also been smuggled to Iran and from there to Afghanistan. Reuters added that smuggling accusations formed the basis of tensions that emerged last week between the Zaydi government and the Iran-aligned Shiite armed group Kataib Hezbollah: Zaydi ordered army units to prepare to enter Jurfa al-Sakher, a town south of Baghdad and one of Kataib Hezbollah's main bases. After initial objections from the group, an agreement was reached through the mediation of Shiite politicians, and the group accepted the army's entry into the town. According to the same report, the Baghdad-Erbil dispute over oil sales authority had previously halted approximately 450,000 barrels daily of oil exports through Turkey for more than two years, until the two sides agreed in September 2025 to resume exports.

Context

Background:

  • September 25, 2017: Following the KRG's independence referendum, Baghdad retook control of Kirkuk and disputed areas.
  • April 2023 – September 2025: Disputes over oil sales without Baghdad's approval halted oil flow through the Kirkuk-Ceyhan line to Turkey for more than two years.
  • November 24–25, 2026: The Zaydi government placed Kurdistan oil fields and border crossings under Baghdad's control; Erbil called the decision "unacceptable" (see Farsçadan's previous report).
  • November 26, 2026: Baghdad denied reports of military troop movements toward Kurdistan amid rising tensions following its decision.

Who and what: The Iraqi Federal Supreme Court (al-Mahkamah al-Ittihādiyyah al-Ulyā) is Iraq's highest judicial body for constitutional review and renders final decisions in center-region jurisdictional disputes. The Kurdistan Regional Government (KRG) is an autonomous regional administration recognized in the Iraqi Constitution; it has its own prime minister (Masrur Barzani) and president (Nechirvan Barzani).

Turkey dimension: The Kirkuk-Ceyhan Pipeline is the sole pipeline through which oil from Kirkuk and the KRG is exported via Turkey; the pipeline's transport capacity is approximately 1.2 million barrels daily (Reuters, as reported in The National, March 16, 2026). Baghdad's decision to centralize oil fields and border crossings and the military discussion at hand are part of regional power balance disputes that could affect pipeline operations through Turkey; however, today's denial carried no new data on pipeline flow.

By the numbers: Kirkuk-Ceyhan Pipeline capacity is approximately 1.2 million barrels daily (Reuters, as reported in The National, March 16, 2026). Zaydi's decision of November 24–25 covers oil fields in Diyala, Kirkuk, Salah ad-Din, and Nineveh (Iraq Prime Ministry statement, as reported by Rudaw, November 25, 2026).

What's next: U.S. special envoy Tom Barrack-backed Baghdad-Erbil talks continue on the KRG's share in Iraq's 2027 federal budget and oil revenue sharing; no firm meeting date has been announced (Shafaq News, November 9, 2026).
