Iran press: claim that gas exports to Turkey extended to 2032 could not be verified

Iran's press reported BOTAS extended gas imports to 2032; the claim could not be verified by foreign sources.

By Farsçadan Editors

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

According to Ettelaat, citing Turkey's state news agency Anadolu, Turkey's state company BOTAS extended its authorization to import natural gas from Iran through April 18, 2032. The report states that the new agreement maintains annual import volume (10 billion cubic meters) and pricing formula unchanged; the previous contract, in effect since 2001, had expired in July 2026.

According to Ettelaat, Turkish gas trade statistics show Iran exported approximately 7.7 billion cubic meters of natural gas to Turkey in 2025, equivalent to 13.2% of Turkey's total gas imports, making Iran the country's fourth-largest gas supplier. The report states that Turkey is pursuing both Iranian and Russian pipeline gas alongside liquefied natural gas (LNG) imports to ensure energy security.

However, at the time Farsçadan prepared this article, this claim could not be verified by an independent Turkish or international source; moreover, existing English-language sources present a different picture. According to a Reuters report, Turkey's Energy Minister Alparslan Bayraktar stated on April 18, 2026, that the gas contract with Iran would expire in coming months but that extension negotiations had not yet begun due to the regional war. Per Haberler.com's report, the 25-year contract in effect since 2001 expired on July 29, 2026, and negotiations could not proceed due to the war; industry sources suggested the flow could continue for some time under "force majeure" provisions. As a result, the apparent contradiction between claims that the contract was formally extended to 2032 and earlier reports that it expired due to war without renegotiation remains unverified.

Context

Background:

  • 2001: A 25-year natural gas purchase agreement was signed between Iran and Turkey.
  • November 2025: Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi announced Tehran was ready to extend the agreement (Eghtesad Online, November 2025).
  • April 18, 2026: Turkish Energy Minister Bayraktar stated that extension talks had not yet begun due to the regional war (Reuters/Gas Processing News).
  • July 29, 2026: Per Haberler.com, the 2001 contract expired; no new negotiations took place due to the war.
  • November 27, 2026 (today): Ettelaat, citing Anadolu, reported the contract was extended to 2032; this claim has not yet been independently verified.

Key terms: BOTAS (Boru Hattları ile Petrol Taşıma A.Ş.) is Turkey's state company managing natural gas imports and pipeline operations. The counterparty, NIGC (National Iranian Gas Company), is Iran's state gas exporter. The contract between the two companies governs the volume and pricing formula of gas transported through the Tabriz-Ankara pipeline.

By the numbers: According to Reuters data, Turkey imported approximately 7.6 billion cubic meters of gas from Iran last year, equivalent to roughly 13% of Turkey's total gas imports (Gas Processing News, April 2026 update). According to regulatory authority data, the pipeline last reached full contracted capacity in 2022; in subsequent years, flow has remained below capacity.

Turkey dimension: The news directly concerns Turkey: if the claim is true, Turkey will remain dependent on Iranian gas for six more years; if the claim confirms the contract has effectively expired, Turkey's shift toward LNG and alternative sources becomes a priority. Turkey had also extended its gas agreement with Azerbaijan through 2030, another aspect of Ankara's supply diversification.

What's ahead: At the time this article was prepared, neither BOTAS nor Turkey's Ministry of Energy and Natural Resources issued a statement confirming or denying the 2032 claim. It remains unclear whether an official Turkish source will verify the matter.
