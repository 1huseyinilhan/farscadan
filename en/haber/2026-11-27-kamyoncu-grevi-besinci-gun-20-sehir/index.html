Truckers' strike in Iran enters day 5: protests spread to 20 cities

In Iran, a truckers' strike protesting diesel shortages and rising tire and spare parts prices spread to 20 cities on its fifth day.

By Farsçadan Editors

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

According to Iran International, citing the Union of All Iranian Truckers and Drivers, a truckers' strike protesting diesel shortages, rising truck tire and spare parts prices, and low freight rates entered its fifth day on Thursday, November 27; the number of cities with strike activity reached 20 with the participation of Malayer in Hamadan Province and Khorramabad in Lorestan Province.

According to a list shared by the union's Telegram channel, Tehran, Isfahan, Ahvaz, Shiraz, Kermanshah, Bandar Abbas, Chabahar, Sirjan, Jiroft, Bam, Geysahn, Divandarreh, Zabol, Qazvin, Kazerun, and Shushtar were among 18 other cities on the strike list. In a separate message, the union called for solidarity among drivers, saying "if one of us hauls for free, we all lose; if we all stand together, we all win," and demanded that freight rates be increased proportionally to the dollar-denominated prices of tires, oil, and diesel. According to Iran International, truckers have ceased loading cargo in various cities since November 23.

Some drivers who contacted Iran International described being unable to continue operating due to diesel shortages, rising oil and tire prices, and low freight rates. Reza Ekberi, head of the Iran Road and Road Transportation Organization, denied that a strike was occurring on November 23, calling the matter "trivial"; by contrast, Jahan-e Sanat newspaper, citing transportation sector representatives, confirmed the strike was taking place. The union warned that diesel and spare parts shortages pose a "serious danger" and affect the entire transport chain: "A truck is not just a driver and cargo; to move, it needs diesel, solid tires, engine oil, and various parts; if any one is missing, the entire transport chain breaks down."

On November 23, the union stated it found insufficient a 22.6% increase in the ton-kilometer index used to calculate freight rates. One driver, in a message shared on the union's Telegram channel, wrote, "The dollar has passed 294,000 tomans, tire and oil and parts prices have skyrocketed, but our freight rates aren't moving." A trucker from Karaj described parts and oil prices as "unbearable," noting that 20 liters of engine oil had reached 20 million tomans.

Context

Background:

  • May–June 2025: Iranian truckers staged a nationwide strike lasting weeks across more than 130 cities in response to three-tier diesel pricing, rising insurance costs, and low wages (RFE/RL, June 2025).
  • November 23, 2026 (2 Azar): The union publicly declared the 22.6% increase in ton-kilometer index insufficient.
  • November 23–25, 2026: The action lasted three days across 15 cities (details); official agencies denied the strike.
  • November 26–27, 2026: On day five, the action spread to 20 cities with the participation of Malayer and Khorramabad.

Key terms: The Union of All Iranian Truckers and Drivers (Ettehad-ye Teşekkol-ha-ye Kamiyondaran va Ranandegan-e Saraser-e Iran) is an independent union organization announcing drivers' demands through Telegram. The "ton-kilometer index" (shakhes-e ton-kilometr) is an official measure set annually by the government forming the base freight rate; drivers argue the index does not reflect dollar-denominated increases in fuel and spare parts.

By the numbers: According to the Central Bank of Iran, year-over-year point-to-point inflation reached 88.7% in October 2026 (Mihr month) (Azernews, Central Bank source, November 2026). Per a trucker's message on the union channel, 20-liter engine oil reached 20 million tomans; the union reported on November 23 that one model truck tire reached 246 million tomans with an 18% weekly increase. These figures are based on drivers' own statements and could not be independently verified with a price index at the time this article was prepared.

Turkey dimension: Cross-border truck transit and exports from Iran to Turkey can experience delays during periods of widespread driver action; however, no cross-border impact specific to this strike was reported in sources reviewed at the time this article was prepared.

What's ahead: The union stated it will continue action until demands are met; no new government freight rate increase or negotiation timeline was announced at the time this article was written. Whether the action will extend to day six and whether official agencies will continue to deny the strike remains uncertain.
