Methanol poisoning spreads nationwide in Iran: 600 hospitalized, at least 26 dead

Methanol poisoning from counterfeit liquor spread across Iran, hospitalizing 600; at least 26 died and 9 experienced brain death.

By Farsçadan Editors

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

According to Ministry of Health Director of Public Relations Hossein Kirmanpur's post on X, methanol poisoning from counterfeit alcoholic beverages seen simultaneously across Iran brought approximately 600 people to treatment centers by the morning of November 29; at least 26 died and 9 experienced brain death. Iran International reported that the official ministry data from November 30 to November 27 (4 Azar evening, 8 PM) across 12 medical science universities' jurisdictions showed 543 poisoning cases, 23 deaths, and 8 cases of brain death; however, subsequent statements from local authorities in Nishapur and Hormozgan exceeded these figures.

According to ministry data, cases were most heavily concentrated in Nishapur Medical Sciences University's jurisdiction (214 cases), followed by Hormozgan (93), Shiraz (81), and Tehran's Iran Medical Sciences University jurisdiction (69); Hoy and Kurdistan each had 21, Alborz had 20, Mashhad 9, Isfahan 7, Zanjan and Hamadan each had 3, and Zabol had 2. Milad Takdisi Haidarian, Deputy Director of Treatment at Nishapur Medical School, announced the case count in his region had risen to 238 and deaths to 5; Farida Hasanzadeh, spokesperson for Hormozgan Medical Sciences University, reported 119 cases in the province and 10 deaths (9 in Bandar Abbas, 1 in Qeshm). Babak Heidari, emergency services chief at Kurdistan Medical Sciences University, reported 4 cases in Sanandaj, Saghez, and Sarabad; Hamid Reza Imrani, Dean of Hoy Medical School, announced 20 cases and 3 deaths in his region, with 7 patients receiving intensive care.

Horasan-e Rezavi Province's Chief Prosecutor announced that the main suspects from a counterfeit liquor production and distribution network operating in Tehran and Nishapur—with distribution in Tehran, Horasan-e Rezavi, Hormozgan, and Fars provinces—had been arrested. Seccad Rezaei, Deputy Minister of Treatment at the Health Ministry, described the situation as "high alert" level in a statement to the press, urging those poisoned to seek treatment without fear of prosecution, and stated that medical records would remain confidential. Addiction specialist Mohammad Reza Kadir-Zadeh warned of risk of the poisoning spreading to other provinces, stating that three main medications used in alcohol addiction treatment are unavailable in Iran.

Iran International, by carrying critical voices arguing that prohibition policy increases poisoning risk, presents a frame absent from state sources; this is not a factual discrepancy but a difference in editorial emphasis reflecting outlet orientation.

Context

Background:

  • 2018 (1397): Nurnews reported 768 people experienced methanol poisoning across 21 provinces, with 76 deaths.
  • September–October 2020 (COVID-19 period): Doctors affiliated with the Health Ministry stated that approximately 480–800 people died from methanol poisoning due to unfounded belief that alcohol prevents coronavirus (AP, 2020).
  • November 22, 2026: Homemade alcohol poisoning in Nishapur was reported with 33 cases and 3 deaths (details).
  • November 23, 2026: 30 new cases and 1 death were recorded in Shiraz and Marvdasht (details).
  • November 26–27, 2026: Case count rose nationwide to approximately 600 and deaths to at least 26; a distribution network was dismantled in Horasan-e Rezavi.

Key terms: Methanol is a much cheaper and more toxic form of alcohol than ethanol; counterfeit liquor producers use it to cut costs, and even small amounts can cause blindness and death. In Iran, production, sale, and consumption of alcoholic beverages are prohibited (Islamic Penal Code article 136 provides penalties up to execution on the fourth offense); this prohibition creates an unregulated underground market.

By the numbers: According to U.S.-based AP reporting on the September–October 2024 wave, at least 26 people died in a comparable poisoning incident at that time (ABC News/AP, October 2024); this shows that methanol counterfeits represent a recurring public health crisis in Iran over years. According to data released by Iran's Legal Medicine Organization in October 2025, 645 people died in 2024 (1403) from alcohol poisoning or consumption of alcohol combined with other substances nationwide. Current case and death figures for the November 2026 wave covered in this article have not yet been independently verified by an international news agency.

Turkey dimension: No sources reviewed reported a direct connection between this poisoning wave and Turkey (cross-border counterfeit alcohol smuggling, Turkish national casualties) at the time this article was prepared.

What's ahead: The Chief Prosecutor's investigation in Horasan-e Rezavi continues and additional arrests of network members are ongoing. The Health Ministry continues monitoring whether cases will spread to other provinces; no new official announcement timeline was released at the time this article was prepared.
