Second mine incident in Hormuz in four days: IRGC Navy warns 'air support insufficient'

The IRGC Navy announced that two tankers caught fire near the Strait of Hormuz east of Khasab, with one hitting a naval mine.

By Farsçadan Editors

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

The IRGC Navy's press office announced that two tankers caught fire east of the Strait of Hormuz, near Khasab (Bandar Khassab), with one striking a naval mine. According to Hamshahri, this was the second such incident in four days, following the IRGC's announcement four days earlier of three tankers hitting mines in the same area.

According to Hamshahri's account, the IRGC Navy's press office stated that one tanker struck a naval mine off Khasab hours earlier, while the second ignited and caught fire in an "unauthorized route" east of the Strait of Hormuz. The statement indicated that these tankers, like those that exploded previously, had disabled their position-tracking systems and were attempting to covertly transit the strait using false routing. According to Hamshahri's report, the IRGC reiterated that both tankers caught fire near the southern coast and near Khasab, cautioned that the designated route was equipped with naval mines, and warned that U.S. military air support would not prevent tankers from hitting mines.

IRNA and ISNA, citing the same statement, reported the incident largely with the same details and without additional commentary. Donya-ye Eghtesad led its report with the phrase "violent explosion" and framed the incident around "two rule-violating tankers."

The IRGC Navy's statement alleged that the tankers had disabled their automatic identification systems (AIS) while transiting the strait and were following an "unauthorized" course outside official shipping lanes. According to the statement, this pattern had been observed in previously damaged tankers as well; the navy therefore describes the route as "unauthorized" and reiterates that it is equipped with naval mines. According to Hamshahri's report, the IRGC specifically emphasized that U.S. military air support in the region could not protect tankers from hitting mines—an assertion that echoed a warning in the navy's initial statement from November 24.

According to Khabar Online's report, Supreme Leader adviser Mohammad Mohber commented the same day on the Strait of Hormuz and blockade allegations, saying "neither will the Strait of Hormuz open nor will the blockade continue." Khabar Online did not specify the context in which Mohber made this assessment or whether it was before or after the latest tanker incident; the statement was shared in short video format and contained no detail.

Today's statements provided no information about the tankers' flag, ownership, crew status, or cargo type. The IRGC's statement attributed responsibility directly to the tankers' use of "unauthorized routes" and did not assign blame to any other party. None of the five sources (IRNA, ISNA, Hamshahri, Donya-ye Eghtesad, Khabar Online) reported casualties or injuries; whether the tankers sank or whether rescue operations were conducted was also absent from today's reports.

None of the sources used language directly linking today's incident to the three-tanker case of November 24; while the IRGC's statements on both dates point to the same area (east of the Strait of Hormuz, near Khasab) and the same allegations (disabled tracking systems, unauthorized routing, naval mines), no official "follow-up" or "ongoing campaign" terminology appeared between the two statements. This connection is Farsçadan's editorial assessment.

Context

Background:

  • November 24, 2026: The IRGC Navy announced that three tankers hit naval mines and caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz, noting that U.S. air support failed to prevent the incident (see Three tankers hit mines and caught fire in Hormuz).
  • November 26, 2026: A tanker near Dubai experienced a nearby explosion; UKMTO reported an unidentified object (see Explosion near tanker off Dubai).
  • November 27, 2026: Farsçadan published a guide on the Strait of Hormuz and energy security (see Strait of Hormuz and energy security).
  • November 28, 2026 (today): The IRGC announced a second mine/explosion incident in the same area.

Key players / concepts: Khasab (Bandar Khassab) is a port town located at the eastern entrance of the Strait of Hormuz, in Oman's Musandam Peninsula; geographically outside Iran's borders, it marks a focal point in heavily transited international maritime traffic. In the IRGC's statements, "unauthorized route" refers to navigation outside the strait's official shipping lanes, undertaken with position signals disabled.

By the numbers: This is the IRGC Navy's second mine/explosion announcement in four days; the November 24 announcement mentioned three tankers, while today's statement involved two tankers. Both announcements contain no verifiable figures beyond the tanker counts.

Turkey dimension: The Strait of Hormuz is one of the critical chokepoints in global energy shipping, which Turkey also relies on for LNG and crude oil imports. The increase in security incidents in the strait keeps questions about the reliability of regional energy transit routes on the agenda. Within the framework of the Mecca Accord, to which Turkey is a party, calls for de-escalation in the region are being raised in a separate report today (see Mecca Alliance's committee reconvened).

What to watch: The IRGC's statement today provided no new timeline or additional warning; no official map or coordinates indicating which sections of the strait are mined were released. It was unclear from today's reports whether UKMTO issued a separate advisory on this latest incident.
