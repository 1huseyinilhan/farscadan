Central Bank froze accounts of 3 crypto exchanges: credibility test underway

Iran's Central Bank froze bank accounts of Nikpardakht, Bit Meet, and RavenX exchanges, citing they 'violated economic order.'

By Farsçadan Editors

ShareWhatsAppXTelegram

Iran's Central Bank issued an instruction to the banking network to freeze the bank accounts of three cryptocurrency exchanges, citing that they "violated the country's economic order." According to Kayhan's report, this instruction was based on the Central Bank's official information portal.

According to Kayhan's account, the Central Bank, with approval from the prosecutor's office and within the framework of Article 42, section "b" of the 1402 (2023) Central Bank Law, sent an instruction to the banking system on Thursday (5 Azar / November 26) to freeze accounts of crypto exchanges that violated regulations. Within the scope of this instruction, according to the report, three crypto exchanges named "Nikpardakht, Bit Meet, and RavenX" were suspended for violating economic order. Under the decision, no deposits can be made to these three companies' deposit accounts without further notice.

According to Kayhan's report citing the Ministry of Economy, Hadi Mahzarniya, head of the Ministry's National Center for Monitoring and Improving the Business Environment, stated in a comment that no cryptocurrency exchange has yet received operating permission, but that according to law, lack of permission does not automatically prevent operation. According to Mahzarniya, the process of regulating exchanges has seen "significant progress" and the government will soon announce which institution will authorize these exchanges.

According to Kayhan's report, Mahzarniya pointed to the high-volume financial transactions of crypto exchanges, the complexity of their operations, and numerous lawsuits filed in courts, arguing that responsibility for authorizing these exchanges should be transferred to relevant regulatory institutions such as the Central Bank, Securities and Exchange Organization, and the Finance Center of the Ministry of Economy. Mahzarniya stated that the choice of ultimate authority rests with the government and that his center opposes transferring this authority to guilds. According to Kayhan's report, Mahzarniya also disclosed that authority over gold platform licensing was transferred from the Central Bank to the association for internet cooperation.

ISNA, in a separate report issued the same day, addressed the topic from a different angle: rather than focusing on a concrete closure decision, ISNA emphasized that as the number of cryptocurrency users increases, the question of "how safe users' assets and information are on these platforms" has become more pressing. ISNA's report indicated that the answer to this question concerns not only user security but also the credibility of regulatory oversight applied to the sector; however, ISNA did not directly reference the Central Bank's concrete decision issued that same day.

Kayhan's report does not clarify how the freeze order affects existing customer balances on the three exchanges—specifically, whether users can withdraw their money. The order only halts deposits to the accounts "pending further notice"; no restriction on withdrawal of existing balances was specified.

Context

Background:

  • 1402 (2023): Article 42, section "b" of the Central Bank Law took effect; this provision became the legal basis for today's freeze order.
  • Approximately two weeks ago: According to Mahzarniya, the crypto exchange regulation topic was discussed in detail in the most recent meeting of the Ministry of Economy's Deregulation Board (Kayhan/Mehr, November 28, 2026).
  • November 26, 2026 (Thursday, 5 Azar): With prosecutor approval, the Central Bank sent the account freeze instruction to the banking network for crypto exchanges that violated rules (Kayhan, November 28, 2026).

Key players / concepts: Sarrafi-ye ramzarzi (crypto exchange) is the Persian press term for platforms that enable trading of crypto assets for Iranian rials or other currencies. Nikpardakht, Bit Meet, and RavenX—the subject of today's report—are private exchanges that have not obtained official operating permits, though under law this lack does not automatically render their operations unlawful.

By the numbers: The Central Bank's order targeted three exchanges directly (Nikpardakht, Bit Meet, RavenX) (Kayhan, November 28, 2026). According to Mahzarniya, to date no cryptocurrency exchange has obtained official operating permission (Kayhan/Mehr, November 28, 2026). The order is grounded in Article 42, section "b" of the 1402 (2023) Central Bank Law; this article grants the authority to freeze accounts with prosecutor approval (Kayhan, November 28, 2026).

What to watch: According to Mahzarniya, the government is expected to announce which institution will authorize crypto exchanges; when that decision is made, authorization application forms will be uploaded to the national licensing portal (Kayhan/Mehr, November 28, 2026). Mahzarniya stated that authority over gold platform licensing was also transferred from the Central Bank to the association for internet cooperation; it was not specified whether this regulation would apply to crypto exchanges (Kayhan/Mehr, November 28, 2026). Whether the three frozen exchanges will exercise their right to appeal or how the legal process will proceed was not addressed in today's reports.
